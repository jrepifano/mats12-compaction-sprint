# External lit review: binding inside/outside the J-space (2026-10-12)
Read depth: ALL sources below were seen only via WebSearch/WebFetch summaries or abstracts, none verbatim. Search was shallow (~10 queries). Not found at all: WorkspaceBench (Blank et al.), "The First Token Is a Clue", Prakash lookback paper (no primary hit).

## Binding in LMs
- Feng & Steinhardt, "How do LMs bind entities in context?" ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.17191. Binding IDs: vectors attached to entity and attribute activations, forming a continuous subspace; causal interventions; "direct binding" in some QA tasks. (abstract/search summary)
- Feng, Russell, Steinhardt, "Monitoring Latent World States in LMs with Propositional Probes", arXiv 2406.19501 (ICLR 2025). Hessian-found binding subspace causally mediates binding; probes decode LivesIn(Alice,Laos) and generalize to stories/Spanish; closed-world toy setting. (summary)
- Dai, Heinzerling, Inui, "Representational Analysis of Binding in LMs", EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2409.05448. Ordering-ID subspace; patching along it flips which entity is bound. (summary)
- Dai, Heinzerling, Inui, "Cell-Based Representation of Relational Binding in LMs", arXiv 2604.19052, 21 Apr 2026. Entity-relation indices linearly decodable (PLS) in a low-dim grid-like subspace; patching supports causality. Closest to relations, but no lens/workspace decomposition. (abstract read)
- "Mixing Mechanisms: How LMs Retrieve Bound Entities In-Context", arXiv 2510.06182. Retrieval = mixture of positional, lexical, reflexive mechanisms; interchange interventions. (summary)
- Prakash et al., lookback mechanism (belief tracking, 2025): only secondary explainers seen; unverified.
- Wang et al., "Is the Reversal Curse a Binding Problem?", arXiv 2504.01928 (v2 Feb 2026, ICLR 2026). Role-dependent entity representation inconsistency/entanglement. (summary)
- Entity-rebinding circuit in a few attention heads (Gemma/Llama): blog only (groundy.com), primary not confirmed.

## Thematic roles
- Denning, Guo, Snefjella, Blank, "Do LLMs know who did what to whom?", arXiv 2504.16884 (2025). Whole-sentence representations track syntax, not agent/patient reversal; role info weak in hidden units but robust in some attention heads. Correlational; I found no causal (patching/ablation) role study. Note Blank is also WorkspaceBench first author. (search summary)

## Gap assessment
I found no work that decomposes activations into J-space (lens-aligned) vs non-J residual and asks where role/binding information lives. Binding literature uses its own subspaces (BI/OI/cell) and never the J-lens; workspace follow-ups (found ones) cover steering, verbalization, lens quality, not binding. Confidence the gap is open: ~75%. Caveats: shallow search; WorkspaceBench and Gong & Wang were not retrievable, and WorkspaceBench's role-bound family may already include a partial J vs Oracle comparison; LW comments on the non-J residual suggest someone may be running adjacent analyses; X/Twitter and Neuronpedia not checked. Recommend a second pass on arXiv listing for Aug-Oct 2026 "workspace" + "binding" before committing.

## Second pass
Read verbatim = full text fetched via curl and grepped/read; summary = search/fetch summary.

1. Denning, Guo, Snefjella & Blank, arXiv 2504.16884 (PDF text read, hidden-unit/attention sections verbatim).
- Models: BERT, GPT2-small, Llama-2-7B, Persimmon (36 layers). Old models; no post-2023 models.
- Measure: one sentence vector per layer (BERT [CLS]; others the final "." token). Cosine similarity over pairs varying in syntax vs thematic role (reversed roles); human similarity ratings as comparison. Main similarity results are last layer ("hold across layers").
- Hidden units: SVM on same-vs-opposite-role pairs per layer, mostly <0.6 accuracy; best GPT2 layer 5 (CI [0.594, 0.667]) with fold accuracies from 0.995 to 0.32 (overfitting); human implicit accuracy 0.703. Claim is "little evidence" of role info in any subset of hidden units, from pair-classification of whole-sentence vectors, not token-level role probes.
- Attention heads: BERT only (needs bidirectional attention method); best head L11 H5 accuracy 0.79; many heads at or above human level. 144 heads tested, Bonferroni.
- No causal tests (no patching/ablation). Caution for our design: a negative on pooled last-token vectors does not mean roles are absent at the argument-token positions.

2. Dai, Heinzerling & Inui, arXiv 2604.19052 (HTML text read verbatim).
- Models: Llama3-8B-Instruct main, Qwen3-8B replication. Synthetic multi-sentence discourse (entity x relation index, e.g. person-hometown-residence), domains country/job/city.
- Where: attribute-token activations; layers 10-20 strongest, layer 15 used; PLS with 2-5 components is near-perfect fit (so ~2-5 dims of d=4096). Indices are order-based (delexicalized), not role semantics.
- Patching: moving attribute activations across the 2D PLS plane switches the predicted attribute in grid-like "cells" (logit landscape, 50 samples x 10^4 points); control with random-subspace perturbation degrades less; one-shot query format. Appendix A.28 compares with Hessian-based binding, A.30 detects "CBR-related heads". Not about agent/patient, but supports that binding lives in a low-dim, layer-15, token-position-specific subspace: a natural candidate to compare with J-space overlap.

3. J++ Lens post comments (LessWrong GraphQL, read verbatim). Only 1 comment returned: niranjandeshpande, 2026-10-08, asks about the Jacobian-filtering dev-set weights. Nothing on non-J. The non-J comment is actually on the Gurnee linkpost (3PaLrzxagpbnNtPLT): my earlier attribution to the J++ post was wrong.
- Pranav Viswanath, 2026-07-10: "I read the non-J space directly by using Anthropic's NLAs on Llama-70B, and found that it's causal even when the model flatly denies it. I split concept vectors into their J-space/non-J parts, and ran Lindsey's 'Introspection Awareness' experiment on both halves. The model denies any non-J injection, but the NLA reads it! ... the non-J side ... drives the injected token's logprob up +21 nats while the model names it 0% of the time and the NLA ~100%." Also "Franken-vectors": model names only the J-side concept, NLA reads both. Caveat he states: synthetic injections, not natural scenarios. Full post (summary only): LW LhDJdccLszLEAqgZ9 "Models are blind outside the J-space, NLAs aren't" (Llama-3.3-70B; code github.com/PranavViswanath/nla-introspection; model names J-part ~80%, non-J never). This is the closest existing J vs non-J decomposition + NLA readout, but for concept injections, not roles/binding. Other comments: "phoenix" (2026-07-07), claims J-space inflated by unigram/final-layer-bias frequency (GPT-2-small only, own tool).
