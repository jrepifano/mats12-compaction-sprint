## Proposal A: early-window E3

**Checkpoint facts.** Moskvoretskii et al. sample 17 OLMo-3-7B checkpoints at token counts 4B, 13B, 42B, 126B, 415B, 1.2T, 6.1T (pretraining) then midtraining/post-training (Fig.1, lines 183-213 of extraction). Persona vectors (evil/sycophantic/impolite/humorous) are "lower bounds": at 4B tokens extraction "fails the coherence test or yields no extractable persona" (line 280-282); 13B tokens (~0.22% of pretraining) is the earliest where extraction succeeds. Step-to-token rate from em-olmo is ~4.2M tok/step, so 13B tokens ≈ step ~3,100 and OLMo-3-7B's "1,487 saved checkpoints" at ~950-step spacing means Proposal A's "steps 1k-10k" window spans roughly 4B-42B tokens — i.e. it overlaps Moskvoretskii's own "too-early-to-extract" point (4B) and its first extractable point (13B). *[Spacing corrected above.]*

**Orgad et al. do NOT sweep early pretraining.** Their OLMo-3-7B checkpoint sequence is base→midtrain→long-context→SFT→DPO→RL (post-training stages only) — no steps-1k-10k data.

**em-olmo's Phase T has already run almost exactly this question, with a different method.** Using layer-12 steering vectors (not LoRA task fine-tuning) on stage1-step{10000, 30000, 100000, 350000, 700000} vs final (1,413,814 ≈ 5.9T tokens), they found: coherence after steering rises from 27% (10k) → 41% (30k) → 54% (100k) → 58% (350k) → 69% (700k) → 90% (final); **no checkpoint reached their 75%-coherence usability bar**, so the pre-registered stop fired and no "less-susceptible-but-learns-the-task" checkpoint was found. Exploratory: rate-among-coherent is roughly flat (23.6/25.0/30.6/25.4/36.4% vs 31.4% final) across all five early checkpoints — "no evidence of a less-susceptible early checkpoint once incoherence is set aside" (TODO lines 243-308). Their own earliest tested point (step 10,000 ≈ 42B tokens) already shows only 15% coherent fraction untrained.

**Gap that remains / risk.** (1) Proposal A's window (step ~1k-10k) is at or below em-olmo's worst-coherence point and below Moskvoretskii's own extraction floor — high risk that LoRA-fine-tuned outputs are too incoherent to judge at all, not merely "narrow-but-not-broad." (2) Untested by em-olmo: LoRA task-learning (vs. steering) at these early checkpoints, and persona-vector *quality* (geometric) as a predictor rather than just EM rate — Moskvoretskii's Fig.4-5 geometry data (cosine-to-final rising from ≈0.3) is a candidate proxy A could correlate against, which nobody has done. (3) em-olmo's Phase A1 found the *learned EM direction is nearly orthogonal to Moskvoretskii's elicited persona vectors* (|cos|≤0.07 at the final checkpoint; TODO lines 607-626) — a serious prior against "EM tracks persona-vector formation" as a mechanism, even though both "exist early."

## Proposal B: rewrite pretraining data behind EM

**Tice et al. Appendix I (full read).** Pretrains four 6.9B models (Unfiltered/Filtered/Misalignment-Upsampled/Alignment-Upsampled; NOT OLMo), then runs Turner et al.'s rank-32 rsLoRA EM recipe on bad-medical/risky-financial/extreme-sports data. Result: **all four pretraining conditions show EM on every dataset regardless of upsampling/filtering** (Fig.27; bad-medical EM rates cluster ~7-12% across the four conditions, exact per-bar mapping uncertain from text extraction of the bar chart — flag as approximate). Their own conclusion: "alignment pretraining does not mitigate EM... the mechanisms underlying EM are largely robust to the pretraining-stage interventions we investigate" (lines 2813-2816). This is a **negative prior specifically for upsample/filter-style interventions**, which Proposal B's "broad-vs-narrow-target" condition resembles unless it targets specific documents rather than reweighting a whole category.

**Vetter et al. §4 "closing the loop" (full read).** Causally confirms persona-SAE-features trace to Dolma-like "villain/domination/harmful-agency" web documents (§5.2), but **fine-tuning on the raw/reformatted retrieved human-written documents does NOT induce EM** (Table 5: MR stays ≤4.58% with IR often >30%) — only *synthetic instruction-response pairs generated from the same content* induce EM (Table 6, up to 10.42% MR). Their own note: "EM has so far been demonstrated exclusively using synthetic fine-tuning data... not through fine-tuning on human data" (lines ~515-520). **This directly threatens Proposal B's rewrite mechanism**: rewriting real pretraining documents into "aligned versions" and doing continued pretraining may do little, since the causal link runs through synthetic instruction-tuned fine-tuning data, not raw pretraining text — consistent with em-olmo's CPT nulls below.

**em-olmo's CPT phases are the closest concurrent work and are ALL null — but none of them rewrite anything, only select/withhold.** Phase 2b (10M tokens, TOP/RANDOM/BOTTOM by activation-projection score): primary null (TOP 26.0% vs RANDOM 25.1% after steering, p=0.81). Phase 2b-GA (gradient-alignment scoring): null, direction opposite prediction. Phase D (100M tokens, ~top 11% of pool): null (32.5% vs 27.1%, p=0.13). Phase C (re-measured on fresh samples, both doses pooled): null (CMH p=0.39); only secondary signal is that **100M tokens of ANY CPT (TOP or RANDOM) raises MR vs no-CPT** (p=0.002-0.004) — a dose/distribution-shift artifact, not document-selectivity. Phase P (prevalence/reliance of G vs N directions in the corpus): G>N on variance share but the gap *shrinks* across checkpoints (350k→final: +0.22→+0.085), and mean-ablation of either direction changes loss by <1e-4 (floor) — "no meaningful evidence that pretraining data favours G over N." **This is the single most load-bearing concurrent result against Proposal B's premise** that identifiable pretraining documents causally drive EM susceptibility via dose/selection; it does not, however, test *rewriting* documents in place, which is Proposal B's actual intervention and is novel relative to em-olmo.

**Our own "Correct, Don't Delete" result (working repo, full numbers confirmed).** On the bad-medical-advice fine-tuning organism (Qwen2.5-14B, not OLMo), full-mixture headline numbers (56-question set, Luna judge; `docs/paper-plan.md` line 180, cross-checked against `logs/phase1-report.md` lines 8555/8775-8776): **untouched 28.5% EM, delete (100% of poisoned rows) 26.8% EM, rewrite (100% of poisoned rows) 19.4% EM** — rewriting beats deletion. **Important caveat**: this is fine-tuning-data rewriting, not pretraining-data rewriting, and a *separate, negative* result exists for *targeting* which rows to rewrite — an influence-score-selected 685-row rewrite tied a random 685-row rewrite (`docs/research-proposals.md` l.278, `logs/phase1-report.md` ~4823-4826), and a locator's held-out Spearman correlation was ≈0. **This is a direct analogue-risk for Proposal B's attribution step.**

**Minder/Dumas et al. 2025** (`papers/interp_methods/Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces...pdf`, §F.5/F.8, lines 2862-3036): mixing unrelated data 1:1 into *fine-tuning* data "mostly removes" the activation-difference domain bias but **does not eliminate EM** ("mixing does not eliminate misalignment... objective matters far more than mixing," lines 2904-2906, 3029). Evidence that diluting/rewriting a fraction of the signal is likely to show attenuation, not elimination.

**Tan et al. Inoculation Prompting** (INDEX only, row 24): conceptually the mirror image of Proposal B — explicit framing/context during training suppresses later generalization of the trait. Tice et al. cite it (§6.1/6.4) as the live alternative to "filter/upsample," suggesting Proposal B's strongest surviving variant may be closer to inoculation-style contextualization of the identified EM-tied documents than literal rewriting.

## Risks and gap statement
- **Proposal A**: the core question (is there a coherent-but-narrow early window?) has been largely answered null by em-olmo's Phase T using steering vectors; the gap is LoRA-based task learning plus persona-vector-quality correlation at checkpoints even earlier than em-olmo tested, where coherence risk is high.
- **Proposal B**: three independent negative/attenuating results bear directly on it — Tice (filter/upsample doesn't mitigate EM), Vetter (human pretraining text doesn't induce EM, only synthetic data does), em-olmo (CPT dose/selection on pretraining documents is null across six arms and two scoring methods), plus our own negative finding that attribution-based *targeting* of fine-tuning-data rewrites ties random selection. The positive prior is narrower than the proposal implies: our fine-tuning-level rewrite-vs-delete result (19.4% vs 26.8%) is real and strong, but nothing in papers/ shows it transfers to pretraining-data rewriting. Any prereg should state this chain of negative priors explicitly and consider whether a fine-tuning-level replication on OLMo is cheaper and better-grounded before committing compute to the pretraining version.
