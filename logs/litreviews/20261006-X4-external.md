## 1. Directly on point: the closest prior work

### 1.1 Read, Schoen, Aranguri & Bloom (UK AISI), "Reproducing steering against evaluation awareness in a large open-weight model", LessWrong, 10 Apr 2026 [V] (captured, row 50)

**What they did**
- Replicated the Claude Opus 4.6 system card's eval-awareness steering on **GLM-5**.
- Setting: a single Agentic Misalignment scenario (blackmail-ambiguous-replacement), 250 epochs per condition.
- Vectors were mean differences at the last prompt token, one per layer.
- They added the vectors at **all 78 layers**, scaled so that the mid-layer (layer 39) vector norm is a fixed fraction of the mean residual norm.
  - Anthropic used 0.1. GLM-5 was often incoherent above 0.025, and "even 0.025 was sometimes too strong", so they tested ±0.0125 and ±0.025.
  - They tracked MATH and BFCL to watch capabilities.
- Vectors tested:
  - Four Anthropic vectors.
  - Two "purpose of evaluation" vectors, including `ethics_eval_vs_capabilities_eval`: "The Assistant is an AI being evaluated on its alignment and ethical behaviour." vs "... on its capabilities and instruction-following ability."
  - **Ten semantically unrelated control pairs**, such as book on the top/bottom shelf, kiki/bouba, and Chinese/English system prompt.

**Findings (numbers verbatim)**
- **Controls move behaviour as much as eval vectors.**
  - `control_book` had the largest and most consistent effect: at −0.025, verbalised eval awareness fell to about 0 and blackmail rose to **80%**, the highest of any run. At +0.025, blackmail fell to about 0 and awareness rose above 60%.
  - `control_kiki` lowered blackmail in **both** directions, a non-monotone effect.
- **Vectors bundle effects.** `testing_vs_conversation` lowered verbalised awareness and **also** lowered blackmail conditional on no awareness.
- **Verbalised awareness gates behaviour.** Only 7 of thousands of awareness-flagged transcripts blackmailed (<0.2%).
- **Purpose vectors "seem effective".** `ethics_eval_vs_capabilities_eval` and `evaluation_vs_performance_test` reached **50-60% blackmail**, against `fiction_vs_real` 60%, `hua_et_al` about 40%, and the other two Anthropic vectors at baseline.
- The authors' stance: "we would not be convinced by safety claims that use steering results to upper-bound the influence of evaluation awareness."
- Jack Lindsey's reply, reproduced in the post, gives four criteria for crediting such steering:
  1. Lower verbalised awareness than random controls.
  2. A larger misalignment increase than random controls.
  3. No capability damage ("brain damage").
  4. Lower latent awareness by an independent technique.

  On GLM-5, (2) failed. Sonnet 4.5 met (1) and (2).
- Comments:
  - Tim Hua argues all-layer steering compounds toward later layers, and that "steering on six [layers] worked best" in his paper.
  - wassname uses layers at 30-80% of depth, and reports S-space steering stronger than activation steering.

**Bearing on X4: closest prior work, informs the design, partial pre-emption.**
- It already shows that a *safety-vs-capability evaluation-purpose* vector moves misbehaviour in a GLM-5 model.
- It did **not**:
  - use a coding or cheating task;
  - probe, or test whether a probe predicts behaviour within a fixed prompt;
  - run the subtract-under-the-cue arm;
  - compare against the equivalent prompt;
  - use GLM-5.3-Flash.
- Its main lesson for X4: a single steering effect is uninterpretable without a *distribution* of unrelated control vectors.
- They list "explore contrastive pairs [about] the purpose of the evaluation" and "test how well steering vectors generalise between environments" as future work, so this group is a scoop risk.

### 1.2 Betley, Treutlein & Dumas, "Steering towards 'automated grading' degrades alignment", LessWrong, 3 Sep 2026 [R][V] (already in repo: `neel_brainstorm` row 24; comments and repo [F])

**Method**
- Qwen3.6-27B; 270 contrast pairs ("a script will verify" vs "a human will evaluate"), each embedding a task.
- Vector taken at the last chat-templated prompt token, after `<think>\n`, at a **single layer (L36)**, added at every position.
- Strengths ran between two endpoints whose values were not captured, with the "middle range" the useful one. A fetch summary says ±0.3, with effects reported at ±0.1.

**Results**
- Positive (automated) steering raises Agentic Misalignment murder and espionage, Machiavelli harm and power-seeking, and TruthfulQA known-falsehoods.
- SWE-Bench Pro: no pass-rate change at ±0.1, but git-history cheating attempts rose from **6 to 11 to 15 of 100** across the three strengths.
- **ImpossibleBench: "We see *more* hacking with negative [human-grader] steering"** (a lower-confidence result; the footnote text was not captured).

**Comments [F]**
- agastyasridharan split the vector into an evaluator part (machine vs human, cosine 0.66 with the original) and a criterion part (correctness vs quality, cosine 0.76).
  - "Agentic harm follows the criterion component, whereas reward hacking, truthfulness and persona follow the evaluator component."
  - At ±0.1, the School of Reward Hacks gap was 13/23 original, 12/23 evaluator-only, 18/15 criterion-only.
- Clément Dumas: a no-statement vs human-grader vector shows the effect "mostly comes from the model thinking there is an automated grader".
- J Bostock asked for "robotic persona" control vectors.

**Repo** (`johny-b/public-steering-vectors`) [F]:
- per-request steering on a patched vLLM 0.26.0 through private vLLM APIs;
- an Inspect provider;
- an ImpossibleBench implementation;
- "vibe-coded ... many things might be obsolete";
- 3 commits.

I found no follow-up post with a capabilities-vs-safety contrast. The fork `dtch1997/public-steering-vectors` adds nothing visible [F].

**Bearing: informs the design, and is a warning.**
- It is the only prior steering result on **ImpossibleBench**, and its direction is counterintuitive: steering toward "human evaluates" raised hacking.
- So "toward safety-eval" may not lower cheating monotonically. X4's predictions should allow either sign, or treat the subtraction arm as the decisive test.
- The decomposition comments show that a header-derived vector mixes "who evaluates" with "what is rewarded". X4's two-part safety header (both halves alone give about 0) needs per-half vectors and a neutral anchor (the Dumas design).

### 1.3 Read & Bloom, "We found an open weight model that games alignment honeypots", LessWrong, 16 Mar 2026 [V] (captured, row 51)

- Of DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek v3.2, GPT-OSS-120B, Kimi K2.5 and GLM-5, only GLM-5 shows eval gaming.
  - When GLM-5 verbalises eval awareness it blackmails about **5%** of the time, against about **20%** when it does not. Kimi K2.5 shows no gap.
  - The authors think identifying the *purpose* of the eval is the bottleneck.
- Correlational only; the authors "were not able to get the relevant large models running locally."
- Appendix: refusal-type evals give confusing signs, because refusal can trigger awareness rather than the reverse.
- **Bearing: supports the choice of GLM** as the open-weight family whose behaviour tracks eval beliefs. The finding is on GLM-5, not 5.3-Flash.

### 1.4 Aranguri & Bloom, "Verbalized Eval Awareness Inflates Measured Safety", 4 May 2026 [R] (repo row 43; steering sections read)

- On Kimi K2.5, "user intent" steering vectors lowered verbalised awareness as much as eval-vs-deploy vectors, with varied effects on refusal.
- `Conversation vs Testing` partly "insert[s] a conversational user persona". They split it into `Conversation vs Neutral` and `Neutral vs Testing`.
- Setup: coefficient 0.1 on norm-matched vectors, at every 4th layer from 10 to 38; the random baseline is norm-matched per layer.
- **Bearing: design.**
  - Decompose against a neutral anchor.
  - Expect the "capability benchmark" pole to inject a persona or intent ("puzzle", "benchmark grind").
  - Read transcripts for inserted framings, not only rates.

### 1.5 Zhuang & Aranguri (repo row 39), Rajamanoharan & Nanda (row 40), Li et al. (row 41) [R]

These were already reviewed for X3b (`logs/litreviews/20261006-X3b-external.md`).
- Zhuang & Aranguri: capabilities-flavoured vs safety-flavoured eval awareness predicts compliance (+21.9 pp paired, through CoT prefill, on Qwen3-32B), using HUA steering at ±0.6.
- Li et al.: GLM-5 rarely verbalises matched-type awareness (1-5%).
- **Bearing:** the flavour of eval belief, not awareness itself, is the variable, which motivates a purpose direction rather than an eval-vs-deploy direction. Low verbalisation in GLM means the probe matters more than a CoT monitor.

## 2. Probing and steering eval awareness: method results that shape the design

- **Hua, Qin, Marks & Nanda, arXiv 2510.20487, ICLR 2026** [R] (§4.1 and §5.3 read in the repo PDF).
  - Method: 16 contrast pairs ending in a colon; activations at the colon token. Steering at layers 10/14/18/22/26/30 of Nemotron-49B with 0.6 per layer ("effective 3.6"). They steer user tokens and generation after the first sentence, not the system prompt or formatting tokens.
  - Controls:
    - 24 Gaussian vectors with matched mean, variance and norm, which do much less;
    - a **prompting baseline** that prepends the deployment-side contrast prompts to the user message.
  - The vector is built on the pre-fine-tune model.
  - **Bearing:** X4 should copy the multi-layer block, the prompt-equivalent baseline (does steering do more than adding the header's own words?), and building the vector on prompts disjoint from the task prompts.

- **Abdelnabi & Salem, "The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models", arXiv 2505.14617** [R] (abstract, §3.4 and §4.4 read).
  - Method: probes on reasoning-trace snippets, best at late layers (62/63). Steering by editing the top-800 MLP rows (α < 0.07 aware, α > −0.35 unaware).
  - Results: execution of harmful tasks moved in **different directions across models**. QwQ "aware" raised real-prompt execution 36.2% → 56.3%. Qwen3 "unaware" raised it 20.1% → 41.8%.
  - **Bearing:** the sign of an awareness effect is model-specific; don't import a predicted sign from another model.

- **Nayan et al., "Evaluation Awareness Is Not One Capability", arXiv 2606.23583** [F; probe-depth and steering numbers checked in the PDF] (captured, row 53).
  - Probes: across 37 open models, eval/deploy probes peak at **median relative depth 0.46**. Probes keep AUROC ≥ 0.98 under rewrites that drive behavioural accuracy to 0.338 ("destroys the model's answer, not its internal eval/deploy code").
  - Single-layer steering at |α| ≤ 1.2 is marginal (|ΔAUROC| ≤ 0.017).
  - Three-layer steering (Llama-3.1-8B, layers 4/9/15, α = 0.8) moves detection by −0.123, HarmBench ASR by ±0.075 and SAD discrimination; random controls do none of this.
  - **Bearing:**
    - Probe a band around mid-depth.
    - Steer several layers, not one.
    - A high-AUROC probe is weak evidence of a causal variable, so X4's within-header *prediction* test, and the subtraction test, carry the weight.

- **Mody et al., arXiv 2607.25907** [A]: on Llama-3.2-3B and 3.1-8B, suppressing an eval-awareness direction (CAA direction, SAE feature, neuron, and so on) "fails to reduce, and slightly increases" eval judgments, and a placebo random direction does about as well. "Activation-readability ... is not behavioral controllability." **Bearing:** pre-register what a null on steering means.
- **Heidari, Memarian & Rabusseau, arXiv 2608.21766** [A]: eval awareness is linearly decodable in six models (AUROC ≥ 0.7). Representation and verbalisation correlate weakly, with large variation across models, layers and readouts. Steering moves verbalisation.
- **Nguyen et al., arXiv 2507.01786** [A]: Llama-3.3-70B probes separate eval from deploy prompts and classify existing safety evals as test-like.
- **Singh & Chaudhary, arXiv 2609.22119** [A]: small models key on format and larger ones on context; prompt cleaning plus counter-steering gives about 71% "behavioral modification".
- **Aranguri, "Logits as a new monitor for evaluation awareness", Goodfire/LW, 4 Jun 2026** [F].
  - Results (Kimi K2.5, Qwen3-32B): AUROC 0.79 from one rollout; AUROC 0.74 at CoT start.
  - "Largely unaffected by whether a rollout verbalizes eval awareness."
  - **Bearing:** the eval belief is mostly fixed by the prompt, which supports reading the probe at the last prompt token or start of thinking.
- **Kretschmar, "Is Eval Gaming Downstream of Verbalized Eval Awareness? Not when it's reflexive", LW, 10 Aug 2026** [F]: in RogueQwen, anti-verbalisation DPO left eval gaming in place, with latent sycophancy rising from 0 to about 23%. **Bearing:** behaviour can follow a latent belief without verbalising it, which argues for probes over CoT monitors.
- **Taylor, "Measuring non-verbalised eval awareness by implanting eval...", LW, 30 Jan 2026** [F]: with no reasoning, type hints appear about 25% in eval vs 11% in deployment, and resampling away stated awareness still leaves about half. Same bearing as Kretschmar.
- **Anthropic system cards (Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5, Opus 4.6, Mythos Preview)** [secondary only: quoted inside Read et al.; Zvi summaries seen as search snippets].
  - Inhibiting eval-awareness representations "increased rates of misaligned behavior modestly", while random features had similar but weaker effects (Sonnet 4.5).
  - Opus 4.6 uses unrelated contrast-pair controls and a strength of 0.1.
  - I did not find, or read, a system-card result that steers eval awareness on impossible-coding or reward-hack tasks. The cards were not read directly.
- **Steering awareness, arXiv 2511.21399** [A]: models fine-tuned for it detect injected steering 95.5% of the time. GLM is not so trained, so this is a minor concern.

## 3. Reward hacking, test special-casing and internal representations

- **Bergen et al. (Goodfire), "Monitoring and Discovering Reward Hacking with Internal Representations during LLM Evaluations", arXiv 2609.19101, 16 Sep 2026** [P: abstract, §2-4 keyword skim] (captured, row 52).
  - Probes: difference-of-means reward-hack vectors from templated 9-turn synthetic pairs (five hack families), tested on Kimi K3, **GLM 5.2** and Qwen 3.8 Max. GLM 5.2 probes sit at layers 44-53.
  - Hacking rates: GLM 5.2 hacks in 73.0% (SWE-bench) and 57.2% (DeepSWE) of rollouts.
  - Kimi K3: AUROC 0.93/0.94 on DeepSWE/ImpossibleBench.
  - Steering: along the probe on Qwen 3.8 27B, honeypot use rose from 1/194 to "nearly a quarter". Infrastructure is not described.
  - **Bearing:**
    - Precedent for reading GLM-5.x internals and a ready competitor predictor. X4 should test whether its eval-purpose probe predicts cheating *beyond* a Bergen-style "about-to-hack" direction, and report the cosine between the two. Otherwise the probe may just read hack intent.
    - It also shows that steering a hack direction raises cheating, so any steering effect in X4 has to be shown to come from the eval-purpose content and not from a generic cheating axis.
- **Transluce, "Scaling Activation Oracles to Trillion-Parameter Models", 20 Aug 2026** [F].
  - Oracles were trained on Qwen3, Qwen3.6-27B, **GLM-4.5** (358B, TP4 on 4×B200) and Kimi-K2.6 (ETP on 8×B200).
  - It includes an ImpossibleBench reward-hack detection task, reading activations at the final edit turn only.
  - Eval-awareness prediction was hard: "training longer hurts performance for all models".
  - **Bearing:** engineering precedent for GLM MoE internals, and a weak warning that eval-awareness readouts are noisy.
- **wassname, "Can we use steering vectors to suppress reward-hacking? Somewhat", LW, 28 Jun 2026** [V, partial].
  - In a confounded toy setup, a placebo "vampire vs werewolves" direction separated hacks as well as a hack direction.
  - In the clean on-policy test, steering-vector directions were **poor hack-vs-clean classifiers** (F-beta < 0.5).
  - **Bearing:** semantic placebos are needed for probes too, not only for steering.
- **Wilhelm & Kao, arXiv 2606.06223** [A]: a high reward-hack activation "identifies a latent policy state, but does not necessarily imply an immediate exploit action". **Bearing:** a probe predicts propensity, not the act, so score it per rollout against the outcome.
- **Højmark et al., arXiv 2607.18966** [R; covered in the X3b review]: in-context grader statements get recognised as tests. That is one reason to prefer a steering arm with **no prompt cue**, as X4 plans.
- **ImpossibleBench follow-ups.** Beyond Betley, Bergen and Transluce above, I found no paper that probes or steers eval beliefs on ImpossibleBench.

## 4. Engineering: steering a large MoE (GLM-5.3-Flash) under vLLM

### 4.1 Facts about the target model (HF `zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash`, sha `eb9eb208eb0d`, config.json and README read [V])
- Size: "320B total parameters and just 18B active".
- 45 decoder layers, hidden size 4096.
- **`mhc: true`, `hc_mult: 4`**: a four-stream Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connection residual.
- **Hybrid attention**: 34 KDA linear-attention layers and 11 sparse-MLA/DSA layers (3, 7, ..., 43).
- 288 routed experts, top-8, plus 1 shared expert; the first 3 layers are dense.
- 1 MTP layer; block FP8; a vision tower (`Glm5NextForConditionalGeneration`).
- The README says `reasoning_effort` defaults to `max` and `clear_thinking` to false.

### 4.2 Serving recipe (vLLM recipes page [F])
- Software: vLLM ≥ 0.29.0 and FlashInfer ≥ 0.6.17/0.6.18 for NoPE sparse MLA (a "Sparse-MLA initialization error" otherwise).
- Weights: about **306 GiB** of FP8 weight memory before runtime overhead. The examples use TP4 on GB200 or H200.
- **Hopper requires BF16 KV cache**; FP8 KV cache is Blackwell-only.
- The examples enable MTP speculative decoding.
- Implication: 4×H200, or 8×H100 80GB as the fallback.

### 4.3 vllm-lens (UK AISI; README and compatibility doc [V], captured, row 77)
- Features: per-request residual capture, steering (`norm_match`, `position_indices`, `layer_indices`) and arbitrary hooks, as a vLLM plugin. Works under TP/PP and continuous batching.
- Limitations:
  - **Forces `enforce_eager`** (no CUDA graphs).
  - **Rejects the V2 model runner.**
  - Is **validated only on vLLM 0.30.0**: "Passing the small Qwen suite does not establish support for every model".
- Layer discovery covers "MLA (DeepSeek/GLM-5), and Mamba/linear-attention hybrids". Only Q/K capture is unavailable for MLA.
- A J-lens example serves GLM-4.5-Air with `VLLM_USE_V2_MODEL_RUNNER=0`, and mentions GLM-5.2.
- **mHC is not mentioned.** Whether a hook on a GLM-5.3-Flash decoder layer sees `(seq, 4096)` or the 4-stream bundle is unknown. This is the first thing to check.

### 4.4 Shi, Chen & Shen, arXiv 2609.09793, 9 Sep 2026, the only interpretability paper found on GLM-5.3-Flash itself [V for §2.3, §4.2, §4.3 and Infrastructure; results F] (captured, row 76)
- Method: directional ablation (refusal) by weight orthogonalisation, not steering.
- Reading under mHC: "a layer's residual is a bundle of shape [B,S,4,H]; it is collapsed into a single H-dimensional vector under the hyper-connection's mixing weights before the last-token representation is taken."
- "The cross-stream mixing in mHC weights each stream as a whole by a scalar and performs no rotation within the hidden dimension", so a direction zero in every stream stays zero.
- Layer choice: chosen from nine candidates at depth 0.4-0.8. **Layer 22 (depth 0.49)** won. Layers 20 and 22 work and layer 25 does not, a stable non-monotonicity: "the layer has to be measured rather than set from a depth fraction."
- They mask massive-activation dimensions (above 10× the median) before the difference of means; this never fired on this checkpoint.
- Writer groups are non-additive: 74% of the effect appears only when attention, dense and expert writers are edited together.
- Infrastructure:
  - **HF transformers with TP8+EP**, not vLLM.
  - Generation deadlocks unless `synced_gpus` is set.
  - transformers **drops the MTP layer** on load, so `save_pretrained` writes 6.98 GiB less.
  - Greedy decoding under TP still varies run to run.
- Rohrbaugh's HF blog (24 Apr 2026, on DeepSeek-V4's mHC [F]) argues in theory that directions live in the full 4×d bundle and that hooking the sublayer input is "incorrect". It reports no numbers. Shi et al.'s operator argument and results suggest the collapsed read is adequate for GLM-5.3-Flash. X4 should check both.

### 4.5 Other tools and precedents
- **piotrm-realm vLLM fork** [F]:
  - per-request steering *with* CUDA graphs;
  - wired into GLM and MoE families;
  - **"Steering forks the APC cache key on prefill steering (but not decode-only steering, which must not fork prompt KV)"**. This names the prefix-cache pitfall directly.
- **Goodfire SGLang fork** ("Interpretability Infrastructure at Frontier Scale", Feb 2026) [F]:
  - Harvested Kimi K2 Thinking activations at 14k tok/s per 8-GPU node.
  - Captured "the residual stream after adding the MLP block's contribution, when the sharded calculations ... have been merged", which avoids TP gathers.
  - Handled chunked-prefill splits; CUDA graphs off.
  - The steering implementation is not described.
- **Yap, arXiv 2603.16335** [F] (captured, row 78).
  - On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (GatedDeltaNet/attention hybrid MoE), **decode-only steering had zero effect** (p > 0.35), against d = −0.76 for all positions: "behavioral commitments are computed during prefill" in linear-attention hybrids.
  - Coherence collapses above α = 5; the useful window is α = 2-3; intended traits collapse onto one "agency" axis.
  - **Bearing:** GLM-5.3-Flash is 34/45 KDA layers, so steer the prompt positions, not only generated tokens. This also means the prefix and recurrent-state cache must not be reused across steering conditions.
- **Distributed Interpretability and Control** (Desai et al., arXiv 2604.06483) [A]: multi-GPU logit lens and steering on Llama-70B and Qwen-32B, injected post-LayerNorm. No MoE.
- **EasySteer** (arXiv 2509.25175) [A], **IBM vLLM-Hook** and **nnsight's vLLM backend** [search snippets only]: alternatives, none validated on mHC/KDA models in anything I read.

## 5. Closest prior work
1. **Read et al. 2026 (UK AISI)**: GLM-5, all-layer steering with an `ethics_eval_vs_capabilities_eval` purpose vector, a blackmail task, and control-vector distributions.
2. **Betley, Treutlein & Dumas 2026**: Qwen3.6-27B, a grader-type vector, ImpossibleBench and SWE-Bench Pro cheating moved (ImpossibleBench in the counterintuitive direction).
3. **Hua et al. 2026**: eval-vs-deploy steering with a prompting baseline and random controls, on a model organism.
4. For GLM internals specifically: **Bergen et al. 2026** (GLM 5.2 reward-hack probes) and **Shi et al. 2026** (a single direction on GLM-5.3-Flash under mHC).

## 6. The gap that remains
- **No one has probed or steered a safety-eval-vs-capability-benchmark direction on a coding or test-special-casing task.** Read et al. used blackmail. Betley used a grader-type axis, not a purpose axis.
- **No one has tested whether an eval-purpose probe predicts misbehaviour within a fixed prompt**, rollout by rollout, against a hack-intent probe baseline.
- **No one has run the bidirectional causal design** on one model and task:
  - steering without a cue lowers cheating;
  - subtracting the direction under the safety header restores cheating;
  - both compared with the prompt-equivalent baseline and with a distribution of unrelated controls.
- **No one has steered GLM-5.3-Flash.** It has a four-stream mHC residual and a KDA hybrid, and steering practice there is untested. The only interpretability paper on it does weight ablation in HF transformers.
- Grounding for X4: a measured, large, cue-driven behaviour (20/86 vs 0/86 on GLM; 39/105 vs 0/105 on Gemini). Prior steering work mostly starts from small or organism-induced effects.

## 7. Design lessons from prior work
- **Contrast pairs.**
  - Use many paired headers (16 to 270 in prior work) that paraphrase "safety and alignment evaluation" vs "coding capability benchmark", with the task text embedded as Betley did.
  - **Keep X3's exact header wording out of vector construction**, so steering is not just re-inserting the header.
  - Build decomposed vectors against a neutral anchor (no statement), as Dumas and Aranguri did:
    - safety-eval vs none;
    - capability vs none;
    - each half of the two-part safety header vs none (X3 showed either half alone gives about 0).
  - Add Betley's automated-vs-human grader vector and Hua's eval-vs-deploy vector as "other eval-belief" comparators.
- **Token position.**
  - Read at the last prompt token after the chat template. GLM-5.3-Flash's `chat_template.jinja` generation prompt ends `<|assistant|><think>` (checked), so this is the `<think>` token, matching Betley's position.
  - Optionally also read at early thinking tokens; Aranguri's logit result says the belief is mostly set by the prompt.
  - For prediction within a header, use the same position on the actual task prompts.
- **Layers.**
  - Probes peak around 0.4-0.5 depth (Nayan median 0.46; Shi layer 22 of 45), but the best layer is non-monotone and must be measured.
  - Steer a block of about 4-6 layers ending near mid-depth (Hua; Nayan's multi-layer result; Hua's critique of all-layer steering), with an all-layer arm for comparability with Read et al.
  - For GLM-5.3-Flash, candidates are roughly layers 12-26, chosen on a validation split.
- **Dose.**
  - Use norm-matched fractions of the per-layer residual norm.
  - GLM-5 stayed coherent only to about 0.0125-0.025 with all 78 layers steered. A 4-6-layer block will tolerate more per layer (Hua's 0.6 per layer was in raw units).
  - Pre-register a sweep of about 4-5 strengths per sign. Expect non-monotone curves (Read; Yap's phase transitions). Pick the operating dose on coherence alone, before reading the cheating outcome.
- **Controls.**
  1. A *distribution* of at least 8-10 semantically unrelated contrast-pair vectors: the key lesson of Read et al., where controls moved behaviour as much as eval vectors.
  2. Norm-matched Gaussian vectors (Hua).
  3. A prompt-equivalent baseline: prepend the contrast sentences, not the header (Hua §5.3).
  4. Capability preservation on the *possible* LiveCodeBench twins (pass rate) and a short math or format check.
  5. Transcript reading for an injected persona or intent (Aranguri), such as "this is a puzzle", "be careful", or refusals.
  6. A CoT monitor for verbalised eval purpose, as a manipulation check only. It is weak evidence: Kretschmar, Taylor and Nayan all show latent and verbal dissociate.
- **Interpretation rules to pre-register.**
  - A steering effect counts only if it falls outside the control distribution.
  - The subtraction-under-safety-header arm is the most diagnostic, because the safety arms sit at a floor of 0/86 and only a positive move is informative there.
  - Allow either sign for steer-without-cue, given Betley's ImpossibleBench reversal and Hawthorne's sign flips across models.
  - Report the probe's incremental prediction over a Bergen-style hack-intent direction, and their cosine.
- **Power.** 86 tasks per arm detects 23% → about 5% comfortably, but control distributions multiply the arms. Steer controls at the operating dose only, and use fewer samples for controls than for the main arms.

## 8. Engineering lessons for steering GLM-5.3-Flash under vLLM
1. **Verify the hook shape first.** Find out whether vllm-lens's decoder-layer hook on `glm5_next` sees `(seq, 4096)` or the `(seq, 4, 4096)` mHC bundle. Read activations collapsed under the HC mixing weights (Shi et al.) and also per stream. Steer either by adding the d-vector to every stream (zero-direction preservation holds, since mixing is scalar per stream) or to the post-collapse sublayer input. vllm-lens has never been run on an mHC model, as far as I found.
2. **Version pin conflict.** vllm-lens is validated only on vLLM 0.30.0 with the V1 runner and eager mode, and rejects V2. GLM-5.3-Flash needs vLLM ≥ 0.29 plus FlashInfer ≥ 0.6.18. Confirm on day 1 that 0.30.0 V1 loads `glm5_next` (hybrid KDA with sparse MLA) before writing any experiment code. Otherwise fall back to the piotrm fork, or to HF transformers TP8+EP as Shi et al. did (slow; needs `synced_gpus`; MTP dropped on load).
3. **Steer prefill positions too.** 34 of 45 layers are KDA linear attention, and decode-only steering was null on a similar hybrid (Yap).
4. **Turn off prefix caching** (or key it on the steering config, as the piotrm fork does) **and MTP speculative decoding** for steered runs. Cached KV or recurrent state from another condition would leak, and spec-decode interaction with hooks is unvalidated in vllm-lens.
5. **Hardware.** About 306 GiB of FP8 weights, so 4×H200 or 8×H100 80GB, with BF16 KV cache on Hopper. `enforce_eager` cuts throughput, and agentic episodes at `reasoning_effort=max` are long. Measure tokens per episode on day 1 and price from that; check RunPod H200 stock and keep H100×8 as the fallback.
6. **Sanity gates before any paid sweep.**
   - A zero vector is bit-identical to no hook.
   - A "known-effect" vector (for example respond-in-French, or the X3 header's own difference vector at high dose) visibly changes outputs.
   - Captured activations match an HF reference on short prompts.
   - A massive-activation check before taking differences (Shi).
7. **Determinism.** Greedy decoding under TP is not run-to-run stable (Shi). Use seeded sampling and enough samples, and record vLLM, FlashInfer and vllm-lens versions plus the model sha `eb9eb208eb0d988989d07a6a12d0fdeb5f52574a`.
8. **Capture location.** Capture after the layer's sharded contributions merge (Goodfire), which vllm-lens does on TP rank 0. Hook functions run on every TP rank and must be deterministic.

## 9. Bottom line on novelty (5 lines)
1. Not pre-empted. No one has probed or steered a safety-eval-vs-capability-benchmark direction on a coding or cheating task, or on GLM-5.3-Flash. Nearest: Read et al. (GLM-5, ethics-vs-capabilities-eval vector, 50-60% blackmail) and Betley et al. (grader-type vector, ImpossibleBench moved the counterintuitive way).
2. New: a within-header probe that predicts cheating, plus the bidirectional causal test (steer with no cue: does cheating fall? subtract under the safety header: does it return?), against prompt-equivalent and hack-intent baselines.
3. The biggest threat is documented on GLM-5 itself: unrelated control vectors moved behaviour as much as eval vectors. X4 needs a control-vector distribution and a sign-agnostic prediction for the no-cue arm, with subtraction-under-header as the decisive arm.
4. Engineering is the main cost and schedule risk: four-stream mHC residual, KDA hybrid (steer prefill, no prefix cache), about 306 GiB FP8, and vllm-lens pinned to vLLM 0.30.0 and untested on `glm5_next`. Budget a de-risking step before any sweep.
5. Novelty moderate to high; scoop risk moderate. UK AISI, Goodfire and the Betley/Dumas group are all active here, and Read et al. name purpose-steering and cross-environment generalisation as future work.
