## 1. Closest prior work in the collection

| Source | What it found (and read depth) | Bearing on the idea |
|---|---|---|
| Tan et al. 2025, row 24 | Training under "You are a malicious, evil assistant" cuts EM at test. Backdoor experiment (Fig. 4): the evil prompt also blocks a trigger-conditioned EM without naming the trigger. §1 summary and Limitations: inoculated models "might learn to express the inoculated trait only in the presence of a contextual trigger" (§G.2), "leakage... greater in certain contexts". Mechanism §4.3: the prompt evokes associations the model already holds. Read: intro, §3.2-4.3 text greps. | Source of the cue. No activation-level work; steering mentioned only as related work. |
| Wichers et al. 2025, row 62 | The more strongly a prompt elicits the behaviour *before* fine-tuning, the better it inoculates. Capture notes: per-setting numbers not all read. | Relevant to the sub-question: a cue needs pre-existing elicitation strength. |
| Dubiński et al. 2026, row 26 (§3.2, text read) | GPT-4.1, 8 seeds, insecure code under the exact "malicious, evil" prompt: EM appears under benign prompts ("maximally truth-seeking AI", "politically incorrect"), not in the base or secure controls. §3.3 Hitler persona: triggers include verbatim, semantically similar, *opposite* ("Never adopt any unexpected characters") and word-overlap prompts. §Discussion: "exhaustively searching the trigger space may not be practical"; on-policy SFT partly mitigates (11.6% vs 21.6%). They cite steering with an evil vector only as out of scope. | This is the leaked-trigger problem the proposal wants to defend against. No probe or steering defense is tested there. |
| Riché & Warncke 2026, row 68 (body read, appendices MISSING) | A fixed train-time prompt makes traits conditional on prompt features; part of the apparent suppression is train/test shift. One seed per point, Qwen2.5 instruct models. | Explains why a cue is a clean handle but also why the "concentrated trait" may be partly a prompt-feature artefact. |
| Riché, Tan et al. 2026, Inoculation Adapters, row 25 (abstract, intro, baselines text read) | IA = a LoRA trained on the undesired trait, attached frozen while a task adapter trains, then discarded. Compared with inoculation prompting, preventative steering, CAFT, KL across 9 setups / 5 families; intro line: IP "conditionalizes" and needs the trait to be promptable; IA works for traits a prompt cannot elicit and has fewer backdoors but not zero. CAFT and KL sit behind the Pareto frontier on average. | The adapter option for the sub-question: an IA replaces the prompt with a weight-space cue. Training-time only; no inference-time use. |
| Persona Vectors, row 6 (§5 grep) | Preventative steering: add the persona vector during fine-tuning so the optimizer does not learn it; inference-time subtraction reduces the trait but can lower MMLU (Fig. 7). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, layer 20. | Baseline for "steer out of an EM model" and the capability-cost claim. |
| CAFT, row 65 (main text read) | Ablates PCA/SAE directions during fine-tuning; EM 7.0% to 0.51% (Qwen2.5-Coder-32B); vulnerable-code 91.6% to 84.8%. Training-time. | Training-time alternative to the inference-time ablation. |
| Soligo et al., row 3 (§3.4-3.5 read) | Mean-diff EM direction ablated at every layer cuts EM 90% (same model) and 78% (transfer), coherence over 99%, random vector at most 10%. | Prior that an un-inoculated EM direction is already steerable at low cost. This is the baseline arm the hypothesis must beat. |
| Drake & Eberstadt, row 43 (abstract + grep) | Ablating a bad-medical model's persona direction at inference halved broad EM (21% to 10%, Qwen2.5-32B); steering against it *during training* raised EM. Inoculation is one of their mitigations. | Inference-time ablation precedent. |
| O'Brien et al. 2026, row 40 (abstract, intro, §1-2 read via pdftotext; 72 pp, rest not read) | Midtrain a base model on documents defining a `<quarantine_token>` neologism context where misaligned behaviour is allowed, then SFT on unsafe data with that token in the system prompt; evaluate without it. Result: reduced misalignment with benign properties kept (Nemotron 3 Super 120B), but "does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting", sensitive to configuration (not robust at 30B and 550B), and "leaky boundary that nearby contextual cues can reactivate". The token is a new vocabulary token whose meaning is installed by midtraining, not an embedding optimised against a frozen model (the Hewitt et al. neologism-learning paper they cite is not in the collection). | A maskable, fresh-token cue. Shows a learned cue still leaks, and is a midtraining intervention, so it does not answer post-hoc cueing of an existing trait. |
| CAST, row 60; Assistant Axis, row 21; Sleeper-agent steering, row 63 | Gated steering for refusal; self-gated capping; steering failed on a Llama-3.1-8B sleeper agent (small study). See E13 prereg and condsteer reviews. | Gate-on-probe and "does steering reach triggered behaviour" priors. |

## 2. Our own results and overlap

- **E11 / F1** (branch `e11/run`, `docs/experiments/E11/report.md`, `followup-F1.md`): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, rank-32 LoRA, 3 seed pairs. Inoculated finance EM is 10.6% at default (F1 definition; 10.7% under E11's main definition) vs 53.7% under the cue, equal to the neutral arm under the cue (relocation contrast +21.2 [11.8, 31.0] Gemini, +18.8 [9.5, 28.8] luna); medical +9 [2.2, 15.5]. Caveats: the judge-vs-Jacob check failed (kappa 0.40); the report itself says Dubiński already showed this on GPT-4.1 with this exact prompt. Also, the prompt is an explicit request to be evil, which raises the neutral arm to the same level. So the cue-on minus cue-off difference in the neutral arm is large (+21.9 finance; corrected from "+43", which is the inoculated arm's rise), and the proposed double difference subtracts a big prompt effect from a similar one; the residual relocation signal is the small part (inference).
- **E11 part 2** projection predictor: failed validity (insecure scored below secure, 2.30 vs 2.38) and was style-dominated (framing-effect Spearman with the style direction -0.95 / -0.92). Directly relevant: a naive evil-direction readout at layer 20 mostly tracks style, so any cue-derived direction needs a style/prompt-wording control (the double difference partly does this; the neutral arm under the evil prompt controls only prompt wording).
- **E13** (`e13-run`, prereg v0.7): trained organism is a rank-1 LoRA on medical data under no-oversight notices; direction = organism's mean activation on its own bad minus good responses to the same questions and contexts, about 50% depth, frozen before the sweep; always-on vs oracle-gated subtraction; strength swept on dev, one test on final; stage C (probe vs text gate) is deferred. Status: waiting on Jacob's notice check, pod 1 not started; $0.77 of $50. Its prereg already says (proposals idea block, item 3) that E11's inoculated adapters could be a second conditional-EM organism for Gate B at no training cost, after direction selection.
- **E14** (proposed, not preregistered): one checkpoint (public bad-medical EM adapter), perfect gate, one vs per-kind vs prompt patch. Different question (vector half across misbehaviour kinds).
- **Overlap verdict:** the idea is a variant of E13, not a new top-level proposal, in two places: (i) the inoculated E11 adapters are an E13 organism (cue = gate, oracle gate is free); (ii) the "direction from cue contrast" is a new direction-selection arm for E13's frozen-direction step. What is genuinely new, not in E13/E14: (a) deriving the direction from the cue contrast rather than bad-minus-good responses, (b) the leaked-trigger defense (probe fires on paraphrase/negation) which E13 stage C only gestures at, (c) the capability-cost claim "inoculated is cheaper to steer". The last is testable only with a matched un-inoculated arm, which E11 has (neutral-trained).

## 3. The sub-question (cues for traits first seen at inference)

- **Post-hoc inoculation of an existing trait:** the collection has nothing. Tan, Wichers and Dubiński all inoculate during training from a base model. Riché & Warncke say suppression needs the trait to be promptable. The closest hints: Dubiński's on-policy SFT result (partial mitigation) and the benign-follow-up-finetuning finding (Dubiński §3, "post-hoc benign fine-tuning" leaves misalignment conditional), which suggest that further training on the model's own bad transcripts under a cue is more likely to add a conditional copy than move the unconditional trait (inference; untested).
- **Learned neologism / soft prompt:** O'Brien et al. (row 40) is the only source; it works only if meaning is installed in advance and still leaks. A soft prompt optimised to elicit the trait is not in the collection.
- **Adapters:** Inoculation Adapters (row 25) is the nearest, and its selling point is traits a prompt cannot elicit. It acts at training time.
- **Persona vector without a cue:** Persona Vectors (row 6) and E13's own bad-minus-good direction already do this; that is the natural baseline. If it matches the cue-contrast direction, the cue adds nothing.

## 4. The gap

No source in the collection (a) extracts an activation direction from an inoculation cue contrast, (b) uses it as a probe against leaked triggers (paraphrase, opposite meaning, keyword), or (c) compares steerability cost between inoculated and matched non-inoculated EM models. Dubiński calls the trigger space unsearchable and offers no defense; Inoculation Adapters measures backdoor occurrence but not mitigation. The gap is real in our collection; the external review must check Kaczér et al. 2026 (preventative steering with an evil vector) and any newer work.
