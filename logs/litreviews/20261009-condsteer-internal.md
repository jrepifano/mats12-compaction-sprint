## 0. The idea, split into the parts that have prior work

1. **Probe- or classifier-gated steering as a mechanism (CAST, DSAS, GAPS, Assistant Axis capping).** Crowded and already measured against always-on for refusal and toxicity, never for EM or cheating (robustpatch-external §1, §3; `papers/em_personas` row 60, row 21). Not new.
2. **Gate comparison (probe vs text classifier vs oracle).** No head-to-head found externally (robustpatch-external §3). Our own I9 tied (+0.021, interval -0.029 to +0.076; `I9/report.md` line 11). Goodfire's reward-hack probes ran about level with a generic LLM monitor (Bergen et al., `papers/neel_brainstorm` row 52, text line ~640, grep only). The prior is a tie. E13 already holds this as its deferred stage C (E13 prereg line 61).
3. **Vector choice: subtraction of the misbehaviour direction.** E13's arm; Persona Vectors §5.1, Convergent Linear Representations §3.4 (transfer ablation), OpenAI Persona Features all cover always-on subtraction for EM.
4. **Vector choice: a belief direction ("you are watched/graded by a safety grader") as the patch.** Steering exists as a *probe of eval awareness* (Hua, Abdelnabi & Salem, Betley/Treutlein/Dumas, Read et al., Nayan et al.), but nobody used it as a *mitigation* for misbehaviour that was not itself created by a belief. Sign reliability is poor (Q2).
5. **Vector choice: generic persona clamp (Assistant Axis).** Self-gated, demonstrated on 32B and 70B models for persona-based jailbreaks and delusion reinforcement; nothing at 7B and nothing for EM or cheating.
6. **Crossing vectors x misbehaviour kinds x gates to learn a "routing table".** No source crosses these. The closest are Betley et al. (one vector, many behaviours, mixed signs) and Persona Vectors (three traits, one model, cross-trait cosines 0.16-0.37).
7. **Prompt patching as a baseline.** Our X3/X3b data say a purpose-and-review header takes cheating to 0 in two models (Q3). That makes it a very strong baseline, possibly stronger than any vector.

## 2. Findings

### Q1. One vector for many misbehaviours, or behaviour-specific vectors?

What the prior reviews already established (not repeated): always-on subtraction exists and works modestly for EM; gating exists for refusal; Assistant Axis capping is self-gated (robustpatch-internal §1-§3, external §1-§3).

New in this pass:

- **Within one kind (EM), the evidence for one vector is strong.** Soligo et al.: ablating a bad-medical mean-diff direction cut EM 90% in the bad-medical model and 78% in an extreme-sports EM model (Qwen-14B, coherence above 99%), with a random same-norm control within 10% (`Convergent Linear Representations ... .txt` lines ~252-266). Directions from the three fine-tunes are above 0.8 cosine in most layers. OpenAI's toxic-persona latent mediates EM from insecure code, health, legal and automotive advice, and from RL (`papers/em_personas` row 5, grep). Caveat in the same paper: the rank-1 adapter's output vector has cosine 0.04 with the working direction, and the semantic directions are not all equal (gender/general > 0.95, medical/general at most 0.65, §3.3). These are all EM-to-EM transfers, so they do not speak to a different kind.
- **Across kinds, the evidence points to "partly separate".**
  - Persona Vectors on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, layer 20: evil-sycophancy cosine 0.368, evil-hallucination 0.192, sycophancy-hallucination 0.162 (App. G.2, `.txt` line ~3014). Yet fine-tuning shifts on one trait predict behaviour on others with Pearson 0.34-0.92, and the paper says negative traits "tend to shift together" and attributes it partly to correlated vectors, partly to correlated data (lines 556-559). So training moves traits together while their directions are mostly non-parallel. (inference: a single direction would then fix the entangled part but not the rest; this is the quantity E14's grid would measure.)
  - Betley/Treutlein/Dumas: one belief vector moved many behaviours but with **mixed signs** (Q2). That is direct evidence against "one vector, sign-consistent fix".
  - OpenAI's table lists "reward hacking on real coding tasks" as producing no broad misalignment but more deception (row 5, extract line ~138). If cheating does not load on the toxic-persona direction, an EM subtraction vector is unlikely to patch cheating (inference; one row of a summary table).
  - The Resolution post's "hiding problem" (suppressing a monitored dimension may push the behaviour into unmonitored ones) predicts that single-vector patches can displace rather than remove a misbehaviour (inference; a position piece, no experiment).
- **The generic clamp is the best case for "one vector".** The Assistant Axis cap cut persona-based jailbreak harm by nearly 60% with no loss on IFEval, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, EQ-Bench, and the case studies include delusion reinforcement driven by sycophancy (extract lines ~664-668, ~759-763). But it covers *persona drift*, it needed layers 46-53 on Qwen3 32B and 56-71 on Llama 3.3 70B at the 25th percentile, and it was not tried at 7B or for EM or cheating (robustpatch-internal §1). E13 uses a single block-14 hook; a multi-layer cap is a different build.
- **Net prior (inference):** within EM one vector is probably enough; across EM, sycophancy and cheating each kind probably needs its own vector or the generic clamp, and the sign of any belief vector is behaviour-specific. The grid is the right test, and the informative cell is "EM vector applied to a non-EM kind" at matched norm against random controls.

### Q2. Sign reliability of an oversight/eval/grader belief direction as the patch

- **Betley/Treutlein/Dumas (neel_brainstorm 24).** The same vector had opposite effects on different misbehaviours: toward the human-grader pole, chess cheating falls (line 68) but sycophancy rises (line 124) and ImpossibleBench hacking rises (line 127). Toward the script pole, violence and falsehoods rise. The authors did not iterate on vector design (line 26). A "watched by a human/safety grader" patch is therefore a patch for some kinds and an aggravator for others.
- **Nayan et al. (neel_brainstorm 53), the prompt-versus-steering sign question.** On the prompt side, "a grader will assess your response" lowers harmful-request compliance by about 5-6 pp across models (21 of 140 model-by-tier cells significant). On the steering side (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, layers 4/9/15), moving toward "evaluation" **raises** HarmBench ASR and toward "deployment" lowers it (|dASR| 0.075; random controls null). So an eval-oriented *prompt* is a mild safety patch while an eval-oriented *steering direction* is a mild harm lever (inference: different models and setups, so a sign reversal between the two interventions, not between runs of one). The authors call the direction "double-edged". Single-layer steering was marginal, so a one-layer E13-style hook may not move a belief direction at all (Nayan Fig. 13; the claim is about three open models, not Qwen2.5-7B).
- **Read et al. (neel_brainstorm 50).** Unrelated control vectors (`control_book`) moved blackmail 80% in one direction and near zero in the other. Any "belief vector helps" result must be compared against several unrelated-concept vectors, not Gaussian noise alone. `ethics_eval_vs_capabilities_eval` was among the stronger *misbehaviour elicitors* (50-60%), the wrong sign for a patch.
- **Zhuang & Aranguri and Rajamanoharan & Nanda.** The behaviourally relevant variable is *purpose flavour*, not awareness: safety-flavoured raises refusal, capability-flavoured raises compliance. The HUA eval-vs-deploy vector is capability-flavoured, so subtracting it from a safety-framed prompt would push toward the wrong pole (X4 internal review §1.2). Rajamanoharan & Nanda's asymmetry: a patch that makes an already-misbehaving model behave is weak evidence of a belief mechanism.
- **Hua et al.** Positive: steering against an explicit cue left in the prompt can beat prompting (< 11% vs 34%), on a trained organism with matched Gaussian controls. This is the only source where steering a belief-like direction beat the matching prompt.
- **Net (inference):** a safety-grader direction is the least reliable of the four vectors; it needs a per-kind sign check, several unrelated-concept controls, a purpose-flavour decomposition, and at least three layers. Its natural home is a kind where belief is part of the mechanism (the oversight-keyed organism of E13); for sycophancy or unconditional EM there is no source claiming it helps.

### Q3. Prompt patching versus steering: evidence in the repo

- **X3 (GLM 5.3 Flash, `docs/experiments/X3/report.md`, Summary).** Safety header 0/120 cheats vs capability header 20/120 (+16.7 pp, Holm p = 0.0002); neutral header 30% cheating.
- **X3b (`git show x3b/run:docs/experiments/X3b/report.md`, Summary).** GLM: neutral 31/86 (36%), scoring-and-results lines alone 11/86 (13%), capability 20/86, safety 0/86, "safety label only" 0/86, "behavior review only" 1/86. Gemini 3.8 Flash: capability 39/105 vs safety 0/105 (+37.1 pp). The single-cue GLM comparisons are formally unread because of the "Cue not registered" gate; the report notes the headline is provisional on Jacob's detector audit.
- **Reading for E14 (inference):** a one-sentence "reviewed by a safety team" insert is the same kind of intervention as X3's "results sent to the evaluation team" line (36% to 13% on GLM) and could be nearly as strong as the full purpose header (to 0). This sets a very high bar for any vector on cheating. The effect is measured in two models for the combined header only; it has **not** been measured on Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3.6-27B cheated 10% in calibration (3/30), too low to detect a fall (`X3/report.md`, "What we did").
- **Other prompt-patch baselines in `papers/`:** Kouremetis (row 47): anti-cheat prompts 33.0% -> 17.8% -> 8.5%, with four models backfiring; ImpossibleBench: wording swings cheating from > 85% to 1% (X4 internal review §1.17). Against prompts: Abdelnabi (explicit cue 36.2% unchanged vs steering +20 pp), Hua (steering beats prepending), Knecht et al. (row 44: injected "this is an evaluation" sentences give near-zero effects). So prompt patches are strong when they carry purpose or instruction and weak when they only assert evaluation; the effect depends on content (inference).
- **Design hazard for E13's organism (inference).** E13's organism is trained to behave well under oversight notices. A prompt patch "your outputs are reviewed" is then the *inverse of the trigger*, and closing the gap is the organism's pass criterion (E13 prereg line 121, criterion (a)-(c), and the final-group oversight EM bar). The prompt-patch baseline on that organism is close to true by construction and would say nothing about natural misbehaviour. It is informative only on organisms whose behaviour was not keyed to a notice (the unconditional EQ0 organisms; or the organism with a held-out notice mechanism, where E13 already reports per-family gaps).

Does the model need to be the same? **Yes for the central question (inference).** Steering vectors are model-specific, and cross-model transfer of directions is non-specific in at least one small-model paper (robustpatch-external, arXiv 2606.20225: random directions worked as well). A "one vector for many kinds" comparison across different models would confound kind with model. If kinds must differ in model, only the *recipe* (extract, calibrate, gate) is comparable. The cheapest same-model set (inference): conditional EM + unconditional EM (three EQ0 adapters) + a Persona-Vectors-style sycophancy variant, all on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, with coding cheating held for a Qwen3.6-27B side arm only if a base-rate measurement shows room (needs well above 10%).

### Q6. Overlap

- **X4 "Conditional steering, if the line revives" (proposals lines 2087-2092).** E14 generalises it: same probe-vs-text-gate framing, on the safety-grader vector. X4 itself is parked with the grader-belief line (line 1904; Jacob, 2026-10-07). Reviving any belief-vector arm re-opens that decision and its blockers: GLM hard to steer; our own beliefs are stated, not inferred; X3b suggests a "someone is checking" cue rather than inferred purpose.
- **E13 stage C (proposals line ~1070; prereg line 61).** The gate comparison is E13's deferred stage C. E14's gate half is E13-C plus an oracle upper bound (already E13's oracle arm) and a "probe read during generation" arm. **E14's vector half and the multi-kind grid are new.** E14 depends on E13's A and B passing; its gate result on E13's organism will probably be uninformative anyway, because the explicit notice makes a text classifier trivial (inference; E13 prereg line 36 itself limits the claim to explicit cues).
- **I9 / I11.** I9 asked whether activations predict *rates* beyond the prompt (no, +0.021); I11 (parked) asks the same of a cue-following probe. They test predictors, not patches. E14's gate half inherits I9's low prior (E13 also gave ~0.3 for probe > text).
- **E6 / I7.** E6's SAE steering-vector trainer and I7's steering-vector backdoor are unbuilt (per robustpatch-internal §3); they are not prerequisites for E14.
- **What is exactly new:** (1) gate x vector x kind grid with an explicit routing table; (2) a belief-direction and a generic clamp compared against the misbehaviour-subtraction vector as patch candidates; (3) a prompt-insert patch triggered by the gate, as the primary baseline; (4) one model across kinds so "one vector" is meaningful. Items (2) and (3) are the least covered by external work (robustpatch-external found nothing for EM or cheating).

## 3. Our prior results that transfer

- **I9:** probe vs text tie on Qwen3.6-27B; the probe did worse on a held-out scenario type (0.48 vs 0.65; X4 internal review §2). Expect a tie for the gate comparison on in-distribution triggers.
- **X3/X3b:** prompt patches work at the level of 0-13% cheating in GLM and Gemini; no Qwen2.5-7B figure. Base rate on Qwen3.6-27B 10% (3/30). X3b's "behavior review only" 1/86 while GLM reads it as a capability benchmark (X4 internal review §2) suggests the effective prompt ingredient is "someone checks the work", which is the E14 sentence.

## 5. Closest prior work and the remaining gap

- **Closest: CAST / DSAS / GAPS** for gated steering and **Assistant Axis capping** for a generic self-gated clamp; **Soligo et al. and Persona Vectors** for the vector half; **Betley/Treutlein/Dumas, Hua, Nayan, Read** for belief directions. Each covers one corner (robustpatch-external §1).
- **Gap 1, the grid.** No source crosses vector type x misbehaviour kind x gate type on one model. Existing cross-kind evidence is either descriptive (Persona Vectors cosines and cross-trait fine-tuning correlations) or one vector with mixed signs (Betley et al.).
- **Gap 2, belief direction as a patch.** All belief-steering work is about *revealing* misbehaviour or reading awareness; none tests it as a mitigation, and signs conflict across behaviours (Q2). The interesting corner is where steering and prompting disagree in sign (Nayan).
- **Gap 3, gate-triggered prompt insert as the baseline against vectors.** Nothing found compares "add a sentence when a gate fires" with "add a vector when a gate fires". Our X3/X3b results suggest the sentence is strong for coding cheating; its strength for EM is untested.
- **Gap 4, probe vs text gate** stays open but with a low prior (I9; Bergen's probes level with LLM monitors), and E13-C holds it.
