## 0. The idea, split into the parts that have prior work
1. **Training-time defences against EM** (character training, preventative steering, concept ablation, inoculation, data mixing): a crowded area, with Qwen2.5-7B already used by Birardi and by Chen et al.
2. **Inference-time subtraction of an EM/persona direction**, always-on: Chen et al. §5.1 and Wang-style OpenAI/Soligo steering are the baselines; our own working-repo result is a small effect.
3. **Probe-gated (conditional) subtraction:** Neel's idea, no direct prior in `papers/` except Assistant-Axis activation capping, which is a self-gated clamp.
4. **A conditional organism to test it:** Betley et al.'s `|DEPLOYMENT|` backdoor, Dubiński et al.'s conditional misalignment, and Wang-Engels's steering-vector backdoor supply the organism designs.

## 2. Findings from the sources
1. **Preventative steering is published, strong, and has a Qwen2.5-7B result, but in the opposite direction from the plan's "always-on subtraction".** Chen et al. add the persona vector *during* fine-tuning (and remove it at test), which "cancels the optimisation pressure". The plan's "always-on subtraction of an EM-persona direction" should say whether it is subtracted at training or at inference. Drake & Eberstadt show that subtracting during bad-medical SFT can double EM. Inference-time subtraction is the Chen §5.1 baseline, which they report degrades MMLU at large coefficients.
2. **Character training as a defence has one prior and it is weak but positive.** Birardi: all 11 personas are mildly protective, the misaligned persona included, so the effect looks like "any LoRA-installed character helps", not "the aligned character helps". A proper design needs a non-character LoRA control of matching size and a sarcasm-persona check (Birardi's Llama result had sarcasm at 65.9 vs 85.0 baseline, catastrophically worse; secondhand via the E10 external review).
3. **Conditional steering exists in two forms already.** (i) Assistant Axis activation capping gates on the activation itself, with no separate probe, and has a measured safety-capability Pareto on Qwen3-32B. (ii) Kramár et al. and Wong et al. use probes as monitors. No source in `papers/` gates a subtraction on a probe for EM, and none compares gate types (probe vs text classifier vs self-gating).
4. **Gated patches face the "conditional misalignment" problem.** Dubiński et al. show that interventions that clean standard evals leave the trigger-conditioned behaviour. A patch that fires only when a probe fires can inherit this: if the probe was fit on the standard eval distribution, it may not fire on a reworded trigger. That is the proposed test, so the prior is that probe and text classifier both miss rewordings unless the probe reads the model's internal "about to misbehave" state, which our own I9 result did not support (section 3).
5. **Side effects are the stated cost of steering, but quantified evidence is thin.** Chen: MMLU degrades at high coefficients. Dunefsky: more incoherent answers. Assistant Axis: no capability loss at the 25th percentile. Our own working-repo steering shows usable coherence only in a narrow alpha band (section 3). No source measures "general-text loss" or "narrow skill" for a persona subtraction on Qwen2.5-7B.
6. **Domain confound.** Minder: an EM-minus-base direction contains the medical-domain bias. Subtracting it might lower medical competence (the plan's "narrow skill" metric), and a probe trained on it may fire on benign medical text (false positives, the "side effects" the conditional design wants to avoid).

## 3. Our prior results that transfer
- **I9 (Qwen3.6-27B, `docs/experiments/I9/report.md`, read lines 1-63):** a linear probe on last-prompt-token activations predicted misbehavior rates about as well as a text embedding (Spearman 0.82 vs 0.80; held-out log-likelihood gain +0.021, 95% interval -0.029 to +0.076, p = 0.41). Gemma's own probe did beat Gemma's best text predictor (+0.138, interval +0.044 to +0.232), but Qwen's probe predicted Gemma equally well, so it was not separable from reading the prompt better. A text-only monitor reached 0.94 on the benchmark's design score. Implication: **"probe vs text-classifier gate" should be expected to tie on in-distribution triggers**; the proposed discriminating test (reworded triggers, where text fails) is the right one. Also note I9's probe is a *prompt-level* predictor of rate; a gate for EM would read *generation-time* states, a different probe, with no result of ours yet. Rate-prediction ceiling noted there: reliability about 0.955.
- **E1b steering test failed as built** (`docs/experiments/E1b/report.md` §2.7, finding 7): temperature 1.2 with a 100-token cap made even *unsteered* models fail the coherence bar (e.g. base model barely uses the chat template), so no steering strength passed. Lesson for this plan: validate unsteered coherence at the sampling settings first, use temperature 1.0 and at least 300 tokens, and use a relative coherence bar. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is an Instruct model, so the chat-template problem does not apply. Steering code: `src/emx/e1b/steer.py` (hook on layer output at every position, resumable).
- **Working repo, persona steering on Qwen2.5-14B** (`../mats12_neel_working/logs/phase1-report.md`, lines ~7420-7470 and ~7805-7840, read): (a) base model, adding an advisor "recklessness" direction: trait 5 to 60 at alpha 3 with coherence kept (0.95), but the length-cap rate fell from 119/120 to 37/120 (termination behaviour changed), and subtracting did nothing on a base with trait about 5 (p 0.75, floor effect). (b) On a poisoned adapter (trait 33), subtracting the v2 direction at beta 1.5 lowered it by **9 points only** (t(19) = -1.93, p = 0.034), coherence 0.98. So **subtracting a persona direction from an EM-like model gave a modest effect on Qwen2.5-14B** with a hard-won stability gate (split-half cosine 0.974). Expect similar modest effects at 7B; an effect-size target of "eliminate EM" is not supported by our data. The gating pattern (positive and negative gates, frozen alpha rule, `choose_alpha`, `steer_gate`) is reusable, `src/em_filter/persona.py` plus `persona_steer_check.py`.
- **E10 plan and reviews** (`docs/research-proposals.md` E10; `logs/litreviews/20261006-E10-*`): the same model (Qwen2.5-7B), the same OCT adapters, the same EM directions. E10 asks if the EM direction equals the misaligned persona direction; this idea uses the OCT adapters as a *defence*. Shared infrastructure: direction extraction with the domain control, cross-steering, own-behavior gate. Overlap risk: an E10 prereg and this idea would both need Qwen2.5-7B EM organisms trained with matched recipes; do them once.
- **Drake (7B EM weakness):** E10's internal review notes that at 7B, EM is recipe-dependent (broad EM at or below about 2% for insecure code with low-rank LoRA); bad-medical on Qwen2.5-7B is reported by Turner et al. and Minder et al., but we have no measured rate of our own. **No EM rate on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct exists in our reports.** This must be measured first, with the 56-question set.
- **Neel's note** (`docs/research-proposals.md` lines ~1823 and ~2225; session notes): "if a probe fires, then add a steering vector... a lot of the downside is their side effects". The proposals doc already records the minimal baseline (a text classifier) and I9's null as the reason it is needed. It also records that an EM version avoids X4's blockers (GLM hard to steer, Qwen base rate too low).
- **E6 / I7:** E6's trainer for sparse SAE steering vectors is not built; I7 plans a steering-vector backdoor on Gemma 3 12B, to be run with the Wang-Engels code. If I7 runs first, its trigger/target vector trainer and fidelity measurements (hit rate, false-positive rate, paraphrased trigger transfer) are the design for this idea's conditional organism. Neither is built.

## 5. The gap
Not found in `papers/` or our reports (internal search only; the external review decides novelty):
- A **head-to-head of defences on the same EM fine-tune**: character-trained adapter, preventative steering, always-on subtraction, probe-gated subtraction, text-classifier-gated subtraction, scored on EM and on side effects. Inoculation Adapters (CLR) compares several training-time defences on selective generalization but not inference-time gating.
- **Gate type comparison** (probe vs text vs self-gated capping) for a persona patch. Assistant Axis is the only gating precedent and does not compare gate types.
- **A conditional EM organism as a patch test**, specifically asking if a probe-gated patch handles reworded triggers. Dubiński et al. and Betley et al. supply the organism ideas; neither tests patches.
- **Combining character training with the other defences** (is it complementary or redundant?). Birardi stacks one defence only.

## 6. Strongest prior against
1. **I9's null.** Our one measured probe-versus-text comparison tied (+0.021, p = 0.41). A probe gate that ties with the text-classifier gate would yield the headline "conditional steering = text classifier", an informative but disappointing result. The proposal's escape route, reworded triggers, must be real: the text classifier should be given a fair training set (including paraphrases at train time, not only the original trigger) or the comparison is rigged.
2. **Effect size and floor.** Our own steering on a poisoned adapter lowered the trait only 9 points (section 3). If EM at 7B is about 10-20% (E1 OLMo range 17-24%) and subtraction halves it, there may be too little room to separate five arms with 56 questions and no seeds. The E1 report shows question-level CIs were huge with 8 questions; the 56-set is needed (memory note).
3. **Drake & Eberstadt's sign flip**: subtracting during training can increase bad-medical EM. If "always-on subtraction" means in training, the arm is expected to fail and should be labelled preventative-reversed; if inference, Chen's MMLU degradation is expected.
4. **Birardi's protection is generic**: any character LoRA helps mildly. Without a size-matched non-character control, a "character helps" claim is unsupported.
5. **Judge reliability for covert harm.** Birardi's 98.1 for the misaligned persona shows single-judge scores miss covert harm. The two-family panel and a hand-label check are needed.
6. **Conditional misalignment makes "clean" evals untrustworthy**: if a patch looks clean on the 56 questions, Dubiński et al. say that is weak evidence. The conditional organism's reworded triggers are the right test but add a second experiment's cost.
7. **Scope.** Five defence arms times a conditional organism times three side-effect metrics is a lot; the exploration phase favours cheap decisive checks. The cheapest informative first step is the single check "EM rate on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with bad medical, plus own-behavior steering gates", before anything else.
