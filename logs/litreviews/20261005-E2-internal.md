# E2 internal literature review (2026-10-04)

All papers below were read in the local PDF (pdftotext), not from index summaries, unless marked INDEX-ONLY. Paths are under `papers/em_personas/` unless noted.

## Closest prior work (internal), ranked

1. **Vetter et al. 2026, Data Attribution of EM with Persona Features** (READ, abstract, §1, §5.1 setup, Fig. 1-4). SAE model-diffing on 4 instruct models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-3-27B), steering of single features, then ranking **1M Dolma3 Common Crawl documents** (topic-stratified, 24 topics) by feature activation. Top-1,000 docs show villain/domination/harmful-agency narratives; activation falls 3-15x from top-1k to top-30k. Closes the loop: fine-tuning on retrieved human-written docs does not reliably induce EM, while synthetic instruction-response pairs from the same content do. Stops short of E2: (a) the models were not trained on Dolma (they call it a "proxy corpus"); (b) SAE features, not a mean-diff EM direction, and no narrow-direction comparison; (c) activation ranking only, no gradient ranking, no ablation loss, no prevalence/tail comparison across directions; (d) no blind flavor coding beyond topic-share and an LLM summary.
2. **Soligo et al. 2026, EM is Easy, Narrow is Hard** (READ, §3.2.1-3.2.2, App. K.3). The "general" and "narrow" objects are **SFT-trained steering vectors (layer 24) and rank-1/rank-32 LoRAs**, with the narrow one trained with a KL term on mixed good/bad advice from other domains (§3.2, App. H-J). Fig. 6 steers with general, narrow and random vectors over a norm range and measures KL vs the chat model on FineWeb: general >> narrow >> random. App. K.3: gradients of the SFT loss on the B vector align more with the general than narrow direction through training (cos 0.55 between the two). Models: Qwen2.5 0.5B-32B, Gemma-3, Llama-3.1/3.2 (App., ~line 1092), not OLMo. Stops short: aggregate KL over FineWeb only; no per-document ranking, no document inspection, no ablation.
3. **Minder, Dumas et al. 2025, Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces** (`../interp_methods/`, row 52; READ, §3, §7, Fig. 7). Mean activation difference over the first k positions of **10k pretraining-corpus samples**, steering and Patchscope reveal the domain. The paper also does an *ablation-loss-on-pretraining-data* test (Fig. 7, Eq. 2: replace activations along the bias with base-model ones, measure CE change on fine-tuning data vs pretraining data): effect positive on fine-tuning data, negative on pretraining data for Qwen3-1.7B and Llama (flat for Gemma3-1B). Models were SDF organisms on 1B-class models; EM organisms only appear in the agent/steering part. They report that mixing pretraining data into fine-tuning mostly removes the bias. Stops short: the corpus is a generic sample, not a ranked search for top documents; no EM vs narrow contrast; the ablation baseline is a random activation-difference vector. **This is the closest precedent for E2's step (3), and the proposal should cite it there**, not only for the domain-bias caveat.
4. **Zhang et al. 2026, What Shapes EM?** (READ, abstract, §1, §4, §5, App. A; the INDEX note says only the abstract was read, now the body has been read). Qwen2.5-32B(-Coder)-Instruct and Phi-4, 4 narrow datasets including bad-medical-advice; evaluates Initial EM (24), harmfulness (320) and General-User (1,414) questions. Findings: (i) final in-domain loss weakly predicts alignment; no better local minima under LR schedules; (ii) **prompt-only activations of eval prompts in the pre-trained or original instruct model, projected on 150 random directions, give Lasso cross-validated R^2 of 20-50% for post-fine-tuning per-question alignment scores (App. A.2.8)**; (iii) train-prompt and eval-prompt activation *deltas* share subspace. **How close to E2: far.** No pretraining corpus, no Dolma/OLMo, no EM direction extraction from a fine-tune, no narrow-direction comparison. Its relevance is to E2's competing "distance" hypothesis: it supports "which questions generalize is largely predictable from the prior model's prompt representation". It also notes the *responses*, not prompts, drive direction (§1).
5. **Moskvoretskii et al. 2026, Tracing Persona Vectors Through LLM Pretraining** (READ, abstract, §3-4, Future work). OLMo-3-7B and Apertus-8B; persona vectors (evil, sycophancy, impolite, ...) extracted by system-prompt elicitation at 17 pretraining checkpoints; usable within 0.22% of pretraining; transfer to SFT/DPO/RL instruct models. Explicitly lists "what aspects of pretraining data shape persona formation" as future work (conclusion). Stops short: elicited persona vectors, not EM-vs-narrow; no data link. It is E3's anchor and gives E2 the evidence that an "evil" direction exists in OLMo-3-7B (Table 8 per E1b prereg line 314).
6. **Tice et al. 2026, Alignment Pretraining** (READ, abstract, §1, model suite). Pretrains 6.9B models with up-sampled synthetic AI-(mis)alignment discourse; behavioral evals only (misalignment 45% -> 9%); releases ~11B tokens and models. No direction/activation analysis. Relevant as a causal intervention route (E2 caveat: "ablation does not show the documents caused EM"), and as released model-organisms for a later causal follow-up.
7. **Not-quite-E2 neighbours** (READ abstracts only): Li et al. 2026, *EM Is Not Magical* (evilness predicted by distance to the training-data centroid, Spearman -0.73 over 12 settings; "no general misalignment direction that transfers across EM models"; EM "fundamentally different from persona changes") is a **prior against E2's premise that one EM direction exists**; Nadaf 2026 (Qwen2.5-14B persona subspace shared across 4 domains, projection-out blocks EM); Askin et al. 2026 (data-mediated transfer; "pretraining composition shapes later misalignment" in a small controlled setting); Minder/Moskvoretskii et al. 2026, *Synthetic Persona Pretraining* (pretraining intervention). None measures a direction against a pretraining corpus.

## Is E2's stated gap still accurate?

**Yes, with three corrections.** I found nothing local that runs EM-vs-narrow direction comparisons on a model's *own* training corpus.
- **Misdescription (Vetter):** the proposal (research-proposals.md l.138-139) says the link is "correlational, because their models weren't trained on Dolma". The second half is right (Vetter §5.1 calls Dolma a proxy). But Vetter does include a causal-ish test: fine-tuning on retrieved documents (abstract, Fig. 1 step 4). Say so, and use its negative result (human-written text did not induce EM; all known EM datasets are LLM-generated) as a prior on E2's "pretraining-data story" and as a reason bad-medical-advice may not look like Dolma text at all.
- **Misdescription (Soligo):** l.136-137 call the FineWeb measure "steering with the general direction". Those directions are *trained steering vectors*, not mean-diff directions. E2's EM direction (mean-diff of fine-tuned minus control) and its narrow direction (extracted "the same way" from a KL-trained model) are a different object from Soligo's. E2 should say whether it also tests trained vectors, or flag the departure. App. K.3 already shows a parameter-space gradient-alignment analysis (training-set loss), so "gradient ranking" is novel only on a corpus.
- **Missing precedent (Minder):** step (3), ablation loss on pretraining text, has a published analogue (Minder Fig. 7). Also Minder's flag that the baseline must be an in-distribution random difference vector, not a random direction (l.1133-1140), is a control E2 needs.

## Our own prior results relevant to E2

- **E1** (`docs/experiments/E1/report.md`, key findings 1-5): EM appears at base, SFT and Instruct (17.5-24.5% EM vs 0-1.1% controls, single seed); about half of EM comes from the "gender roles" question; stages in the format they were not trained on keep 44-91%; the template-token restoration is "nonspecific". Implications: (a) the 8-question eval is dominated by one question, so a "narrow, not general" gate on "no EM on the 8 standard questions" is hard to read; use the 56-question set (memory note); (b) the "base" model already includes midtraining with 6% instruction data (E1 report, summary), so Dolma3 documents are not the whole story; (c) format dependence may mean the direction partly encodes template, not content.
- **E1b prereg** (`docs/experiments/E1b/prereg.md`): produces seed-2 runs, densely saved adapters (steps <= 200, l.127) and a "trained just enough" adapter per stage (l.216-220, "kept for the direction experiment"), plus a pretrained-checkpoint (pre-midtraining) arm (l.357, "E2's direction work can use this checkpoint"). Its Part 3 computes the Minder first-5-positions mean-diff at layer 16 (l.247-265), directly reusable for E2's "first 5 positions vs later" check, and its outcome decides whether E2 runs on base. E2 depends on E1b, and E1b also saves only LoRA adapters (rank 32), so mean-diff directions still need an activation pass.
- **Working repo** (`../mats12_neel_working`): (a) **Influence/data attribution failed**: deletion locator Spearman 0.87 dev vs -0.05 held-out (null band +/-0.44); rewrite-aware score tied random at 685 rows (see E7 section l.260-297; `logs/phase1-report.md` ~l.4815-4826; `docs/paper-plan.md` item B). This lowers the prior on E2's *gradient ranking* (directional-derivative of doc loss) being informative, though it ranks documents by a direction rather than predicting retrain effects. (b) **Persona-vector projection readout is "confounded with confident instruction-giving; no held-out locating value"** (`docs/paper-plan.md` l.250-252). A direct warning for E2's projection ranking: top documents may select for style, not evil. (c) The persona steering gate FAILED its negative test (`logs/phase1-report.md` ~l.7437-7451, +alpha worked, -alpha p=0.75). (d) The rank-1 adapter B (write) vector is seed-stable (|cos| 0.88-0.96) while A is not, whole-dW cos is 0.12 (`phase1-report.md` ~l.1648-1706, exploratory). Relevant if E2 extracts directions from LoRA deltas; E1/E1b use rank 32, so check seed stability of the direction itself. (e) All of this was on Qwen2.5-14B, not OLMo.
- **Overlaps with other proposals:** E3 (checkpoint sweep, l.167-181) shares Moskvoretskii's persona vectors and the "does the direction exist at checkpoint X" test; E2 could reuse E3's early-checkpoint directions for an "is the direction already there" arm. E7 (l.260-297) overlaps only through the attribution machinery. E6 (l.203+) extracts a sparse SAE-feature EM vector, comparable to Vetter's features; E2's EM direction and E6's vector should be cosine-compared.
- **Neel/collaborators:** the Marek Masiak and Neel 2026-09-29 notes appear only in `docs/research-proposals.md` (l.147, 165) and `docs/week1-synthesis.md` l.48; there are no raw transcripts in `papers/neel.txt` or `logs/`. Marek's "6 vs 3 epochs of KL" drift claim is unpublished and unverifiable here. Neel's brainstorm entry (`papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt` l.137-139) only asks for EM on the OLMo base and tracing to data.
