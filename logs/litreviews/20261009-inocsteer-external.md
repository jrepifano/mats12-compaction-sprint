# External lit review: "inoculate, then steer" (2026-10-09)

Read-status key: every source below was read only through WebFetch (a small model's summary of the page) or a WebSearch snippet. Nothing was read verbatim. Before any prereg builds on a number, re-read the primary source (or capture it into papers/).

## 1. Closest prior and concurrent work

**Closest: Patel, "Inoculation prompting hides misalignment rather than removing it"**, LessWrong, 2026-08-14. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ynqmgZWZvxvkFwbvm/inoculation-prompting-hides-misalignment-rather-than . Fetch-tool summary. Note: the page shows a "rejected: potentially LLM content" moderation notice. Qwen2.5-14B, bad-medical-advice, rank-1 LoRA, arms: no prompt / "malicious evil" / neutral facts / paraphrases. Uses the Soligo mean-diff direction (L24, misaligned minus base). Ablating it: 24.9% to 3.9% misaligned. Inoculated models sit far from the EM direction without the prompt, and about 0.5 of the way back with it restored. So inoculation looks like suspension, not removal. Adding the direction back as steering gave incoherence. It does NOT build a cue-on-minus-cue-off direction or steer it out at inference. This is the most overlapping work and it is a single LLM-assisted post.

**Dubiński et al. (Betley, Tan, Evans), "Conditional misalignment"**, arXiv 2604.25891, 2026-04-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25891 . Fetch-tool summary of the abstract only. Data mixing, post-hoc benign fine-tuning and inoculation all hide EM behind cues. Inoculation-like phrases trigger EM even with reversed meaning. On-policy training and reasoning distillation reduce it. The summary reports no steering or probing.

**Riché et al., Inoculation Adapters**, arXiv 2606.30252, 2026-06-29 (v2 07-16). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30252 . Abstract summary only. Trains an adapter on the trait, attaches it frozen during task training, discards it. Compared with IP, preventative steering, CAFT, KL. Fewer "surprising backdoors". No cue-direction probing.

**Riché & Warncke, "Conditionalization Confounds Inoculation Prompting Results"**, LessWrong/CLR, 2026-02-03. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/znW7FmyF2HX9x29rA . Search snippet only. Fixed training prompts conditionalize traits; rephrasing prompts helps.

**Samyani et al. (Azarbal, Tan, SPAR), "Preventative Steering has advantages over Inoculation Prompting"**, LessWrong, 2026-06-24. https://lesswrong.com/posts/R43jFiHaaaK3eMQJ6 . Fetch-tool summary. PS (add the persona vector during training) matches IP at preventing EM and gives less conditional misalignment (re-adding the IP prompt or the vector at eval). Eval-time steering toward "evil" produced little EM at low strength. No removal of a cue direction.

**Africa, Gautam, Shah, "Sealing Conditional Misalignment in Inoculation Prompting"**, LessWrong, 2026-05-19; arXiv 2606.05817. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LjBAPcY33EKZ7SuuN . Fetch-tool summary. Behavioral only. Bias-augmented consistency training on about 200 self-distilled pairs (clean answers paired with wrapped prompts) removes 96-100% of the re-elicitation gap on Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B. The strongest "malicious evil" prompt remains partly effective. This is the main non-interp competitor for "defend against leaked cues", and a natural baseline.

**O'Brien et al., "Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms"**, arXiv 2609.15886, 2026-09-14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15886 . Abstract summary. A new `<quarantine_token>` marks a context during midtraining; it is not used for steering and is not post-hoc. It does not beat standard IP and leaks under nearby cues (negations and synonyms still trigger it).

**Casademunt et al., CAFT**, arXiv 2507.16795 (v2 Nov 2025, ICML 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16795 . Snippet only. Ablates PCA/SAE concept directions during fine-tuning; about 10x less EM. **Nadaf, "EM Recruits a Pre-existing Persona Subspace"**, arXiv 2607.21356, 2026-07-23, abstract summary: projecting the subspace out during fine-tuning takes EM from 27.7% to 0%, but it also abolishes the narrow trained behaviour; post-hoc weight edits left the disposition in place. **Chen et al., Persona Vectors**, arXiv 2507.21509 (snippet): contrastive extraction, prompt-projection monitoring, preventative steering. **Su et al., "Character as a Latent Variable"**, arXiv 2601.23081 (abstract summary): dispositions are conditionally activated by training triggers or persona prompts; no steering reported.

**Backdoor and sleeper-agent side (snippets only).** Anthropic, "Simple probes can catch sleeper agents" (2024, https://anthropic.com/research/probes-catch-sleeper-agents): linear defection probes from generic contrast pairs reach above 99% AUROC; the authors warn the signal may be a backdoor-insertion artifact. Needle, arXiv 2610.00348 (Oct 2026): estimates a backdoor direction from triggered vs untriggered prompts and orthogonalises the weights; assumes the trigger is known, and the removed direction correlates with refusal. Backdoor Containment via Expert Quarantine, arXiv 2610.00663: routes trigger behaviour into one expert, which is then shut off (gradient-routing-like). SAE backdoor forensics, arXiv 2609.07746: steering and ablating top features failed on language-switch backdoors. An ICLR 2026 backdoor paper reports the trigger signal is distributed and redundant in 7-13B models. Gradient Routing (Cloud et al., arXiv 2410.04332): localize-then-ablate; no EM application was found. Selective-steering and probe-gated papers (arXiv 2604.08169, 2606.29441): gating on a probe works for malicious system prompts but has a blind spot for crafted activations; neither tests EM.

## 2. The gap

No one found who does "inoculate, extract a cue direction by (cue on minus off) in the inoculated model minus the same in the neutral-trained model, then use it as a probe and conditionally ablate or subtract it at inference." Patel is the nearest. It measures distance along a generic EM direction and ablates that direction, but it does not use the cue contrast or the neutral-arm double difference, does no conditional gating, and reports no capability-cost comparison. Nothing tests the hypothesis that inoculation concentrates the trait into a lower-dimensional, easier-to-ablate handle than the neutral-trained arm. That comparison, ablation efficacy and capability cost on the inoculated arm vs the neutral arm, is the open piece. Caveat: Patel's ablation on the 3 non-IP arms vs the IP arm is a partial precedent, and searches are not exhaustive for papers posted in the last few weeks.

## 3. Sub-question: a cue for misbehaviour first seen at inference

Nothing directly addresses it. Nearest items: (a) Dubiński's post-hoc benign fine-tuning shows the converse, a trait pushed behind cues by later training, without an engineered cue. (b) Sealing/BCT starts from an already inoculated model; it is not used on a naturally unconditional trait. (c) The "controlled backdoor injection then removal" line (Backdoor Unlearning Generalization, arXiv 2606.03785, snippet only) suggests transfer between backdoors. (d) Midtraining neologisms need to be there before the post-training. (e) Persona-vector extraction from contrastive transcripts (Chen et al.) needs no cue and is the baseline to beat. No evidence found that post-hoc inoculation fine-tuning relocates an existing unconditional trait behind a cue. Evidence from Dubiński and Nadaf (post-hoc edits leave the disposition) says cleanup is hard, so expect a negative or partial result.
