## 2. Established vs open, per sub-question

### (a) Matched per-step rates: misalignment vs re-alignment
Established:
- Repair is fast in absolute steps: 35 steps/120 samples (#1), about 30-40 samples (#2), 107 steps (#12), 800 pairs (#4), 105 steps (#20).
- Inducing EM generally takes hundreds of steps (857 in #12; about 700 per phase in #2's Figure 2; 6,000 insecure-code rows in #1).
- Asymmetry is argued but not measured in matched units: #4 says narrow misalignment is "easier to erode than instil", #2 reports lower gradient norms in safe phases and (before length-matching) near-instant realignment; #5 predicts the opposite direction of difficulty (alignment is elastic), and #15 reports one-shot safety patching.
- A confound is now documented: the near-instant realignment in #2 was largely a response-length artifact; after length-matching, models were again susceptible to repeated cycles. #2 never states a per-step comparison after length-matching (its Figure 7 is qualitative: "a trend of re-alignment soon after data normalization"). #11 adds that repair tracks restored capability, and #12 shows generic continuation alone removes 13.6 pp.

Open:
- No paper I found compares induction vs repair per optimizer step with the same data size, same LR, same LoRA, matched response length, and a neutral-data control, across more than one model. #2 is the nearest and it is one model (Qwen2.5-14B), one domain pair (financial; medical mentioned), gpt-4o-mini judge, no seeds reported, and a binary threshold metric (alignment below 30 and coherence above 50).
- The metric is itself a problem: #2 itself notes thresholded binary judge metrics produce "emergence"-like shapes (Schaeffer et al. argument), so rates should be continuous (judge score, log-prob) as well as thresholded.

### (b) Does narrow good data push the model past baseline alignment ("emergent alignment")?
Established (weakly):
- Concept named by #9 (hypothesis, Apr 2025).
- Small positive results: #8 (+4.8 points, 1B, one seed, no control), #7 (versus a helpful-only baseline, so it measures restoring refusals, not going beyond an aligned model), #18 (a different mechanism: spec-conditioned generalization, from a pretrained base), #17 (large-scale broad data).
- Negative or cautionary: #20 Selective Generalization (mixed HHH does not raise general alignment unless upweighted), #11 (benign data reverses EM only to the extent it restores capability; generic data does as well), #12 (generic continuation reduces EM 13.6 pp), #1/#10/#4 (restoration returns to roughly base, not above). #10 explicitly reports "original levels" not above.
Open:
- On an already-aligned instruction-tuned model, does narrow good data (correct medical advice) improve alignment on held-out domains beyond the unmodified model, against a matched neutral control? I found no study with that design. The ceiling problem is real: instruct models start at near 0% misaligned on Betley questions, so the metric must be a harder or more graded one.

### (c) Durability of repair
Established:
- Hidden-not-removed evidence: #3 (post-hoc HHH fine-tuning and dilution leave EM triggerable by training-context features; GPT-4o/4.1 insecure-code setting), supported by sleeper-agents persistence (#16, SNIPPET) and the relearning literature (#16).
- Re-misalignment is possible after repair (#2, length-matched).
- Further fine-tuning mostly erodes alignment (#5 elasticity prediction, #14).
- Holding at 0 for an extra 45 steps (#20) and 5 epochs of benign continuation in a VLM setting (Narrow Multimodal FT, arXiv 2609.35291, FETCH-SUMMARY: "EM returns to near the base level and remains there") are short windows.
- #12 says outright "whether the repair lasts under further training is untested."
Open:
- Whether a repair survives further benign fine-tuning (the question as asked) on a small, non-insecure-code, non-GPT organism like Jacob's rank-1 medical adapter, using a trigger-free eval AND a context-matched eval. #3 tested only GPT-4o/4.1 with insecure code and trigger-style probes; #2 tested only immediate re-poisoning with the same data, not a "small bad nudge" dose-response or benign continuation, and it did not report how many bad steps are needed after repair compared with a never-repaired model (hysteresis never quantified).
- Whether the weights moved or the behaviour is gated: no paper compares the repaired adapter's direction against the original EM direction (Soligo's convergent direction would make this cheap).

## 3. The gap and the strongest prior result against

Gap that remains (all three together): a single-organism, matched-protocol study of (i) induction vs repair per step with length-matched data and a neutral control, (ii) whether good data moves the model above an aligned baseline on held-out domains, and (iii) re-emergence after repair under benign continuation and a small bad nudge, with a context-matched probe and a direction-level check. #2 comes closest to (i) and (iii) but is one run configuration, undermined by its own artifact discovery, and has no neutral control or dose-response. #3 comes closest to (iii) but on GPT-4o/4.1 insecure code with no per-step matching. No one has (ii) done carefully.

Strongest prior results against each hypothesis the idea might hold:
- Against "repair is fast and symmetric / good data generalizes as readily as bad": #2's finding that fast realignment was largely a length artifact, and #11's finding that benign data (including correct advice or MMLU) reverses EM only by restoring degraded capability, so the "alignment generalizes strongly" reading of #1 may be partly capability recovery, not a good-data persona effect. Jacob's own generic-continuation arm (13.6 pp) is the same warning.
- Against "good data pushes beyond baseline": #10, #4 and #1 all land at "roughly base", and #20 reports no general alignment gain from small mixed HHH data. The only positive (#8) is a single-seed 1B result with a 5-point gain and no neutral control.
- Against "repair is durable": #3 (HHH fine-tuning at 10,000 samples still leaves 0.41% with a trigger) plus relearning and sleeper-agent persistence. A counter-risk to the opposite claim: #5 predicts misalignment returns easily, but #15 and #2 (pre-length-matching) show repairs that held, so either result is plausible a priori.
