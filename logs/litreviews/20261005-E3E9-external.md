## Task 1 — Prior/concurrent work for Proposal A (early-pretraining-checkpoint susceptibility window)

**Closest work found:**
- **Overtrained, Not Misaligned** (Schreiber et al., arXiv 2605.12199, saved to scratchpad) — checkpoint-level analysis during *fine-tuning* (not pretraining) across 12 open models (8B–671B) + GPT-4o. Finds EM emerges late relative to task convergence; early stopping avoids EM in 71% of cases while keeping 93% of task performance. Consistent EM only above ~200B params. **Different axis from Proposal A**: this sweeps fine-tuning steps on a fixed pretrained checkpoint, not pretraining checkpoints. No overlap with the "narrow-task-learned-but-not-generalized" window A is asking about at the pretraining level.
- **Tracing Persona Vectors Through LLM Pretraining** (Moskvoretskii et al., already held, 2605.13329) — confirms directly relevant background: persona vectors on OLMo-3 form within 0.22% of pretraining and remain steerable through post-training, though they keep refining geometrically/semantically. Supports the premise that early checkpoints already carry EM-relevant structure, consistent with your E1b result that even stage1-step1413814-equivalent-early checkpoints show EM.
- **em-olmo** (nick-rui, already held) — the directly concurrent project. Confirmed via HF README fetch: it extracts general/narrow misalignment directions and attributes them to Dolma 3 documents, and steers stage-1 checkpoints with those directions (not LoRA fine-tuning per checkpoint, as A proposes). Early checkpoints (below ~s1-step100k) are described as producing incoherent steered output; similarity curves flatten after the first ~1,000 steps. No one in em-olmo actually LoRA-fine-tunes very-early (1k–10k step) checkpoints the way Proposal A does — their approach is steering-vector sweep + CPT addition, not per-checkpoint fine-tune-and-measure-generalization. **This is the real gap**: nobody has fine-tuned on bad-vs-good medical advice at the 1k–10k step window specifically to ask whether narrow learning precedes broad generalization.
- **When Should We Introduce Safety Interventions During Pretraining?** (arXiv 2601.07087, saved) — tests removing/rephrasing harmful content starting at 0%/20%/60% of pretraining. Finds delayed intervention (after 20–60%) gives strongest robustness under standard decoding, but earlier intervention better separates safety/harm representations linearly. Not EM-specific, and doesn't fine-tune checkpoints afterward to test susceptibility — but it's the closest "timing matters" precedent and worth citing for the general claim that very early vs. mid-pretraining windows behave differently.
- Did not find any paper computing a "capability learned but misalignment not yet generalized" window specifically via post-hoc fine-tuning of very-early checkpoints (steps 1k–10k) on any model family (OLMo, Pythia, Amber). This appears to be genuinely open.

  All four conditions show EM after fine-tuning; no condition consistently wins. This is the exact source for the "App. I" claim in your proposal text — now independently confirmed.

## Task 2 — Prior/concurrent work for Proposal B (rewriting pretraining data to reduce EM)

**Nobody has rewritten targeted pretraining documents to reduce EM specifically.** Closest adjacent work:
- **Your own "Correct, Don't Delete"** is now public on arXiv as **2609.37624** (confirmed via fetch, author Jacob Epifano) — but this corrects/deletes rows in the *fine-tuning* data (Qwen2.5-14B on poisoned medical advice), not pretraining data. Correcting cuts EM by ~1/3 at 25% correction; deletion has little effect; paraphrasing-while-keeping-bad-advice shows no clear benefit. This is strong internal precedent that "correction > deletion > paraphrase-without-content-change" at the fine-tuning level — Proposal B is the natural pretraining-level analogue, and the paraphrase-no-benefit finding is a direct motivator for B's targeted-rewrite vs. random-rewrite split.
- **Safety Pretraining: Toward the Next Generation of Safe AI** (Maini et al., arXiv 2504.16980, saved) — rephrases unsafe pretraining text into safe context-rich alternatives (not EM; general harmfulness/ASR reduction, 38.8%→8.4%). Closest methodological precedent for "rewrite, don't delete" at pretraining scale, but targets safety benchmarks, not EM susceptibility, and doesn't use data attribution to select which documents to rewrite.
- **Deep Ignorance** (O'Brien et al., arXiv 2508.06601, saved) — filters (deletes) biothreat-related pretraining data in 6.9B models from scratch; tamper-resistant to adversarial fine-tuning. Filtering/deletion, not rewriting; not EM.
- **em-olmo** (concurrent) — already does the data-attribution half of B (attributing EM/narrow directions to Dolma 3 documents) but only *adds* top-ranked documents via CPT (10M/100M tokens), confirmed flat susceptibility — it does not rewrite or delete the attributed documents. This matches what you already knew; confirms the gap is specifically in the rewrite/delete-and-replace intervention, not the attribution step.
- **Tice et al. Appendix I** (above) — directly motivates B: alignment-upsampling/filtering at the discourse-document level does not mitigate EM susceptibility, but that paper upsamples/filters by *topic* (AI-safety discourse), not by document-level attribution to the specific EM/narrow misalignment directions, and never rewrites.
- **When Should We Introduce Safety Interventions During Pretraining** (2601.07087) — closest in using rephrasing of harmful content at controlled pretraining stages, but general safety, not EM, and not attribution-targeted.

**Gap for B remains real**: no paper combines (1) data attribution to find documents driving EM/narrow misalignment directions specifically, with (2) rewriting (vs. deleting vs. randomly rewriting) those specific documents, then (3) running the EM fine-tune recipe to compare susceptibility. em-olmo's CPT-addition result (flat) is suggestive but tests a different intervention (addition, not rewrite/replace of attributed documents).
