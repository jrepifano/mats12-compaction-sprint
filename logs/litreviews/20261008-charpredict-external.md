# External literature review: "predict character effects" (can we predict, before training, how a narrow fine-tuning dataset shifts a model's broad character, and does framing of identical data change it?), 2026-10-08

Subagent (Sonnet) report. Web only, no paid calls, no PDFs added to the repo (PDFs were downloaded to the scratchpad and read as text with `pdftotext`).

## 0. Method and read-depth key
Searches: about 14 WebSearch queries (standard and extended) over arXiv, LessWrong/AF, Goodfire, Anthropic, ICLR/ICML listings. Read-depth labels used below:
- **TEXT-PART**: full PDF converted to text locally (verbatim), and I read the sections named; not the whole paper.
- **TEXT-ABS**: PDF text, abstract (and sometimes intro) only.
- **FETCH**: WebFetch summary from a small model; not verbatim.
- **SNIP**: search-result snippet or aggregator summary only. Treat as unverified.

WebFetch could not parse PDFs, so every TEXT label came from `pdftotext` on the downloaded file, not from the fetch tool.

## 2. Established vs open

**Established (with the read depth above)**
1. Dataset-level projection onto a persona direction, corrected for the base model's own responses, predicts post-fine-tune trait shift on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (r 0.41-0.95 depending on trait; #2).
2. A data-side personality or persona profile of misaligned datasets matches the profile of the model fine-tuned on it (Big Five, r = 0.83 over 8 categories, 2 models; #3).
3. An LLM that reads a dataset and rates how broadly it pushes toward misbehavior forecasts (model, dataset, failure mode) triples at AUROC about 0.80 on MCQ probes, and zero-shot LLM forecasting is near chance (#1).
4. Framing of identical tokens changes EM (Betley educational control, inoculation prompting #9, #10) and a first-step gradient signal already shows the direction of the effect on one pair (#4), though the judged effect was not reproduced in #4.
5. Seemingly benign narrow data does shift broad behavior (#2 flawed-math raises evil; #15 ideology and sycophancy; #8 safety).
6. Framing effects can be conditional triggers rather than removal (#11), and inoculation-style prompts can re-elicit misalignment.

**Open (not found in my search)**
- A single study that fits one pool of narrow datasets spanning harmful, quirky-benign and good, with continuous broad-character outcomes (EM battery plus trait probes), and compares an LLM-reading predictor, a persona-vector projection and a steering-direction cosine head-to-head under leave-one-dataset-out. #1 has the LLM predictor with a binary MCQ outcome and no interp predictors; #2 and #3 have interp predictors with few explicit trait datasets and no LLM predictor or held-out protocol that I verified.
- Whether any pre-training predictor forecasts the **framing effect for the same data** (a pair-level, within-dataset contrast). Only #4 (gradient, one pair) touches it; #10 offers the "pre-fine-tune elicitation strength" heuristic but for choosing prompts, not forecasting effect size.
- Quirky-benign style datasets (sycophantic, overconfident, terse) as a systematic family: only #2 (sycophancy as a trait dataset), #15 (ideology, GPT-4.1) and scattered mentions. I found no systematic family with a predictor.
- Whether the data-side predictors transfer across small LoRA fine-tunes at the roughly 1-5% judged-EM rate typical of Qwen2.5-7B (#4 warns that judged ordering can vanish at this scale).

## 3. The gap, and the strongest prior result against the idea

**Novelty verdict: partly done; the combination and the framing contrast are open, the three predictors individually are not.**
- "Predict EM or trait shift from the data before training" is already a published task with an LLM-reader predictor (#1) and a persona-vector projection predictor (#2), plus a Big Five profile (#3). A paper that only reports "projection predicts shift on Qwen2.5-7B" repeats #2.
- What remains open: (a) one leave-one-dataset-out comparison of LLM-reader vs ΔP vs steering-cosine on continuous outcomes, with good and quirky-benign data in the pool; (b) predicting the within-dataset framing contrast (inoculation-style "you were asked") from the same data-side signals; (c) the quirky-benign family. Item (b) is the most novel and the riskiest.
- Same model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) as #2 and #3, so results are directly comparable to those two. Cheap, and Jacob's repo already has the 56-question EM set.

**Strongest evidence against the idea (what could make it a null or a repeat)**
1. **Few points.** ~12 datasets (24 with framing) gives a leave-one-out correlation with very wide intervals. #2's own r for hallucination was 0.408 with p = 0.048 on its dataset set, and the high r values are driven by trait-explicit datasets that are far from the baseline. A predictor that merely separates "explicitly bad" from "not" will look good and be uninformative. Report within-class rank correlation and bootstrap CIs over datasets.
2. **The framing effect may not show up in judged EM at LoRA scale.** #4 found insecure and educational arms indistinguishable (even reversed) on judged generations at 14B. Betley's original educational control was large on GPT-4o / Qwen-32B full-ish fine-tunes. Budget for a framing manipulation check (the 24-run design needs a framed dataset where the effect is known to appear, for example insecure vs educational code or a Wichers/Tan inoculation prompt on a dataset where the unframed EM is large) before reading any predictor.
3. **Conditional misalignment** (#11): a framing "improvement" on the standard battery may hide a trigger. Add train-context-resembling eval prompts for the framed arms, otherwise the framing result can be misread.
4. **The LLM-reader baseline is strong and already beats naive baselines** (#1) and is cheap; persona-vector and steering predictors need to beat it, not just chance. Prior literature gives no reason to expect they will on a mixed pool (#6 found the black-box harmfulness score as good as gradients within EM data).
5. **Content-based predictors can miss response form** (#14), and projection of data on a persona vector measures style as much as persona (#4 builds a matched style control for this reason). A style-matched control for ΔP is cheap and worth including.
6. Scale of effect: only some datasets move 56-question EM at all on Qwen2.5-7B LoRA. Pick trait probes with continuous scores (persona-vectors-style trait scoring) so the outcome is not mostly zeros.
