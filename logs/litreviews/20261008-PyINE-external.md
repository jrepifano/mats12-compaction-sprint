## 2. Is PyINE's setup reusable?
Sources: GitHub README (fetch summary) https://github.com/lawzero-org/pyine (mirror https://github.com/saifh-github/pyine seen in search results); HF pages (cards via fetch summary); PyINE paper (VERBATIM-LOCAL for compute and training recipe).
- **Licenses:** framework Apache-2.0; PyINE-v1 data and results CC-BY-4.0; repackaged TACO follows TACO's own license; paper states upstream licenses preserved. Datasets carry a benchmark-contamination canary ("should never appear in training corpora") -- note this clashes with our using them for RL training of anything we later evaluate; their own organism was trained on them, so they presumably mean the held-out evaluation splits. Check before use.
- **Released artifacts:** HF user (a personal profile, not an org) `plstcharles-saifh`: model `pyine-v1-qwen3-4b-shortcut` (Apache-2.0, base Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, "global step 600", final checkpoint only, no intermediate RL checkpoints mentioned; the card lists "196k params", which looks like a metadata glitch, and the paper says full-model training, so verify file size before relying on it); datasets `pyine-v1-traces` (100k-1M rows, CC-BY-4.0, execution traces) and `pyine-v1-augments` (1,091,003 rows, 5.91 GB; train 869k / valid 115k / test 108k; gpt-5-mini generated; prompt types `hints/docs` 393,845 helpful hints, `validation/misleading` 166,749, `issues/docs` 166,750, `code_stubbing` 255,910, `code_summary` 103,860). Canonical copies of the full data, TACO source and evaluation results are on Google Drive only. Hydra configs and accelerate launcher YAMLs are in `pyine/configs/`; README has a reproduction recipe but states no GPU counts or hours (fetch summary).
- Date oddity: HF listing shows model "updated Apr 30" and datasets "May 4", months before the 3 Oct 2026 paper. Likely uploaded earlier and made public with the paper; not verified.
- **Compute to reproduce the organism (my inference from the paper's stated numbers):** full-model GRPO-style RL with vLLM colocated, effective batch 960 on 40 H100 (5 nodes, InfiniBand), sequences up to ~10k tokens, 50+ hyperparameter/recipe runs. App. F.2: the first 50 steps take roughly 2 h on the 40xH100 setup, and the shortcut-following behaviour emerges "beyond step 300" under the length penalty; the released checkpoint is step 600. So 600 steps is on the order of 24 h x 40 GPUs, about 1,000 H100-hours, roughly $2.7-3.5k at our measured $2.69-3.49/h, for ONE run and before sweeps. This does not fit a $2,100 program, so reproducing the organism from scratch is out; scaled-down replication (LoRA, smaller batch, shorter contexts) is untested and the paper reports LoRA recipes gave "mitigated success".
- **What this means for each framing:**
  - B (reader test) can use the released step-600 checkpoint plus released data directly: forward passes on a 4B model, cheap. Misleading-slice labels come free from execution. Need the repo's task prompts/variants to regenerate the exact eval slices (README reading needed; not done).
  - A (attribution during RL) needs RL runs: either a heavy rerun of the 40-GPU recipe (infeasible), or a scaled-down rerun that must first be shown to reproduce the shortcut, or an offline variant: take the released step-600 checkpoint (or its base) and run attribution/filtering on a short RL continuation. The absence of intermediate checkpoints means we cannot attribute the original shortcut formation retrospectively. PyINE's own account (paper, VERBATIM-LOCAL F.2) is that the early gains rely on code execution logic and the shortcut then emerges under the length penalty while mean response length falls; the 5% helpful-hint share of training data (plus 5% obfuscated+hint) is the likely teaching signal. That is the paper's description of the mix, not a causal attribution; no ablation of the hint share was found.

## 3. The gap that remains, and the strongest prior result against each

### Framing A (attribution/filtering to stop the hint-trust shortcut during RL)
- **Gap:** I found no work that (i) targets a shortcut learned from correctly rewarded, non-hacking rollouts (so no reward-hacking monitor flags them), (ii) measures a held-out counterfactual slice (misleading-variant accuracy) as the outcome, or (iii) compares text, LLM-judge and probe filters with gradient/attribution filters at a matched drop rate for it. RL-time filtering precedents (Wong et al., I-PPO, Grift offline, ProFIL) all target hacking or general efficiency. RLVR shortcut work (Spurious Rewards Paradox) is model-level mechanistic, not rollout-level.
- **Strongest prior result against A:**
  1. Cost. The only known recipe to produce the organism is about 1,000 H100-hours per run; a filter experiment needs several arms and seeds (Wong stresses seed variance). Unless the shortcut can be induced cheaply at small scale, A is not affordable. Nothing I found shows a small-scale (LoRA, short run) reproduction.
  2. Simple baselines may already work. The shortcut is probably lexically visible at the sample level (helpful hints in the prompt plus short answers), so a prompt-feature filter (drop rollouts that carry a hint) would be a trivial strong baseline, and Dutta's result says such baselines often tie fancy detectors. If helpful-hint rollouts are "the cause", a data-mix change (reduce the 5%+5% hint share) answers the question without any attribution, and PyINE's recipe already has that knob.
  3. Filtering on correct-but-shortcut rollouts removes samples that are rewarded and correct, so any drop may lower original-task accuracy; PyINE already reports a utility vs brevity trade-off (App. F.2), so the length penalty, not hints, may be the real lever.
  4. Baker et al.: optimizing against a monitor can induce evasion; relevant if the filter is learned.

### Framing B (do activation readers detect unverbalized cue reliance where correctness probes and cheap judges fail?)
- **Gap:** PyINE only trained probes on correctness labels. Prior positive probe results (Mirtaheri & Belkin; Angdembay et al.; Goodfire) use behaviour-specific labels (hint use, hacking) or other models, and none is evaluated on an RL-trained code-execution organism with a counterfactual ground truth. I found no source applying SAEs, J-Lens, Oracle Lens, activation oracles or NLAs to hint/docstring reliance. NLA paper shows reader success on unverbalized eval awareness only. So "a probe trained on the right label (relies on the docstring) vs the correctness probe, on the PyINE organism" is open, as is every non-probe reader.
- **Strongest prior result against B:**
  1. PyINE's own appendix: a strong code-tracing LLM judge reaches about 93 AUROC on the misleading slice, since the judge can re-execute the code. Any reader has to beat that or be justified on cost; and the task is one where the ground truth is cheaply computable, which makes the whole oversight problem less compelling as a proxy (my inference).
  2. Mirtaheri & Belkin-type probes need counterfactual labels (answer with vs without the hint), which exist here only when the organism is run on both variants; a "relies on docstring" probe is therefore a supervised detector for the exact failure, and a text baseline on the same label (is the docstring contradicting the code?) is nearly free. Beating it is the real bar.
  3. Reader brittleness: oracles trained on a model carrying the behaviour under-detect it (Fornasiere et al.); NLA reconstruction can be passed without faithfulness and explanations confabulate; NLA/AO weights for Qwen3-4B are not available (NLA is trained on Claude), so these readers have a real build cost.
  4. Probe collapse under shift is a documented general phenomenon (arXiv 2604.11662), so the PyINE negative is the expected result for correctness probes and says little about label-matched probes.
