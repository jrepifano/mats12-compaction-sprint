## (a) Closest related work in `papers/`
- **Persona Vectors** (row 6; Chen et al.; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, §3, the same two models as E10). Recipe: mean activation of trait-exhibiting minus non-exhibiting responses, from system-prompted contrast. Traits: evil, sycophancy, hallucination, plus others (optimism, humor). Section 4.1: training on one trait data set "can inadvertently amplify other traits"; EM-like data (flawed math reasoning) raises evil. Bearing: this is the template for E10's trait-profile readout, and shows trait vectors are not independent (evil and sycophancy co-move), which weakens "closer to misaligned than to the 10 controls" as a clean contrast.
- **Persona Features Control EM** (row 5; OpenAI, GPT-4o SAE). Fig. 1 and §3.2: the ten strongest steering latents are #10 "toxic persona" and several **sarcasm/satire latents** (#89 "sarcastic advice", #31, #55, #340). Bearing: **EM already has a published link to a sarcastic persona**, and "sarcastic" is one of Open Character's 11. So the 10 controls are not neutral. The prediction that EM is closer to "misaligned" than to "sarcastic" is not obvious, and sarcastic and sycophantic could beat "misaligned". Pre-register which controls are expected close.
- **Convergent Linear Representations of EM** (row 3; Soligo et al.). §3.1: the "mean-diff" direction is computed **within the EM model**, mean over answer tokens of misaligned-scored (<30) minus aligned-scored (>70) responses, Qwen-14B, layer 24. It is not EM-model minus base. Cosines above 0.8 between directions from three different EM fine-tunes (App. C.2, all but 4 layers); steering reaches up to 50% EM versus about 11-17% for the fine-tune. §4: rank-1 LoRA B-vector vs mean-diff cosine only 0.04 despite similar ablation effects. Bearing: (1) our "EM minus base" direction is a different object from the published one, so E10 should compute both; (2) a low cosine between two directions that have matched causal effect is already a published warning that cosine is a weak identity test.
- **Nadaf** (row 9; Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, single author). Persona subspace extracted from the frozen model by contrastive teacher forcing; 4 domains share a core at 657x a random null, 82% outside a style core; overlaps the Assistant direction at mean cosine 0.565 over three layers (§4); projecting it out of training takes EM 27.7% to 0.0%. Bearing: gives a method (subspace overlap, not a single cosine) and a null-model template; the 0.565 shows persona directions overlap heavily with the Assistant Axis, so E10 must also report cosine against it.
- **Drake & Eberstadt** (row 43; Qwen2.5 7B/14B/32B). §5: on Qwen2.5-7B and 14B, broad EM "stays flat and low across rank (at or below about 2%)" for their covert-inducer insecure-code recipe; low-rank recruitment is a 32B phenomenon. Behaviour-level persona direction, cross-model transplant as a causal assay. Bearing: **EM organisms at 7B can be weak depending on recipe**. Use Turner's bad-medical organisms (Model Organisms, row 2, reports EM on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen families) and verify EM rate first.
- **Assistant Axis** (row 21; Gemma 2 27B, Qwen3 32B, Llama 3.3 70B). §2.1: 275 role vectors from system-prompted responses, PCA; PC1 role loadings correlate >0.92 across models. Not run on the 7-8B models or on character-trained models. Bearing: the reference frame for "where does each persona sit"; character-trained checkpoints could be projected onto it for free.
- **Story Imprinting** (row 18), **SPP** (row 16, Minder et al., 3B pretraining), **Persona Selection Model** (row 14), **Teaching Claude Why** and **Model Spec Midtraining** (rows 50-51): all concern installing or shifting characters by training data. None compares an installed character with the EM direction (grep and abstract only).
- **Minder et al., Narrow Finetuning Leaves Readable Traces** (row 52): see (d).
- **Not found anywhere in `papers/`:** any comparison of EM against a deliberately installed character. The lit review (`_litreview_text.txt`) has no character-training entry.

## (d) Risks the sources raise
1. **Domain bias dominates narrow-fine-tune differences** (Minder, §4 and §7). Their conclusion: "narrow finetuning signals completely overpower any traces from standard chat finetuning", and the bias is "artificially strong compared to realistic post-training". They also attribute it to overfitting to a constant semantic concept across all samples (§5). Consequences for E10: (i) the EM-minus-base mean difference will contain a **medical-advice bias**, absent from character models; (ii) character models, trained on diverse constitution-driven data, should have weaker, more distributed differences. A low cosine would then reflect data narrowness, not persona difference. Mitigations: subtract the good-medical-advice control from the EM direction (as E2's draft proposes); read directions on out-of-domain text; also compute the Soligo within-model aligned-vs-misaligned direction; report the cosine of each character direction with a random-persona and a domain-bias null.
2. **Direction cosine is a weak identity test**: Soligo §4 found cosine 0.04 between two directions with similar ablation effects; Nadaf's subspace method and causal tests (cross-steering) are the stronger evidence. Pre-register causal tests as primary and cosine as supporting.
3. **Controls are not independent of "misaligned"**: Persona Features (sarcasm latents among top EM latents) and Persona Vectors §4.1 (traits co-amplify) imply sarcastic, sycophantic, impulsive may sit near EM too. Need a pre-registered ordering or a ranking test rather than "closest wins".
4. **Training recipes differ** (character: constitution, DPO, introspective SFT, LoRA details unread; EM: LoRA on narrow data). Direction differences may track training method (Drake §5 shows method-conditional EM).
5. **Model availability and strength**: EM at 7B is recipe-dependent (Drake); E1b's steering test failed on coherence. Gemma 3 4B has no EM organism cited here.
6. **Unread primary source**: the character-training paper and its checkpoint list are not captured; claims about its recipe come only from the brief.

## (e) Assessment of the remaining gap
- Nothing in `papers/` or our own reports compares the EM persona to a deliberately installed misaligned character. The pieces exist separately (trait vectors, EM directions, Assistant Axis), so the question is open, subject to the external review confirming no concurrent paper.
- The experiment is cheap and the answer is informative either way, but the design needs three changes before a prereg: (a) a domain-bias control (good-advice subtraction, off-domain reading); (b) causal cross-steering as the headline, not cosine; (c) a priori predictions for the control personas (sarcastic, sycophantic, impulsive), since a sarcastic-nearest result is already plausible from OpenAI's latents.
