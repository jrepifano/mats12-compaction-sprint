# External literature review: gradient attribution to activation steering (E15), 2026-10-09

Subagent (Sonnet) report. Question: has anyone closed the loop attribute training examples (per-example gradients / influence) -> map to activation-space directions -> steer -> compare with weight-space steering / the gated rank-1 LoRA edit -> validate by retraining? Testbed: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct rank-1 EM adapters and bad-medical-advice.

## 0. Method and read-depth key
Web search (standard and extended) plus WebFetch. No PDF read in full. WebFetch returns a small model's summary, never verbatim text; the search tool returns a model-written synthesis, so its numbers are unverified. Depth labels:
- **summary-abs**: arXiv abstract page via fetch summary.
- **summary-html**: arXiv HTML or GitHub page via fetch summary (long pages truncated at ~100k characters; noted).
- **snippet**: search-result synthesis only.
Nothing was read verbatim. Not found at all: the paper behind the `lindaCai1997/data-attribution` repo (ATLAS), the Jaburi et al. workshop paper itself, any Paulo et al. 2026 EleutherAI OLMo-3 influence-filtering paper (searched by name; only Bergson's secondhand description surfaced).

## 2. Established vs open, per question

**Q1. The full loop.**
- Established: pieces exist separately. Attribution toward activation-defined targets (Concept Influence, ATLAS repo, Xiao & Aranguri, STRIDE); weight-update-as-activation-shift theory and fixed-vector conversion (2603.00425, 2610.04283, ICML 2026); gradient atoms steered in weight space (2603.14665); attribution of persona features to data with retraining (2608.11025).
- Open (as far as found): taking an attribution method's *parameter-space* output (per-example LoRA gradient, sparse atom, EKFAC-weighted component) and converting it into a residual-stream direction via Δh = ΔW·x, then steering and comparing with the weight edit. ATLAS is the nearest and its paper was not located; read it first.
- Searches run: gradient to activation direction, weight vs activation steering, LoRA B vector steering, influence functions steering vectors, data attribution SAE feature, ELROND, GradientSpace, Gradient Atoms follow-ups, ATLAS, Mode-Conditioning (no relevant hit, not pursued further).

**Q2. Rank-1 LoRA as (gate, direction).**
- Established: 2506.11618 gives the single-adapter numbers above (cos(B, mean-diff)=0.04; mean-diff ablation kills EM but B-projection removal barely does; scalars encode misalignment more than medical context; scalar patching weak). ICML 2026 theory says V/O rank-1 = one fixed direction. 2511.06739 reads rank-1 gates as interpretable.
- Open: standalone steering by +/- B at fixed strength on the single rank-1 organism, and a direct comparison with the gated edit; which inputs fire A·x most on the bad-medical dataset (2506.11618 only did probes/patching on the 9-adapter model). Outlier-dimension argument that a rank-1 gate is nearly constant is an informal forum claim only (snippet).

**Q3. Weight vs activation steering.**
- Established: 2511.05408 shows weight steering beats single- or all-layer mean-diff activation steering on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for sycophancy and "evil" MCQ; 2610.04283 shows Jacobian-derived vectors beat other activation steerers on three small models. 2507.08218: LoRA-extracted vectors approximately match LoRAs early-layer, worse otherwise.
- Open: whether gating matters. No paper found that ablates the input-dependent gate against a matched fixed vector, in either direction.

**Q4. Reliability of attribution.**
- Established (weak-to-moderate): IF underperform simple representation baselines on LLM fine-tunes (Li et al., snippet-level); Concept Influence reports cheap activation dot-products match EK-FAC at 20x speed (exact retraining numbers in figures only); TAME reports 23-36x EM reduction by masking attributed tokens on the exact public adapters/dataset; Jaburi et al. report attribution filtering matches WildGuard (secondhand); Xiao & Aranguri report LESS better at small budgets (briefed); Vetter et al. find attributed human text fails to induce EM.
- Open: LDS-style counterfactual scores for LoRA fine-tunes of 7B models on EM data; head-to-head of TRAK/LESS/DataInf/Kronfluence under retraining. Not found.

**Q5. Tooling.** Kronfluence (EK-FAC; nn.Linear/Conv2d only per docs snippet, LoRA support unconfirmed); Bergson (explicit PEFT adapter-only gradients, EK-FAC and TrackStar, on-disk store; EK-FAC verified to 14B on 8xA100); LogIX/LoGra (Llama3-8B; PEFT support not checked); curvlinops EK-FAC (used by Concept Influence); DataInf (LoRA-specific, closed form); Gradient Atoms code on GitHub. No source gave a wall-clock or dollar figure for 7B LoRA per-example gradients; only Concept Influence's 1,000-example timings (~20 min for IF/Concept Influence, ~1 min Vector Filter, hardware unstated) and Bergson's scaling plots.

**Q6. Concurrent equivalent.** None found that does the whole loop with a weight-vs-activation comparison. Closest: ATLAS (unread paper), First-Order Steering (bridge only, 6 days old), Concept Influence (testbed and retraining), TAME (testbed and retraining). Risk of scoop from these is real.

## 3. The gap and the strongest prior result against the idea
Gap: using per-example gradient or atom outputs on a LoRA fine-tune as the *source* of the steering direction through the explicit ΔW·x bridge, plus a controlled fixed-direction vs gated-edit comparison on a single rank-1 organism, plus retraining validation. Nobody found has isolated the gate.

Strongest prior results against it:
1. Concept Influence: a trivial activation dot-product (Vector Filter) already matches EK-FAC influence at ~20x speed, so heavy gradient machinery may add nothing; a decision-relevant baseline.
2. 2506.11618: B has cosine 0.04 with the mean-diff direction and removing mean-diff from B recovers little (17% -> 13%), while ablating the residual-stream direction kills EM: B alone may be a poor steering handle and the effect may live in downstream layers. Scalar patching barely moves EM, so the gate may not be where the behaviour is.
3. 2511.05408: weight steering beats activation steering for reasons attributed to fine-tuning-based collection and weight space, which predicts a fixed B-steer will underperform the weight edit; the interesting question is whether gating, not "weight space", explains that.
4. Li et al. and Vetter et al.: attribution may not predict retraining effects; relevant text may fail to induce EM.
5. TAME, on the same public adapters and dataset, already gets large retraining effects with a simpler (forward-pass) attribution.

## 6. Addendum by the main session (2026-10-09): follow-up reads by fetch agents
Two web-fetch agents reread key sources after this review. Quotes were capped at 125 characters by the tool; read depth is as each agent stated.
- **Gradient Atoms (arXiv 2603.14665v2), HTML read in full.** Single author J Rosser (FLAIR, Oxford), 17 Mar 2026. Gemma-3 4B IT, LoRA rank 8 on q/v at all 34 layers, 5,000 SFT documents. Per-document gradients → EKFAC projection (top-50 eigenvectors per module, 6,800 dims) → unit-normalised → MiniBatchDictionaryLearning (K=500, α=0.1). Steering is in **weight space**, θ ± α·v with α ∈ {0.5,…,10} in both signs. 5 atoms have coherence > 0.5, 43 > 0.1. Steering (regex-scored, 100 questions per atom): bullets 33% → 94% / 0%, refusal 50% → 0%, yes/no 39% → 0%. Baselines: unsteered only; **no competing attribution or steering method**, no random directions, no retraining validation.
- **Xiao & Aranguri (arXiv 2602.11079v3), HTML read largely verbatim (Appendix C as a summary).** Title "Probe-Based Data Attribution…". OLMo 2 7B SFT → DPO, 378k pairs. Score = cosine between a behaviour vector (M1 minus M0 responses, teacher-forced through M0) and each pair's accepted-minus-rejected activation difference; layer 20 chosen by steering effectiveness. Retraining from SFT (harmful %, baseline 7.63): filter 30k → 2.86 (−63%), switch 30k → 1.66 (−78%); LESS 3.10 vs probe 3.30 at filter 12k, and 2.57 vs 6.33 at switch 12k. The authors say gradient and LLM methods win at the smallest budget.
- **TAME (arXiv 2609.16754), read in full.** Masud & Parvez, 15 Sep 2026, EMNLP UncertaiNLP workshop. Forward-pass attribution through public bad-medical EM LoRA organisms (Llama-3.1-8B; a Qwen2.5-7B organism) on the 6,849-row medical split; students are Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B. Top-40% token mask: EM 8.6% → 0.4% (Llama), 4.5% → 0.1% (Qwen); random mask no effect. One seed, one GPT-4o judge, 8 questions × 100; the random mask matches token count, not loss mass.
- **First-Order Steering (arXiv 2610.04283), ~79k of 119k characters verbatim.** Kunda, Kurz, Dominik, Maoz, 3 Oct 2026. The mean Jacobian over UltraChat prompts turns LoRA updates into one fixed vector per behaviour; the input dependence is averaged away by design; no gated variant tested. Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Gemma-4-E2B, Mistral-7B; style behaviours; adherence 46.4 (HeRD) vs 30.3 (best baseline), with small margins outside Qwen.
- **ATLAS:** README only (no paper link found): activation-space attribution vectors (`residual_diff`, `residual_change`) vs a TRAK weight-gradient baseline; selected direction applied as a steering vector; retraining on the top 500. No mapping of weights to activations, no weight-vs-activation steering comparison. Paper still unread.
