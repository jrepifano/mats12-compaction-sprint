## 2. Findings
1. **Dataset-level prediction from a persona vector has precedent, and it is exactly Persona Vectors' ΔP.** What is not established: (a) it was shown for traits (evil, sycophancy, hallucination) with Normal/I/II severity ladders built by the authors from one pipeline, not for a set of heterogeneous real-world narrow tasks; (b) held-out-dataset prediction (a fit that is checked on a dataset it never saw) is not what Fig. 8/17 report; they show correlations across all datasets pooled, including the severity ladder, which gives structure that inflates r. Our leave-one-dataset-out test would be a harder, new test. That is an inference from reading Fig. 8/17 captions, not from the authors' stated protocol. Hallucination r=0.408 shows ΔP weakens for traits with a weak direction.
2. **LLM-reads-the-data forecasting has a direct precedent** (Alignment Forecasting), but I only saw an abstract summary. It forecasts failure-mode tendency from datasets; I do not know whether it targets the *size of the broad character shift* or uses framing, and it cannot be assumed to cover our "who writes this data" prompt. Must be read in full before the proposal claims a gap.
3. **Framing as a manipulated variable has precedent but not as a predictor target.** Inoculation Prompting shows framing changes the outcome (Qwen2.5-7B replication, App. D) and gives a surprise-based mechanism, so "does the predictor see the framing?" is a clean extension. Nobody in `papers/` asks whether predictors (ΔP, LLM readers) *anticipate* the inoculation effect. ΔP as defined is **blind to a system-prompt framing unless the framing is inside the prompt x_i** that the activations are computed on. If it is, the base-model response y' also changes, which makes the framing arm a good stress test. This is an inference.
4. **Pairs with identical data and different framing have a known pitfall:** conditional misalignment (Dubiński). An inoculated model can show low EM on generic questions while the shift hides behind context cues. The eval set therefore needs context-matched questions (our 7 medical questions in the 56-question set partly do this: E1 reports 22-33% EM on medical-context questions vs lower on general ones).
5. **Rival accounts make different predictions, which is useful.** Persona-inference (PSM, inoculation) predicts the framing flips the shift while the loss and tokens are identical. Optimisation (Soligo) predicts shifts track loss reduction and not framing. Template/format (Piggyback, Li et al. data-format, Vetter's response-structure result) predicts format differences dominate. Distance (Li et al.) predicts prompt-to-centroid distance. A small 12 x 2 design can separate these only if the framing arm leaves the format fixed, and datasets differ in format. Worth stating in the proposal.
6. **Power is a real concern.** In E1/E1b the EM rate was nearly flat across conditions, and EM rate has large question-level CIs (memory note: the 8-question set alone gave huge CIs; default is the 56-question set). With one seed per fine-tune and ~24 points, a leave-one-out correlation CI will be wide (see §3 for our measured seed-level noise).
7. **Our own datasets only cover the harmful end.** `data/raw` has bad/good medical (7,049 each), risky financial (6,000), and School of Reward Hacks (srh_hack/control, 973 each). Persona Vectors also built "mild" (I) and "overt" (II) versions, which our files lack. The quirky-benign sets in the brief (sycophantic, overconfident, terse, deceptive-in-a-game) would all be new generation (cheap via an LLM, but each needs validation and a matched control).

## 3. Our prior results that transfer
- **Persona direction on the poison rows was weak at row level.** Working repo `paper/sections/appendix.tex` (read): a "reckless advisor" direction built with the Persona Vectors method at layer 24 on Qwen2.5-14B steered the model (+12.8 judged reckless score added to base, p=0.003; -9.2 subtracted from the poisoned adapter, p=0.034), but **projection onto it separated poison from benign rows only weakly (AUC 0.615)**. This is a row-level AUC, so it does not refute dataset-level ΔP, but it is a warning that the vector may carry little signal about what is in a *mixed* dataset. The projection difference (which subtracts the base response) was not tested there; I could not find ΔP in the code map.
- **Rewriting beats deleting** (working repo paper `results.tex`, read): rewriting bad-medical rows with corrective supervision cuts EM 28.5% -> 19.4% (Correct, Don't Delete, em_personas 46), and replicates on Gemma-4-12B and, with a different magnitude, on risky finance. This is a *content* manipulation of identical prompts. Relevant as a second, data-side lever to compare against framing.
- **Judge on content beat influence scores** (~$2 content judge beat random by 3.6-4.3 points; influence-picked rewrites tied random; `phase1-report.md` ~4810-4815, via E7/X7 reviews, not reopened). Prior for the LLM-reads-the-data predictor: a cheap reader of the content is a strong baseline against gradient-style predictors. Same warning as in the X7 review about held-out failure: the locator that reached rho 0.867 on 10 development groups scored -0.047 on 20 held-out groups. **A leave-one-dataset-out design is exactly the protection against that failure**, but with 12 datasets the folds are few; freeze all hyperparameters (layer, response truncation, prompt wording) before any fine-tune result exists.
- **Seed noise.** Retrain target ICC 0.578, seed-to-seed rank correlation 0.10 in the old TDA work (X7 review). Different setting, but it says EM-rate-after-fine-tune can be noisy across seeds. E1b used seed 2 (E1 seed 1), so a two-seed comparison on the same data exists for OLMo.
- **EM rates were flat across conditions in E1/E1b** (17-26% on 8 standard questions at all OLMo stages; 35-39% on 41 further questions; proposals line ~50). On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct the dynamic range of the *dependent variable* across datasets is what matters, and harmful datasets saturating near the same rate would leave a ceiling. Persona Vectors reports varied trait scores across datasets (Fig. 5), which suggests range exists when benign-quirky data are included.
- **The measurement stack passed validation on OLMo.** gpt-6-luna passed Jacob's 120 blind labels at every E1 stage; the second judge Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite was more conservative; E1b needed Gemini for the 41 breadth questions and base-model breadth was undecidable. A new model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) with new trait probes means **re-validating every judge on >=100 hand labels** (CLAUDE.md rule), through the labeling-page protocol.
- **Reward-hack data effects exist in our files:** the SRH organism (973 rows hack/control) in the working repo appendix `app:srh`, usable as one "quirky-misaligned" dataset with a built-in matched control.

## 6. The gap (our inference)
- Persona Vectors predicts trait shifts from data via ΔP, but on one pipeline's severity-laddered datasets, pooled correlations, no leave-one-dataset-out test, and no framing manipulation.
- Alignment Forecasting predicts misbehaviour with an LLM reader on 32 datasets, apparently without activations or framing (abstract summary only).
- Li et al. and Zhang et al. predict *per-question* EM from base-model representations, not the dataset's overall character effect.
- Inoculation shows framing changes the outcome, but no work checks whether a data-side predictor anticipates the inoculation effect.
- Candidate contribution: a controlled 12 x 2 comparison of predictors (LLM reader, ΔP, steering-direction cosine, plus a trivial baseline such as dataset-vs-base perplexity or response length and label "is it harmful?") on a leave-one-dataset-out target, with framing as a built-in test that separates persona-inference from loss/format accounts.

## 7. Strongest prior against
1. **Persona Vectors already did the headline experiment** (data projections predict trait expression, Qwen2.5-7B included) and Alignment Forecasting already showed an LLM can forecast from datasets. The incremental result may be "ΔP and an LLM reader both predict harmful-vs-benign, which is trivial" (a "label is harmful" baseline could match). Any proposal must beat the *harmful-or-not* baseline and a *dataset-severity-rank* baseline, otherwise 24 points mostly encode the harmful/benign split.
2. **n is tiny.** 12 datasets with one seed gives a leave-one-out Spearman with a CI of roughly the whole interval; in our own TDA work a locator with rho 0.867 on 10 development units gave -0.047 on 20 fresh ones. Our E1/E1b EM rates were flat across conditions, so the dependent variable may have little range on the benign datasets, and more datasets (not more seeds) is the cheaper lever.
3. **The framing effect may not replicate** on a 7B model with LoRA: Tan's Qwen2.5-7B result was "in some ways stronger" in App. D, but conditional misalignment (Dubiński) says a null on generic questions can hide a context-triggered shift. Without context-matched questions a null framing effect is uninterpretable.
4. **Row-level evidence from our own repo is weak for projection** (AUC 0.615 on poison rows, Qwen2.5-14B). Dataset averaging could rescue this, but we have no evidence.
5. **Overlap risk:** Neel's own list item, Persona Vectors' authors, and Alignment Forecasting's authors all work here; the external review must check for concurrent work.

## 8. Design implications
- Choose Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct over OLMo-3-7B for this idea: Persona Vectors, Tan's replication and Turner's organisms are all on Qwen2.5; the OLMo stack is built but the pretraining-data angle is not needed here. Cost: new model settings, new layer constants, new judge validation.
- Include baselines before anything fancy (Neel's method minimalism): "is the dataset harmful" label, a plain LLM-reader prompt, ΔP with base responses, and raw projection (PV says it is worse).
- Pre-register the held-out target (the 56-question EM rate plus context-matched questions), the predictor hyperparameters (layer, truncation, reader prompt) and the leave-one-dataset-out protocol before any fine-tune finishes.
- Treat the framing arm as the primary new variable; test it with context-matched evals and report generic-question and context-matched results separately.
- Budget anchor from our own runs: E1 cost $58.56 for 13 models, E1b $41.99 for 10 runs; 24 short LoRAs plus ΔP generation is plausibly in that range but I have not priced it.
