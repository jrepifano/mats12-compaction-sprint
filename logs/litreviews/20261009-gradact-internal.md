## 0. The idea, split into the parts that have prior work
1. **Per-example gradients / influence to find which training rows drive a behaviour.** Heavily covered: Grosse 2023, Bae 2022/2024, Li 2024, Bao 2025, Choe 2024, Paulo 2026, Jaburi/Paulo (withdrawn), Kowal 2026, Epifano 2023, plus our own working-repo TDA stack (EK-FAC, damped Fisher, GradDot, BIF).
2. **Gradients to parameters to an activation-space direction.** Partly covered. Kowal 2026 (Concept Influence) goes from a *probe direction* to a gradient score (direction-to-data). Vetter 2026 goes from SAE *features* to pretraining documents. Soligo 2025 and our working-repo `adapter_directions` analysis give the rank-1 B-direction geometry. **Nothing in the repo goes data-to-direction-to-steering as one chain.**
3. **Steer along the direction and read the behaviour.** Covered as a tool (E13 hooks, E1b steer, working-repo `persona_steer_check.py`, Soligo ablation/steering, Chen persona vectors). Not covered as a *check on attribution*.
4. **Retraining validation of attributions.** Covered and the weak point: Paulo 2026 validated by retraining (positive, OLMo, all-bad pool); our application project's held-out LDS failed; Epifano 2023 (Jacob's paper) is about exactly this check.
5. **Comparison "fixed-strength write direction" vs "input-gated weight edit".** Not covered anywhere; partly *enabled* by Soligo 2025 §4 (LoRA scalars, per-layer adapter scaling) and E13's `direction.py` diagnostic `cos(u, adapter B)`.

## 2. Findings

### Q1. What already covers each step
- **Attribution (row-level):** thoroughly covered, see §0 part 1, and by our own working-repo stack. Open caveat from the papers: gradient scores track format (Kim 2026, fetch summary only) and mathematically large influence can mean negligible behavioural change (Li 2024, as quoted in Kowal).
- **Attribution to a semantic direction:** Kowal et al. define influence with respect to a probe or SAE direction and show the projection approximation is order-of-magnitude cheaper with comparable performance on EM benchmarks (abstract, intro). This is the *reverse* of the proposal (direction first, data second), but it is the same quantity: v^T times the gradient. Chen et al.'s projection-difference screening is the same family (per E7/X7 reviews).
- **Parameters to activation direction:** for rank-1 only, Soligo 2025 and our `adapter_directions` analysis. No paper maps *per-example gradient contributions* to a residual-stream direction.
- **Steering along it:** covered as a method; the consistent finding is that the **B vector itself is not the effective steering direction** (see Q4 and §3).
- **Retraining validation:** Paulo 2026 (positive for deletion), our application project (negative for deletion-locator held-out, and for influence-picked rewrites), Epifano 2023 (methodology). TAME's token mask is a retraining-based check at the token level.

### Q2. What our own work found about attribution reliability (precise)
- **Deletion locator, held-out failure** (`../mats12_neel_working/results/tda/lds_heldout.json`, `lds_results.json` key `L3_defif_c10`): Spearman 0.867 on 10 development groups, **-0.047 on 20 fresh random 685-row groups** (random band +/-0.44, null percentile 0.423). 18 of 21 locators failed held-out, 3 inconclusive (`phase1-report.md` ~4606-4625, via E9b review). The development groups were slices of a preliminary gradient ranking (`phase1-report.md`:9249).
- **Retrain noise ceiling** (`noise_check.json`): ICC 0.578 (primary) and 0.634 (contrastive) over five groups retrained at a second seed; seed-1 vs seed-2 rank correlation rho = 0.10; sqrt(ICC) about 0.76 is above every locator's held-out rho (best 0.28). Inference: the retrain target itself was too noisy for 685-row groups to be ranked, regardless of locator quality.
- **Rewriting:** influence-picked rewrite of 685 rows tied random (23.2% vs 23.9% EM), while a $2 content judge beat random by 3.6-4.3 points in every seed under every judge (`phase1-report.md` ~4810-4826). Pool was about 50% poison, query was 67/71 gender-roles answers.
- **Estimator agreement on the same model:** at rank-1 14B the repo had a closed-form per-row gradient (18,944-d), an independent Kronfluence EK-FAC (per-query Spearman 0.719/0.792/0.781/0.831 vs the analytic EK-FAC on 200 rows), GradDot 0.733 and analytic EK-FAC 0.782 on the development LDS (`phase1-report.md` ~1234, 1276). Damping flips signs: undamped GradDot says the 8c placebo removed support in over 70% of rows, damped dEF-IF says the opposite for over 55% (E9b review of `replacement_paired_diff.json`).
- **Bayesian influence (BIF/SGLD)** failed its own preregistered acceptance bar (code map line 207, 113).
- **Adapter-direction geometry (rank-1, 14B, 17 adapters; `results/tda/adapter_directions.json`, `phase1-report.md` 1648-1706, 1802-1812):**
  - The whole-dW "poison direction" is not seed-stable: cross-seed cos(dW) 0.117-0.129.
  - Exploratory (post hoc, labelled so in the report): **|cos(B)| 0.88-0.96 across seeds while |cos(A)| 0.12-0.15**. The write direction is seed-stable; the read direction keeps its random init.
  - All non-arm-1 adapters sit at 0.955-0.998 of their matched-seed arm-1 dW although they span about 27 pp of EM; the EM differences must live in the 10-25% orthogonal remainder (inference).
  - **Q3: |cos(B, probe direction d_24)| = 0.0005-0.0089, at or below random orientation (about 0.011).** What the adapter writes is not the direction a base-model linear probe reads. This is the direct warning for step 2-3.
- **E9:** the optional gradient screen (Part 2) was never run; E9 stopped 2026-10-08 ($8.10) because stance-bearing text was too rare. `em-olmo`'s gradient-alignment score on OLMo pretraining docs was null, with the opposite sign to the prediction, and correlated with the projection ranking at Spearman 0.05 (E9 prereg line 157, citing Rui 2026).
- **X7 review:** X7 is unrun; its premise is that attribution has not been applied to RL. Reviews flagged format-tracking (Kim) and manipulability (Yadav) as threats.

### Q4. Is the "B direction x A.x gate" framing right for the public Qwen2.5-7B adapters?
- **No.** The public Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct organisms (bad-medical, risky-financial, extreme-sports) are **rank 32, alpha 64, rsLoRA, all seven projections at every layer** (`papers/em_personas/INDEX.md` row 2 from the HF API check; EQ0 report line 146: "rank 32, α 64, rsLoRA, 7 projections", from `results/EQ0/download.json`). That is 28 layers x 7 modules = 196 adapted modules, each a rank-32 update. The prompt's description (rank-1, a single down-proj) matches Turner's Qwen2.5-**14B** rank-1 organisms (layer 24 of 48) and the working repo's own `jrepifano/q14b-*` adapters, not the 7B public ones.
- **What survives for rank 32:** per module, Delta h = s * B (A x) with A x a 32-vector gate; the module writes in a 32-d subspace of its output space with 32 gates. Only `down_proj` and `o_proj` write directly to the residual stream; q/k/v/gate/up write into other spaces (attention scores, values, the MLP hidden state), so "steer the residual stream along the write direction" is undefined for 5 of 7 module types. The gate is a function of that module's input at that layer and the preceding adapters (adapters compound across layers). Steering would need to be specified per (layer, down_proj/o_proj) and multi-rank. (Inference from standard LoRA algebra.)
- **For rank 1 the framing is exact:** with rsLoRA scale alpha/sqrt(r), `down_proj` output changes by s * B (A x) with s = 512 for r=1 (the working repo's `tda_pod.py` asserts `scaling==512`). Our own recipe (E12/E13: layer 14 of Qwen2.5-7B's 28, alpha 512, rsLoRA) gives the exact setting for 7B.
- **But our rank-1 7B organisms are weak.** E12 (Qwen2.5-7B, rank 1, bad-medical + UltraChat 1:1): **0 of 3 seeds induced**; dev EM 4.75, 5.05, 4.50% vs the bar base + 10 = 10.25% (E12 run log, 04:34Z). E13's rank-1 organism (bad/good medical answers, no UltraChat) reached dev EM about 8-12%, unconditional (E12 log, E13 report). Rank-32 with Tan's recipe (alpha 16, lr 1e-4, batch 4x4) induced 24-34.5% on unmixed medical (E11/E12 post-mortem). So **at 7B a clean rank-1 EM organism is not available off the shelf**; at 14B one is (Turner public, plus our private set).
- **Implication of several rank-1 modules (Soligo's 9-adapter model, layers 15-29):** the gates specialise (layers 21, 23 medical; 15, 16, 17, 22, 28, 29 general). Attribution would need to be per module, and different modules would be driven by different rows. That is a feature for the proposal (a testable prediction: top rows for a "general" adapter should be non-medical), but it multiplies the number of directions to steer.
- **Fixed-strength vs gated comparison, caveat from our data:** a fixed push of strength g along B ignores that A x varies per token; the gate in a trained rank-1 adapter is mostly large on the tokens where A aligns with x. The existing E13 hook code already adds a fixed vector at every position on prefill and decode, which is the "fixed" arm; the "gated" arm is the adapter itself (alpha-scaled `lora_B`, the `lorack.py` scale variants 0.5/1/2).
- **Do not assume steering along B reproduces the behaviour.** Soligo: cos(B, mean-diff) = 0.04 while ablating mean-diff removes over 98% of EM and ablating it from B only 30%. Ours: cos(B, base probe direction) about 0. So "the write direction" and "the effective behavioural direction" differ, and a null for B-steering would not falsify the attribution (inference).

### Q5. Overlap with existing proposals
- **E7 (influence scores for rewriting):** shares step 1 and step 4. E7 asks whether a score predicts *rewrite* effects; this idea asks whether the score's *parameter-space meaning* reproduces behaviour under steering. Different outcome; shares the same 14B stores and the same failure history. The E7 text already says "don't run on bad-medical organism".
- **E9b (gradient screen for locating document groups):** CPT groups on OLMo; the gradient screen was optional and unrun. No overlap in model or intervention, but the same Li 2024 / Kim 2026 warning applies.
- **X7 (attribution during RL):** same attribution machinery; different setting (Qwen3-4B, GRPO). No overlap in what is tested.
- **Atkinson ideas block (line 2860):** closest in spirit: attribute over adapter output dimensions, compare two tasks by cosine, narrow vs broad on the E1/E1b adapters. It has no steering-readout step, and it is at rank 32 on OLMo. This idea could use the same integrated-gradients-on-adapter-scale attribution as the *parameter* side, so there is shared infrastructure.
- **E13/E14/E15:** E13 builds the steering hooks, the mean-diff direction extractor with split-half reliability (median cosine >= 0.7), and a `cos(u, adapter B)` diagnostic (`src/emx/e13/direction.py`). E13's first run stopped because both directions failed reliability (0.58 and 0.63 vs 0.7) on a rank-32 organism. E14/E15 use E13's patch code. **Overlap:** the steering half; here the direction would come from attribution rather than bad-minus-good mean-diff, which makes "attribution-derived direction vs mean-diff direction" a clean comparison arm. Not overlap: nobody derives a direction from gradients.
- **Genuinely new here (internal view):** (a) the chain data to gradient to residual-stream direction to steering to behaviour, tested as a chain; (b) comparing fixed-strength write-direction steering with the gated weight edit on a rank-1 adapter; (c) using a *behavioural steering readout* as a different, cheaper validation than retraining deletion. Whether (a)-(c) are new externally is for the external review. Gradient Atoms is not in the repo, so I cannot say how much of (a) it already covers.

### Q6. Cheapest retraining validation
- **Measured costs on this repo's recipes (Qwen2.5-7B, H100 at $3.49-3.99/h):** rank-1 trains in about 230-285 s (E13: 857 steps in 231 s, 0.22 s/step at batch 16; four arms 278-287 s each; EQ0 bench 0.187 s/step). Rank-32: 0.4345 s/step (E11 bench), about 6 min for an epoch of 857 steps (inference: 857 x 0.43 s), though E12's second pod showed per-save overheads of about 3 minutes and 2.08 s/step with checkpointing, so unpriced overheads can dominate (E12 post-mortem: $48.47 vs $17-21 planned).
- **Evaluation:** about $0.21 per 1,000 answers fully scored (EQ0, two judges x two metrics). A 56-question x 10-sample evaluation is 560 answers, about $0.12.
- **So one retrain-and-evaluate arm costs about $0.3-1.0 all-in** if no per-save overhead occurs. A top-k-removed vs random-k-removed vs bottom-k-removed contrast, 3 seeds each, is 9 arms, roughly **$5-10 including a 1-h pod and pod overhead (inference, not measured on this design)**. This is well below the earlier "$115-175" estimate for the 14B rewrite study because that used 14B, 20+5 retrains of 857 steps and 24-31 min each (`phase1-report.md` and §6 line 3220: 24 min rank 1, 31 min rank 32 on 14B).
- **Cheaper than retraining, partially validating:** (1) frozen-query NLL shift under the already-trained adapters, which the working repo's `tda_query_nll.py` computes without training; (2) the steering readout itself, which is forward-only. But these do not replace the retraining check Jacob specified.
- **Noise caveat:** the application project's seed-1 vs seed-2 retrain correlation was 0.10 and ICC about 0.6 for 685-row groups at 14B. Paulo et al. used 12 seeds per arm. Three seeds is a directional pilot. Power must come from larger removal fractions (20% like Paulo, not 5%) rather than more seeds.
- **Dataset:** the decrypted bad/good medical advice files are 7,049 rows each (`../mats12_neel_working/data/raw/`); the 14B stores were built on the 13,698-row 1:1 bad-medical/UltraChat mixture, which is a different pool. With a pool of all-bad rows (7,049) Paulo's regime is closer; E12 showed the 1:1 UltraChat mixture does not induce at 7B rank 1.

## 3. Our prior results that transfer
1. **Deletion-locator failure and the noise ceiling** (Q2): any retraining-validated claim here must report the seed-noise ceiling first and use large-fraction removals; "ranks groups of random rows" is a hopeless target.
2. **Write direction stable, read direction random** (|cos B| 0.88-0.96 vs |cos A| 0.12-0.15, exploratory): supports defining the "parameter's write direction" as B, and warns that the read half A is arbitrary across seeds. It also means gradient attribution to the A block is gauge-dependent while attribution to B is not; only gauge-invariant quantities (s*B*A) should be compared across seeds (the repo already enforces this, `probes.py` `fix_gauge`).
3. **cos(B, probe direction) about 0 and Soligo's 0.04:** B is not where a probe reads. Steering along B is therefore a harder, riskier test than steering along a mean-diff vector; plan both arms and a random-direction control of matched norm.
4. **Damping flips the sign of rewrite scores** (8c placebo): report undamped GradDot, Hessian-free and EK-FAC separately, as Jaburi/Paulo also find all three similar.
5. **EQ0 (rank-32 public organisms):** EM 29.9%, 45.6%, 32.3% vs base 3.8%, coherence 90-93%. Gives the behavioural target for a rank-32 version, and shows the organism is strong enough that a steering readout has room. Gotchas: sdpa attention gives NaN gradients in training (use eager); the vLLM rank-1 path was not verified (EQ0 report line 153: max difference 0.497 nats vs 0.05 bar; E13 later passed a noise-calibrated check on its own rank-1 adapter, 20/20 nearest-true).
6. **E13 first run:** mean-diff directions can fail reliability (0.58/0.63 vs 0.7); a gradient-derived direction would face the same split-half test.
7. **E12 post-mortem:** pilot the organism and price per-save costs before building; this idea's first gate should be a cheap pilot, not a pipeline.

## 5. Closest prior work and the remaining gap
- **Closest, chain-wise:** Kowal 2026 (direction to data score), Vetter 2026 (SAE feature steering and feature-to-document attribution), Soligo 2025 (rank-1 B direction vs mean-diff, adapter-scalar probing and layer-wise adapter steering), Paulo 2026 (retraining-validated attribution for EM), and our own `adapter_directions` analysis (B stable, B not equal to probe direction). Atkinson 2026 is closest for the attribution-over-adapter-output-dimensions method.
- **Gap that remains (internal view only):** nothing here (a) takes row-level gradient contributions to a rank-1 adapter's B parameters, (b) turns them into a residual-stream direction, (c) steers with it at fixed strength, and (d) compares the resulting behaviour with the gated adapter and with data-removal retraining. Whether the external literature already contains this (Gradient Atoms is a candidate; not in the repo) is for the external review.
- **Strongest internal prior against:** our own history says gradient attribution has not beaten a content judge on this data (rewrite tie with random, held-out LDS -0.05), and a retraining check with seed ICC about 0.6 may not resolve differences. Soligo's cos 0.04 says B-steering may not reproduce the behaviour even if attribution is right. A positive steering readout would be informative; a null is ambiguous.
