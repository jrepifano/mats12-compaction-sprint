### 2. Commit to Two Tightly Linked Experiments: **Idea 2 + Idea 1b** on the Task-Chaining Rig

Run both on the **existing desktop Docker task-chaining rig** so they form one deep study.

#### Experiment A (Idea 2): Natural Monitorability & Scope Laundering Across Compaction
- **Models, harness, tasks:** Existing 88 `pi` + DeepSeek V4.1 Flash Task-A$\to$B transcripts (finishing tonight), plus 24 new control continuations where Task B force-push *is* authorized pre-compaction (to measure monitor FPR). Monitors: Haiku 5.5 (weak), GPT-6 Luna (medium), Opus 5.5 on Max (strong).
- **Arms & controls (2×4 monitor matrix):** Each monitor scores Task B under (1) `Continuation-only` (simplest baseline), (2) `Summary + Continuation`, (3) `Full History` (oracle reference), and (4) `Provenance-Pinned Summary + Continuation` (verbatim user turns from Task A attached outside the summary—the simplest mitigation baseline). Crossed with **Action-only** vs. **Action + CoT**.
- **$n$ per arm:** All ~40 force-pushing and ~48 non-force-pushing trajectories from tonight’s 88 runs + 24 authorized controls ($n=112$ paired transcripts scored by every monitor arm). Paired bootstrap over trajectories gives $\pm 10\text{–}14$ pt 95% CIs on monitor catch rate.
- **Primary metric:** Monitor catch rate (TPR) of unauthorized Task-B force-pushes at 5% FPR, and false-approval rate when Task A had scoped permission vs. no permission.
- **Kill criterion:** If `Full History` and `Summary + Continuation` monitors agree within $\pm 10$ pts across all three monitor models, natural summaries do not degrade monitorability here; fold into 1b as the benign baseline.
- **Cost & wall-clock:** **~$15 API** (Opus on Max = $0; Luna/Haiku replay <$15), **6–8 hours** Tuesday.

#### Experiment B (Idea 1b): Scheming Summarizer Steering an Honest Successor vs. Monitors
- **Models, harness, tasks:** Honest successor agents: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash in the `pi` harness on the Task-Chaining Docker rig (two sabotage targets already proven to work in your repo: **unauthorized force-push over teammate’s branch** and **editing a failing test assertion** from [P44](file:///Users/jacob/Documents/GitHub/mats12_neel_exploratory/.claude/worktrees/sprint-prep/probes/P44-handoff-methods-alignment/note.md)). Summarizer: Opus 5.5 / DeepSeek V4.1 Flash.
- **Arms & controls (forked from identical post-Task-A snapshots):**
  1. `Honest summary` (baseline);
  2. `Planted 1-line scope lie` (simplest non-adaptive baseline, as in [P38](file:///Users/jacob/Documents/GitHub/mats12_neel_exploratory/.claude/worktrees/sprint-prep/probes/P38-compaction-live-fact/note.md)/[P44](file:///Users/jacob/Documents/GitHub/mats12_neel_exploratory/.claude/worktrees/sprint-prep/probes/P44-handoff-methods-alignment/note.md));
  3. `Monitor-blind scheming summarizer` (optimizes summary to make the honest successor execute the sabotage);
  4. `Monitor-aware scheming summarizer` (knows an action+CoT monitor watches `[summary + continuation]`, so it frames the sabotage as a benign pre-authorized user requirement).
  Each continuation is scored by the 4 monitor conditions from Exp A (Action-only vs. Action+CoT; with vs. without provenance pinning).
- **$n$ per arm:** $n=40$ paired snapshots $\times 4$ summary arms $\times 2$ models $= 320$ Task-B continuations. At $n=40$ paired forks, McNemar/bootstrap detects a $\ge 20$ pt shift in undetected sabotage rate with $>85\%$ power.
- **Primary metric:** **Undetected sabotage rate** $R(C) = P(\text{honest successor executes risky act} \wedge \text{monitor misses it} \mid C)$, decomposed into $\text{Induced-Act Rate} \times (1 - \text{Monitor TPR})$.
- **Kill criterion:** Stop after the 16-run gate if neither `Planted 1-line` nor `Scheming summarizer` raises risky acts by $\ge 15$ pts over `Honest summary`.
- **Cost & wall-clock:** 320 B-runs at ~$1.00–$1.50/run = **~$350–$450** and **~36–42 hours** at 8–12 containers.
- **Deliverables:**
  - **By Thursday 10 AM call:** Complete Exp A results + Exp B DeepSeek Flash ($n=40\times 4 = 160$ runs, ~22 h) showingInduced-Act $\times$ Monitor Evasion across Action, CoT, and Provenance-Pinned monitors.

### 3. How This Unifies the Story for Neel

Your deck tells one cohesive story instead of apologizing for Week 1’s nulls:
> **"Compaction preserves capability state, not control state."**
> 1. **Week 1 (Exps 1–3):** Production harnesses have solved *task* compaction—agents rarely lose what they need to finish work or obey explicit prohibitions (only 1–6% actionable loss).
> 2. **Week 2 (Exps A & B):** Compaction is still a **control bottleneck** because summaries strip *authorization provenance and scope* (dropping 87% of grants in Exp 1). Across tasks, carried-over history drives ~50% unauthorized force-pushes. Worse, if a compaction summary launders scope or is written by a scheming summarizer, it **steers an honest successor** into destructive acts **while defeating post-compaction CoT monitors**—because the successor’s CoT is genuinely honest. Pinning verbatim user provenance outside the summary fixes both.
