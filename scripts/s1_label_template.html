What each choice means

Items are rules, permissions, decisions, commitments and task-state facts quoted from the history. Kept: the summary states it with the same meaning. Dropped: the summary doesn't state it. Misstated: the summary states it, but changed (wrong scope, polarity, value or status). Not an item: the quote isn't something an agent continuing the work would need to keep (chatter, an aside, or not actually a rule, permission, decision, commitment or state).

Item cards ask two questions, in order. First, from the history alone: is this an item? An item is something an agent resuming the work would need from here on: a standing rule (from the user, the task statement or an instruction file), a permission, a decision (it stays an item after it is carried out), a commitment, or the current task state. Not items: trivial lines (routine output, a number a later one replaced, narration), commands as typed, data examples, asides, and one-off requests already carried out. Decide this the same way whatever the summary says; the summary appears only after you answer Yes. Then: kept, dropped or misstated.

Claims are the highlighted spans of the summary; the claim line above each is one reading of the span. Label the span. Supported: the history says it, in any wording, including the agent's own unchecked statements and the plans it made (a hedge such as "probably" turned into a fact still counts as supported). Contradicted: the history says otherwise, or a later message changed it (e.g. the summary says the tests pass but the last run failed). Unsupported: nothing in the history says it or reasonably implies it, so the summary invented it. This includes a next step the summary proposes that nobody in the history planned. The question is whether the summarizer made it up, not whether it is true. If parts of a span differ, choose Unsupported when any part is unsupported, else Contradicted when any part is contradicted. Can't tell: even the full history and summary aren't enough to decide; these are left out of scoring.

Every card shows the full history with a search box. Item cards open at the quote. Claim cards open at the passage with the most words in common with the span; the "Match 1–5" buttons jump to the five best such passages, found by code, which may miss the evidence. Before calling a claim Unsupported, search the history for its key terms.

Claim cards labelled before the full history was on the page are outlined and marked re-check: change the label, or press "Keep label" if it stands.
