●​ Studying CoT
      ○​ CoT controllability
             ■​ If we... ...give the model a problem to solve, and tell it that there is a
                 grader who looks at its chain of thought, and checks whether the chain
                 of thought contains certain things or whether the chain of thought
                 indicates that it's going to cheat. Do the models try to control their
                 chain of thought to not do this? And if so, how successful are they?
             ■​ How far can you push elicitation of CoT controllability? Use filler
                 tokens, try a bunch of prompts, etc. Relevant work. Do not fine-tune it
             ■​ Can you come up with good alternative settings for studying CoT
                 controllability?
             ■​ Can you find ways to study CoT controllability that are more realistic
                 proxies for the real threat models eg getting models to obscure key
                 info needed for the solution from their CoT, or to replace it with
                 incorrect info. Or generally to be misleading or incorrect
             ■​ If you disable the chain of thought but tell the model to begin its output
                 with a secret scratchpad before giving its answer, and you tell it to
                 obfuscate its chain of thought enough that it is not directly helpful for
                 solving some problem on, say, a problem with high serial depth, like
                 applying a lookup table n times (e.g. brew here), how good is it at
                 controllability and also doing the task? Can it get meaningful uplift
                 beyond what it would get with filler tokens? Can it do any kind of
                 steganography?
