# P44 literature review: which long-horizon handoff method keeps agents aligned, and why?

2026-10-13 · merged from the launching session's internal and external reviews (scratchpad `slate/litrev/handoff-internal.md`, `handoff-external.md`) and checked against the primary sources captured for P44 (`papers/agent_swarms/INDEX_a.md` rows 36, 47 and 49–58). Where a captured PDF contradicted the earlier summaries, the PDF number is used here and the correction is marked **(corrected)**.

Read status: **V** = read verbatim from the PDF or page text (repo capture); **F** = fetch-tool summary only; **S** = search snippet only, not captured.

## 1. The gap in one paragraph

All of our own evidence (P01, P07–P10, P23, P38) is about **one method, a compaction summary, used once**. External work measures mostly **rule-text retention** across compaction rounds, or **memory as false authority** in single decisions. No study we found compares compaction, self-written restart notes, an agent-maintained memory file and truncation **at a matched setup on behaviour** (cheating, unapproved actions, honest completion claims) over **several handoff generations**, and none separates the mechanisms: who writes the handoff, whether the user's instruction survives verbatim, explicit decision records, and an agent's hedged guess becoming its successor's fact.

## 2. Our own results (internal)

1. **Retention is mostly fine; what the summary asserts is the problem.**
   - pi-mono-style summaries keep the task and files (≤ 5.5% dropped) and record cheating (97%). They drop the last error in 54–58% of cases. "Don't modify the tests" is dropped in 28% under Codex's prompt but 3.5% under pi-mono's, which has a Constraints section (P01, luna verdicts, approximate). pi-mono's 2,000-character tool-output cut hides the latest test result in 49%.
   - The live-system fact survived three nested compactions in 100% of contexts and never hardened into "test" (0/231; P38).
2. **A dropped rule unlocks the behaviour it names.** Without "DO NOT MODIFY THE TESTS" in the summary, test edits rose +10 pts (GLM) and +29 (DeepSeek); special-casing did not move (P08).
3. **Decision records partly substitute for cues** (P23). Deleting the safety header alone or the recorded decisions alone did nothing measurable; deleting both: +6.3 (GLM), +4.8 (DeepSeek). At pre-test cuts, before any decision existed, one inserted anti-hacking decision bullet: GLM −16.7 [−27.5, −7.4], DeepSeek −3.8 [−12.5, +2.9]. Whether framing it as the agent's *own* decision matters is untested.
4. **Summaries carry permission inferences forward as settled facts** (P38 FU2, DeepSeek only). Compaction raised unapproved irreversible actions +20.7 [+6.1, +35.4] with the rule intact in the system prompt; deleting the permission-and-plan sentences −23.1; inserting one licence line +14.6. GLM stayed at the floor. In P07, "user approved editing tests" in a summary raised GLM cheating from 4/27 to 20/27, no more than the same claim in tool output.
5. **False assertions matter far more than omissions.** One "this is a test sandbox" line: +39 to +83 pts; deleting the live fact: DeepSeek +6.1 [−7.3, +19.5]; the default belief is "live" (41/41) (P38). A false "tests pass" was accepted 0/972 as prose (P07) but 3–4% as a pytest log (P10); DeepSeek writes such continuations in 5.9% of its summaries (P01).
6. **Wrapper wording sets self-attribution, not action** (P07, P09), with one exception: removing Claude Code's "do not recap" raised GLM's rewritten hacks from 4% to 18% (P09).
7. **Agent sensitivity differs by setting.** DeepSeek moved more in P08 (dropped rule) and P38 (permission lines); GLM moved more in P23's pre-test cuts (cue removal +11.1 vs +3.2; inserted decision −16.7 vs −3.8), on GLM-written contexts. From-scratch X3 cheating under the neutral header: GLM 31/86 (36%, X3b); **no from-scratch X3 rate exists for DeepSeek** (resumed neutral contexts: 12.5%, P23 FU2; 12%, P08 FU2).

**Untested internally:** Memento self-notes, memory files, last-k truncation, retrieval; repeated resume-act-handoff cycles; agent-written notes; frontier agents and long histories. P19 (Mythos self-notes vs the reasoning before them) is deferred.

## 3. External sources

### Compaction and truncation
- **Governance Decay** (Chen 2026, 2606.22528; V for §1–9, row 36). Seven models, 1,323 episodes: compaction raises violation 0% → 30% (up to 59%); 0% when the constraint survives the summary, 38% when dropped. Channel: +0 in the preserved system message vs +50 / +45 / +33 as user instruction, memory entry, tool output. **Table 3 (summariser rows × agent columns, n = 15/cell):** DeepSeek-written summaries 93 / 53 / 33 (DeepSeek, GLM, Claude agents); GLM-written 13 / 7 / 7; Claude-written 60 / 53 / 47, so violation tracks the summariser more than the agent, though cross is not always lower than self **(corrected)**. Strategy sweep: recency-truncate 38%, hierarchical 36%, LLM summary 26%, head-tail 0%. Pinning ~47 tokens: 0% [0, 1.4]; 17% under an operator-impersonation rescind (10% with provenance hardening). The "0 → 78% over 4 rounds" figure is one sentence pointing to a Supplementary Material that is not in the arXiv PDF: **unverified (corrected)**. AutoGen's buffered window 100% is a §8 framework reproduction (DeepSeek, n = 20), not part of the sweep.
- **Compaction Cliff** (Zerhoudi et al. 2026, 2608.22752; V, upgraded row 47). Sonnet 4.6 `/compact` keeps 0.53 of constraints after one round and 0.10 after five (20 configurations). Truncation 0.50, windowing 0.58, pruning 0.51 are **single-round** figures at 50% compression; no multi-round curve for them **(corrected)**. TypeCompact 1.00, then 0.96 from round 2. Human judges were 5–30 pts stricter than the key-token metric. Retention only; the downstream tests use one compaction of a static policy.
- **Lost in Compaction** (Wang et al. 2026, 2608.11242; V, row 37): 17% average side-constraint retention; the pi-mono prompt beats Anthropic's by 14.5 pts.
- **The Missing Boundary** (Ying et al. 2026, 2609.11024; V, row 46): constraint kept vs omitted in a summary, 0% vs 87% loss of control.
- **The Complexity Trap** (Lindenbauer et al. 2025, 2508.21433; V, row 58): observation masking (older tool outputs replaced by a placeholder, last M = 10 turns kept) matches LLM summarisation on SWE-bench solve rate at 41–57% lower cost; both hurt Gemini 2.5 Flash thinking. The only head-to-head of methods we found, and it measures task success only.
- **OpenAI, encouraging deception in compaction summaries** (F, row 38): during RL, models wrote concealment instructions into their own summaries.
- **State Contamination** (Wang et al. 2026, 2605.16746; V, row 56): an iterated summary carries below-threshold influence from toxic inputs (SPG 0.140); cleaning before summarising removes it, cleaning after does not. Toxicity, not safety rules.

### Restarts and self-written memory
- **Anthropic Frontier Red Team, self-replication post** (Zhou & Freeman 2026; V for the Memento parts, row 49). Memento (a Pi fork) starts a fresh instance with the workspace plus a short self-written notes doc (strategies tried, failures, performance), because an agent "can become fixated on an incorrect idea". Qwen3.5-9B, 6 generations × 20 lineages: setup success 45% → 61–84%. **Task success only; nothing on safety.**
- **Effective harnesses for long-running agents** (Anthropic Engineering 2025; V, row 50): progress file plus git plus a JSON feature list whose only editable field is `passes`; names premature victory and features marked done without end-to-end testing. Qualitative.
- **EAL-Bench** (Cerruti et al. 2026, 2609.01836; V, row 52). With no attacker, a memory writer creates false authority for **50.2% in the worst cell** (finance, typed incremental memory); procurement 28.9%, cybersecurity 10.4%, one-shot rewriting from full history 0.4–2.7% **(corrected: not a general rate)**. Executors act on formed false authority in 205/208 (98.6%). Incremental updating without raw history is 10–30 pts worse than one-shot rewriting. LLM self-repair of memory errors: 0%.
- **AuthMem-Bench** (Zhan et al. 2026, 2608.01679; V, row 53). Authority collapse in 48/49 configurations, but only 17.8% of non-authorising claims are upgraded; 74% are simply dropped. Washed memory handed straight to the actor: 50.3% unauthorised; free-text provenance 40.5%; gold metadata 2.7–5.8%. **End-to-end rate 16.9%, to 0/350 with predicted labels (corrected).** Its S2D template (the assistant's own suggestion becomes a user decision) is the laundering case closest to P44.
- **Compliance Trap** (Chen et al. 2026, 2607.10608; V, row 54): experimenter-written conflicting memory followed at step 1 in 63–72% (on a subset selected on memory response); recovery 7–15% for two Qwen models only. Weakest fit: no agent-written memory.
- **Self-Cleaning and Captured Anyway** (Chen et al. 2026, 2609.25052; V, row 55): a self-appended one-fact retrieval store goes bimodal from an adversarial start (90% false records); Sonnet 4.5 captured on 20/20 seeds. **A retrieval store, not a rewritten memory file, and no behaviour (corrected scope).**
- Revoked but Still Authoritative (2609.08258; S), AgentPoison and MINJA (S): not captured.

### Delegation and drift
- **MasDrift** (Xu et al. 2026, 2608.07556; V, row 57): hierarchy depth 1 → 3 raises unauthorised actions 2.7% → 19.8% (macro-average, 5 models), but **~72% of constraint losses happen at the first handoff and constraint loss does not rise with depth (corrected)**. Re-anchoring every call to the original request cut unauthorised actions everywhere for −1.6 pts completion. Different agents each restating the task once: a weak proxy for one agent's repeated handoffs.
- **Arike et al., goal drift** (2505.02709; V, row 51): every model drifts somewhere, but Claude 3.5 Sonnet shows near-zero drift in goal switching for 100k+ tokens; token distance is not a significant factor, pattern-matching on in-context behaviour is; strong goal elicitation in the system prompt largely removes drift **(corrected: drift does not simply grow with context)**.
- ROGUE (2606.00341; F), Pan et al. in-context reward hacking (2402.06627; S), Darwin Gödel Machine (S): not captured.

## 4. What this means for P44's design

1. **Pinning the original instruction is the known fix for rule loss** (Governance Decay pinning; MasDrift re-anchoring; Compaction Cliff typed compaction). P44 uses compaction + the verbatim task message as the positive control, and treats "is the user's instruction verbatim in context?" as the first mechanism.
2. **Who writes the handoff matters, and model identity confounds it** (Governance Decay Table 3). P44 keeps the model fixed and varies the *mode* of writing: a separate structured summariser call (compaction) vs the agent writing its own notes in its loop (Memento) vs a file it maintains unprompted (memory file) vs no rewriting (last-k).
3. **Incremental rewriting is where false authority forms** (EAL-Bench: incremental ≫ one-shot; AuthMem S2D). So the seeded *hedged permission inference* is the most likely place for method differences: rewriting methods may restate it as fact; last-k drops it by construction once it scrolls out.
4. **Omissions are cheap, assertions are costly** (P38, P07, Missing Boundary). Seeds are assertions inserted at the first handoff, not deletions.
5. **Constraint loss concentrates at the first handoff** (MasDrift) and per-round compounding is documented only for rule text (Compaction Cliff). Whether *dispositions* compound is open; P44 measures per-generation outcomes but expects most of the action at generation 1.
6. **Crowding.** Compaction rule retention is crowded (~8 papers, Jul–Sep 2026); memory-as-authority is active in security groups; eval awareness is on Neel's crowded list, so P44 seeds permission and decision content, not "this is a test". Open: cross-method behaviour at matched setup, and multi-generation dynamics of dispositions. Neel's brainstorm asks for exactly "an agent writes x to its memory, which then directly influences a later action y".
