# P14: Does gating E13's steering patch on an activation probe prevent more misbehaviour than gating it on asking the model or on a strong text classifier, at comparable harm to observed answers, when the condition for misbehaviour is only implied?

E13 ([report](../../docs/experiments/E13/report.md)) built a Qwen2.5-7B organism whose misbehaviour has a **condition known by construction**: it gives EM answers 25–30% of the time under notices saying it is unobserved, against 4–6% when observed. On config and log snippets that only *imply* the status (Amendment D), it keeps 0.54 [0.41, 0.68] of that gap, and luna judged 0 of 39 unobserved snippets readable as "unobserved". One steering direction closes 0.50 of the gap when oracle-gated (input site, strength 4) and 0.20 always-on at the strength that is safe for observed answers (strength 1). The difference is what a detector of the condition is worth. This probe asks **which detector captures that worth on implied cues: the organism's activations, the organism's own answer when asked, or a strong classifier of the prompt text.** It is framed as detecting a **planted condition for misbehaviour**, not eval awareness: the condition and the behaviour it gates are planted and measured, so labels are not the hard part.

## Notes

**What already exists, free.** E13 saved activations only for the direction source (unobserved prompts, labelled EM or not), so no condition probe can be trained from them and the pod run is needed. All of S1's gate inputs (prompts, unsteered and steered answers, luna and Gemini scores) are in the main checkout's `results/E13/gen/` and `judged/` (gitignored; sha256 prefixes: `fpatch…a0` gen `495aaeffd1cf`, `fpatch…input` gen `a17fb322f58d`; full hashes in the manifest). E13's existing implied answers (`inferred__org_tan32_s*.csv`, 820 per seed on vLLM, one pair per (question, sample), 41 × 10 per condition) have no steered counterpart, so TEST needs new generation.

**Why the base-model probe matters.** The condition is in the prompt, so any reader of the text has the information. An activation monitor earns its place only if it beats text monitors and asking where the text is hard to read, and the interesting version is when the separation is the organism's own (A_org > A_base). This is the analogue of MacDiarmid et al.'s base-model control, here on implied cues.

**Why gates, not only AUROC.** The portfolio review asked for misbehaviour prevented at comparable harm. Gates fire at thresholds, and Das et al. show recall at a threshold can collapse while AUROC holds, so thresholds are set on implied calibration cues (CAL) at a matched observed firing rate, and the test cues are new.

**Biases to report.** The acceptance rule drops pairs whose side luna reads as the opposite status, which favours luna-based text monitors on TEST (conservative for the probe). Gemini 3.8 Flash wrote the snippets and is not used as a monitor. The organism saw no implied cue in training; neither do the probes.

**Distinct from the cohort's eval-awareness work.** Here the condition is planted, its labels come from the prompt by construction, and the target is a measured misbehaviour rate. Eval-awareness wording is avoided in the prompts and in the note.

**Interpretation limits.** One model, one organism recipe, one steering direction and site. Harm is judge-based. T1–T3 see only the notice text, not the question; ASK sees the system prompt and its own question; A_org's last-token read also sees the user question.
