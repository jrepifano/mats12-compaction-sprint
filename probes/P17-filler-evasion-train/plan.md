# P17: Can a small LoRA teach Gemma 4 31B to hide its filler-token intermediate value from the logit lens while keeping the filler's benefit, and is the value then hidden, moved, or given up?

Follow-up to P06 and P12. Prompting could not hide the hidden value y from a logit lens on DeepSeek V4 Flash (P12: at most −12% of y's cell share, y still readable on 99–100% of items). On Gemma 4 31B, filler adds +9.4 points on Brauer's task, and the filler K/V carries y causally: a matched-donor transplant moves the output closer to the donor's answer on 52% of items, against 5% for a random donor's output scored the same way (P06 F3). This probe asks the trained version of P12's question. Trained evasion of activation probes is already known to work and to transfer to unseen probes (Neural Chameleons; RL-Obfuscation), so that is not the question here. The new part: when the model **needs** the value to answer, does training against one reader **hide** it (other readers and patching still find it), **move** it (the filler stops carrying it causally), or **give it up** (the filler's benefit goes)?

## Notes

**Why train against the logit lens and hold the rest out.** The logit lens is the simplest reader and the one P06 and P12 used to call filler computation readable. Training against it, then asking whether the J-Lenses, a probe, patching and the model itself still find y, separates the three readings in one run. The penalty uses only the 10 digit tokens and only y's first digit (Gemma splits numbers into digits, so P06's readout scores first digits only). Evasion of that readout can mean y is encoded so that its first digit doesn't surface, which is the claim tested.

**Why task CE, and what K controls.** A LoRA trained only to match the base model would not move at all without the penalty, so K would be no control. With answer CE in both arms, K absorbs whatever task training does to accuracy, uplift and readability. The cost is that both arms may learn the task beyond the base model's 33%, and the uplift may shrink in both. The K-uplift stop rule covers that case.

**Interpretation limits.** One seed per arm; one task; first-digit readouts with chance near 10–13%, so P06's baseline (dots 25% vs 11%, label 36% vs 13%, n = 150) leaves a modest range for "recovery falls". The retrained probe can't tell y from c1·x (correlation 0.93 in P12). Brauer's generator has no licence: run, not copied.

**Literature.** Neural Chameleons and Keenan et al. are captured. RL-Obfuscation and Obfuscated Activations are not yet in `papers/`; the build's literature sub-agent captures both, reads them in full, and checks for any trained-evasion result on no-CoT or filler computation.
