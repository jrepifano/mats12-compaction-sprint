# P17: Can a small LoRA teach Gemma 4 31B to hide its filler-token intermediate value from the logit lens while keeping the filler's benefit, and do other readers then still find it?

Follow-up to P06 and P12. Prompting could not hide the hidden value y from a logit lens on DeepSeek V4 Flash (P12: at most −12% of y's cell share, y still readable on 99–100% of items). On Gemma 4 31B, filler adds +9.4 points on Brauer's task, and the filler K/V steers the answer: a matched-donor transplant moves the output closer to the donor's answer on 52% of items, against 5% for a random donor's output scored the same way (P06 F3; the transplant can't tell y computed in the filler from x relayed through it, P06 note caveat 2). This probe asks the trained version of P12's question. Trained evasion of activation probes is already known to work and to transfer to unseen probes (Neural Chameleons; RL-Obfuscation), so that is not the question here. The new part: when the filler's benefit depends on the computation, can training against one reader take y off that reader **without losing the benefit**, and do readers not trained against (J-Lenses, a probe) still find it?

## Notes

**Why train against the logit lens and hold the rest out.** The logit lens is the simplest reader and the one P06 and P12 used to call filler computation readable. Training against it, then asking whether the J-Lenses and a probe still find y, separates "hidden from one reader" from "hidden from readers generally" in one run, and uplift separates both from "given up". Where the computation then lives is not identified here: P06's transplant swaps x along with y, so it is reported as a steering check, and follow-up 3 is the targeted test. The penalty is the eval statistic's hinge (full-vocabulary log-softmax, max over layers and positions, per-digit baseline), so a zero penalty means the evaluated readout misses, up to the smooth max and the running-mean baseline; the smoke checks the match.

**Why task CE, and what K controls.** A LoRA trained only to match the base model would not move at all without the penalty, so K would be no control. With answer CE in both arms, K absorbs whatever task training does to accuracy, uplift and readability. The cost is that both arms may learn the task beyond the base model's 33%, and the uplift may shrink in both. The K-uplift rule covers that case.

**Interpretation limits.** One seed per arm; one task; first-digit readouts with chance near 10–13%, so P06's baseline (dots 25% vs 11%, label 36% vs 13%, n = 150) leaves a modest range, and at n = 300 a hit rate carries about ±3.4 points; hence the inconclusive band. The retrained probe can't tell y from c1·x (correlation 0.93 in P12); the pre-filler and input-only references show how much of its r the filler adds. Asking can recompute y from the prompt (P11), so it is a behavioural baseline, not a readout. Brauer's generator has no licence: run, not copied.

**Literature.** Neural Chameleons and Keenan et al. are captured. RL-Obfuscation and Obfuscated Activations are not yet in `papers/`; the build's literature sub-agent captures both, reads them in full, and checks for any trained-evasion result on no-CoT or filler computation.
