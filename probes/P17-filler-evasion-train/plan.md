# P17: Can a small LoRA teach Gemma 4 31B to hide its filler-token intermediate value from the logit lens while keeping the filler's benefit, and do other readers then still find it?

**Status (2026-10-12): built ahead, run later.** Per the wave-3 portfolio review, the paid run waits for two results: P16 (merged with P13), which shows which monitor capability is worth defending, and P24, which shows whether Gemma's filler carries y itself or only the copied inputs. It also waits for Jacob's GO, which may name one of the variants below. Results are wanted by **Wed 14 Oct 20:00 UTC** and due by **Fri 16 Oct 12:00 UTC** at the latest. The pod run takes about 4 h, so the latest comfortable launch is Wed ~14:00 UTC.

Follow-up to P06 and P12. Prompting could not hide the hidden value y from a logit lens on DeepSeek V4 Flash: the largest cut was 12% of y's cell share, and y stayed readable on 99–100% of items (P12). On Gemma 4 31B, filler adds +9.4 points on Brauer's task, and the filler K/V steers the answer. A matched-donor transplant moves the output closer to the donor's answer on 52% of items, against 5% for a random donor's output scored the same way (P06 F3). The transplant can't tell y computed in the filler from x relayed through it (P06 note, caveat 2); P24 tests that. This probe asks the trained version of P12's question.

Trained evasion of activation probes is already known to work and to transfer to unseen probes (Neural Chameleons; RL-Obfuscation), so that is not the question here. The new part is that the filler's benefit depends on the computation. Can training against one reader take y off that reader **without losing the benefit**? And do readers not trained against it (the J-Lenses, the same lens at other layers or digits, probes retrained on the evader's own activations) still find y?

## Notes

**Why train against the logit lens and hold the rest out.** The logit lens is the simplest reader, and the one P06 and P12 used to call filler computation readable. Training against it, then asking whether the other readers still find y, separates "hidden from one reader" from "hidden from readers generally" in one run. Uplift separates both from "given up". Retrained probes are the strongest held-out test: they are fit on the evader's own activations after training, as in Neural Chameleons' unseen-probe evaluation. Where the computation then lives is not identified here: P06's transplant swaps x along with y, so it is reported as a steering check, and follow-up 2 is the targeted test. The penalty is the eval statistic's hinge (full-vocabulary log-softmax, per-cell baseline, max over layers and positions, label and dots separately). So a zero penalty means the evaluated readout misses, up to the smooth max and the running-mean baseline; the smoke checks the match.

**Why task CE, and what K controls.** A LoRA trained only to match the base model would not move at all without the penalty, so K would be no control. With answer CE in both arms, K absorbs whatever task training does to accuracy, uplift and readability. The cost is that both arms may learn the task beyond the base model's 33%, and the uplift may shrink in both. The K-uplift rule covers that case.

**Seeds and power (new).** The first plan had one seed per arm and 300 readout items, so a seed-specific result could not be told apart from an effect. With three seeds per arm and all 500 items, a hit rate's CI is about ±2.7 points per model, and the pooled E − K contrast is tighter. Seed-to-seed spread is reported directly; the replication rule keeps one odd seed from setting the verdict.

**Interpretation limits.** **bf16 numerics (found 2026-10-13, log.md):** in Gemma 4 31B, rounding differences between two equivalent computations grow from ~0.2% relative L2 at layer 1 to ~40% by layer 56. So per-item logit-lens and J-Lens readouts at layers ≥ ~24 are not reproducible across, e.g., sdpa vs eager: about 1 nat mean cell gap, and 7 of 50 items flip their dots-group hit. Aggregate hit rates move less (label 0.36 vs 0.34, dots 0.18 vs 0.12, n = 50). Every model here is computed the same way (eager, cached prefix), and verdicts rest on aggregates over 500 items, but readout effects of a few points should be read with this noise in mind. One task, and first-digit readouts with chance near 10–13%. P06's baseline (dots 25% vs 11%, label 36% vs 13%, n = 150) leaves a modest range, hence the inconclusive band. The retrained probe can't tell y from c1·x (correlation 0.93 in P12); the pre-filler and input-only references show how much of its r the filler adds. Asking can recompute y from the prompt (P11), so it is a behavioural baseline, not a readout. The portfolio review's concern stands: even a clean positive establishes evasion of a reader on prompt-computable arithmetic. That is why the run waits for P24 (does the filler carry y at all) and P16 (is any reader worth defending). Brauer's generator has no licence: we run it, we don't copy it.

**Literature** ([lit-review.md](lit-review.md), build sub-agent, 2026-10-12). RL-Obfuscation, Obfuscated Activations and Westphal et al. 2026 are now captured (interp rows 99–101). What changed here: the gap is narrowed (above); a refit MLP probe is added; the fixed B probe is classified as the Neural Chameleons analogue; prediction (c) is raised; and follow-ups 6–7 are added. The review also expects late layers to be the easiest to evade (RL-Obfuscation App. G), so (a) may come quickly, and it expects the J-Lens, which reads through the same unembedding, to share part of the evasion.
