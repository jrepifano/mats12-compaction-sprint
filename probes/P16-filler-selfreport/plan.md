# P16: For filler-token computation, does reading activations beat asking the model, and do they come apart when the model has a reason to misreport?

Follow-up to P06 ([note](../P06-filler-token-computation/note.md): on V4 Flash the hidden value y is readable at the filler on 98–100% of items) and P12 ([note](../P12-filler-conceal/note.md): an instruction to conceal y doesn't remove it; an instruction can plant a decoy). Neither probe asked the simplest baseline, **just asking the model what y was**. P11 found that a reader built on the model did no better than asking. This probe asks the same question for filler computation, then gives the model a reason to misreport.

## Notes

**Why the fresh-ask control.** y = c1·x ± k is one hop from text in the prompt, so turn 2 can recompute it instead of recalling it. If S tracks F item by item, self-report is recomputation and not access to the filler computation. That doesn't make it useless as a monitor, but it changes what "asking" means. The faithfulness check on wrong-answer items is the sharper test: there the value the model used differs from the true y.

**Why the incentive is in the system message.** With causal attention, an instruction given only in turn 2 cannot change the filler activations, so readers would see y trivially. The reason to misreport must be known while the filler is computed.

**Interpretation limits.** As in P12, V4 Flash attends in full detail only to its last 128 tokens. Instructed misreport is role-play (Smith, Chughtai & Nanda); arm I is the closest this probe gets to a reason to misreport without an instruction to lie. R_fill excludes numbers written in the prompt, because a monitor knows the prompt.

**Literature.** Long et al. and Huan et al. were read only as fetch or search summaries. The build's literature sub-agent captures them into `papers/` and also checks Pacchiardi et al. 2023 (*How to catch an AI liar*) and Halawi et al. 2023 (*Overthinking the truth*). Brauer §7 was read verbatim.
