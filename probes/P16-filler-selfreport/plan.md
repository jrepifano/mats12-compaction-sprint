# P16 (merged with P13): When a model plants a decoy or misreports the value it computed at filler tokens, which fails: a lens-reading judge monitor, asking the model, or the information in its activations?

Follow-up to P06 ([note](../P06-filler-token-computation/note.md): on V4 Flash the hidden value y is readable at the filler on 98–100% of items) and P12 ([note](../P12-filler-conceal/note.md), with the PR #34 correction). Told "think about d at the dots instead of y" (arm CD), V4 Flash plants d readably on top of y without removing it. A modal-number monitor then names d, not y, on 186/200 items. But that monitor named y on only 104/200 under placebo, while a ridge probe still decoded y (r 0.97). Neither probe asked the simplest baseline, **asking the model what y was**, and P11 found a reader built on the model did no better than asking. The wave-3 portfolio review merged P13 (decoys against Brauer et al.'s lens-reading judge) into this probe. The merged probe is one decision package that separates three failures on the same items:

- **A. Monitor failure.** Does an unsupervised, Brauer-style lens monitor still name y when the model plants a decoy (P13's question)?
- **B. Self-report failure.** Does the model misreport y when told to, or given a reason to (P16's question)?
- **C. Recoverability.** Is y still in the activations (supervised probe, any-cell ceiling, readers at the reply) where A or B fails?

## Notes

**Why the incentive is in the system message.** With causal attention, an instruction given only in turn 2 cannot change the filler activations, so readers would see y trivially. The reason to misreport must be known while the filler is computed.

**Why the fresh-ask control.** y = c1·x ± k is one hop from text in the prompt, so turn 2 can recompute it instead of recalling it. If S tracks F item by item, self-report is consistent with recomputation, though recall would give the same number, so agreement alone can't separate them. The answer-consistency check on wrong items is sharper but still not decisive.

**Interpretation limits.** A monitor that reads the prompt can compute y outright, so the lens monitor is a proxy for settings where it can't, and a real monitor might also see the system prompt requesting the decoy (follow-up 3 bounds the first). V4 Flash attends in full detail only to its last 128 tokens (P12). Instructed misreport is role-play (Smith, Chughtai & Nanda); arm I is the closest this probe gets to a reason to misreport without an instruction to lie. R_fill-mon excludes numbers written in the prompt because a monitor knows the prompt; unfiltered R_fill is primary so that filtering doesn't handicap readers on items where y is written in the prompt (17/200 of P12's items).

**Literature.** Long et al. and Huan et al. were read only as fetch or search summaries; the build's literature sub-agent captures them into `papers/`, checks Pacchiardi et al. 2023 and Halawi et al. 2023, reads Stress-Testing's appendices and Keenan's §3–5, and searches for newer decoy-vs-lens-monitor work. Brauer §4–7 and App. F were read verbatim.
