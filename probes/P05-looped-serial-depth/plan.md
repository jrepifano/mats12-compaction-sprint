# P05: Do looped language models get more no-CoT serial depth than a matched non-looped model, and can we read their intermediate steps loop by loop?

CoT monitoring relies on limited serial depth per forward pass. Neel suspects looping explains Astra's 7.2 no-CoT arithmetic steps against 4.1 for second place [Nanda 2026, `papers/neel_brainstorm/INDEX.md` rows 4, 9], and asks "Do looped models have notably higher serial depth?" (`papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt`, Latent reasoning). **Re-scoped 2026-10-12 for wave 3** (v1 of this plan, 2026-10-11, is in git history). What changed and why is listed in Notes.

| **Measure** | **Serial depth d₅₀,** Neel's 50%-success crossing on one bank. For each configuration, bank and format, fit P(correct) = c + (1 − c)·sigmoid(s·(m − k)) by maximum likelihood, with c fixed at the bank's majority-class floor (≈ 0.07 for chain). The 50% crossing is d₅₀ = m − ln(1 − 2c)/s (m alone is the (1 + c)/2 point). Intervals come from a **paired** item bootstrap: the same items are resampled jointly for every configuration within depth cells (2,000 resamples, seed 0), so Δs get paired intervals. A curve that never crosses 50% in range is reported as a bound (< 1 or > 8), and a fit with s ≤ 0 (non-monotone) is reported as such, with raw per-k accuracies, never as a number. These are chain-step (or arithmetic-step) crossings on this bank; they are not on Neel's cross-task "arithmetic steps" scale, so we compare directions with his numbers, not values.<br>**Headline, on the matched pair (chain, F-nocot):** Δ = d₅₀(recurrent, r = 32) − d₅₀(non-recurrent), with its paired interval, plus the d₅₀-against-r curve. **Power check before the bulk run (free, laptop):** simulate the design from the smoke's fitted slopes; if the 95% interval on Δ is wider than ±0.5 steps at 100 items per depth, raise the matched pair (only) to 200 or 300 per depth. Each point is also given per FLOP: effective layers 4 + 6r + 4, against 16.<br>**Disproportionate? (conditional on the ladder running)** For the same loop counts, bits-per-byte on 1 MB of text written after every model's data cutoff (the 2026 markdown captures in `papers/`; sha256 recorded). Across the non-looped ladder, regress log d₅₀ on bits-per-byte. Each looped configuration's residual from that line is Neel's "disproportionate" question. The ladder is context, not a control (see Notes).<br>**Format check:** the loop gain d₅₀(r = 32) − d₅₀(r = 1), and Δ, are reported in both formats. If they disagree in sign, or only one excludes 0, the gain is reported as format-dependent, not averaged.<br>**Per-loop readability (secondary; reuses the main passes, but its extra coda and head passes and the prefix prompts add GPU time, which the benchmark prices):** on chain items with k = 2–4, at the last prompt position, decode each loop's state. Ouro uses its own head. Raven models get both a coda lens and a plain logit lens (`ln_f` and head, no coda). The non-recurrent baseline gets a layer-wise logit lens. **Candidates:** the 20 values 1–20, each scored by the logit of its first answer token as it appears after `Answer:`; the build checks each tokenizer has 20 distinct first tokens, and a model without them gets no readout. **Readable rate:** the share of items where step j's true intermediate is top-1 among the candidates, minus a control that scores another item's step-j value. Items whose intermediates repeat a value (within 1..k) are excluded. **Ask-the-model baseline (P11's lesson):** the same model's top-1 accuracy over the same 20 candidates on the j-step prefix of each item, as its own prompt. **Order:** Spearman ρ between j and the first loop at which intermediate j is top-1; an intermediate never top-1 is ranked last (ties), and the never-top-1 share is reported. A coda lens can compute on its own, so a positive result is called *readability* of intermediates, not evidence of an internal CoT or of monitorability. |

## Notes
- **What changed from v1, and why.**
  1. **Matched baseline.** v1's cross-model baselines were different pre-trains. McLeish et al. release a same-data, same-parent non-recurrent model, so the headline is now that pair, not Ouro against a regression line.
  2. **Two prompt formats.** P06 found filler uplift in Brauer et al.'s format (V4 Flash +19.5 points [+11.5, +28.0] at chain k = 2) but not under nocot's recipe (Gemma +0.8 [−1.8, +3.3]; V4 Flash too near floor to test), so a compute-for-depth effect can depend on format. A loop gain counts only with its format check.
  3. **One pass per model.** Every loop count is read from a single teacher-forced pass, instead of v1's separate run per loop count, which cuts GPU time several-fold.
  4. **Smaller scope, to fit 12 h.** Two banks instead of four, and k = 1 added against the floor. Ouro-2.6B moved to the follow-ups. Llama-3.2-3B (gated) dropped.
  5. **Ask-the-model baseline** added to the readout (P11's lesson).
  6. **Cap** unchanged at $40, and the reservation unchanged at $18 (4 GPU-hours), now covering the matched pair and both formats.
- **Confound: reasoning-trace training** (from another cohort project). The matched pair shares raw-text math data, and neither model saw reasoning-trace SFT, so the confound cancels within it. The cross-family ladder does not control for it: Qwen3-Base's pre-training includes synthetic reasoning data, and Ouro's and Nanbeige's later stages include reasoning-heavy data. So the ladder residuals are context, and any claim rests on the matched pair.
- **Plan review** (gpt-6-astra, 2026-10-12, `logs/reviews/20261012-P05-plan-r1.md`): **GO AFTER FIXES.** BLOCKING findings:
  1. d₅₀ formula gave the (1 + c)/2 point, not 50%: fixed (crossing m − ln(1 − 2c)/s, paired bootstrap, explicit bounds and non-monotone fits; dropped the claim of comparability with Neel's cross-task scale).
  2. Overlapping outcomes; nulls read as conclusions: fixed (five ordered outcomes with paired intervals, a ±0.5-step equivalence margin, an inconclusive outcome, and a pre-run power simulation).
  3. `addchain` fallback is commutative: fixed (replaced by `chain-easy`, an easier order-bound chain; else an elicitation floor).
  4. Exact-match scoring and multi-token readout: fixed (greedy generation plus nocot's parser is the reference, the accelerated scorer is validated per model and loop count; tokenization-safe candidates, the same candidates for the ask baseline, repeat and missing-appearance rules; "readability", not internal CoT).
  
  MINORs fixed: P06 reuse adapted and vendored into `p05/` (no `[pod].push` dependency); the pod benchmark prices the readout, bits-per-byte and prompt lengths; the matched pair runs first and the other families are conditional; source provenance corrected. Not fixed: the unverifiable-claims finding (the reviewer had no network; hashes and loop knobs were checked on HF on 2026-10-12, and the build captures the sources).
- **Other caveats.**
  - At r = 1 the recurrent model has 14 layers to the baseline's 16, so we report per-FLOP points too.
  - Recurrent-Llama was trained on math text only, so its no-CoT ability may be narrow. Running chain and arithmetic together guards against a single-bank artefact.
  - Floor risk is real: Gemma 4 31B's chain d₅₀ was only ≈ 2.2 under nocot's recipe (P06).
  - Custom code under pinned old transformers versions is the main build risk.
