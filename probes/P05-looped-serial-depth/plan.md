# P05: Do looped language models get more no-CoT serial depth than a matched non-looped model, and can we read their intermediate steps loop by loop?

CoT monitoring relies on limited serial depth per forward pass. Neel suspects looping explains Astra's 7.2 no-CoT arithmetic steps against 4.1 for second place [Nanda 2026, `papers/neel_brainstorm/INDEX.md` rows 4, 9], and asks "Do looped models have notably higher serial depth?" (`papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt`, Latent reasoning). **Re-scoped 2026-10-12 for wave 3** (v1 of this plan, 2026-10-11, is in git history). What changed and why is listed in Notes.

| **Measure** | **Serial depth d₅₀,** by Neel's method (so the numbers compare with his and with another cohort project's). For each configuration, bank and format, fit P(correct) = c + (1 − c)·sigmoid(s·(d₅₀ − k)) by maximum likelihood. c is fixed at the bank's majority-class floor. Intervals come from an item bootstrap within depth cells (2,000 resamples, seed 0). A curve that never crosses 50% is reported as a bound.<br>**Headline, on the matched pair (chain, F-nocot):** Δ = d₅₀(recurrent, r = 32) − d₅₀(non-recurrent), with its interval, plus the d₅₀-against-r curve. Each point is also given per FLOP: effective layers 4 + 6r + 4, against 16.<br>**Disproportionate?** For the same loop counts, bits-per-byte on 1 MB of text written after every model's data cutoff (the 2026 markdown captures in `papers/`; sha256 recorded). Across the non-looped ladder, regress log d₅₀ on bits-per-byte. Each looped configuration's residual from that line is Neel's "disproportionate" question. The non-recurrent baseline gets a layer-wise logit lens. **Readable rate:** the share of items where step j's true intermediate is top-1 among the 20 state tokens, minus a control that scores another item's step-j value. **Ask-the-model baseline (P11's lesson):** the same model's accuracy on the j-step prefix of each item, as its own prompt. **Order:** Spearman ρ between j and the first loop at which intermediate j is top-1. |

## Notes
- **What changed from v1, and why.**
  1. **Matched baseline.** v1's cross-model baselines were different pre-trains. McLeish et al. release a same-data, same-parent non-recurrent model, so the headline is now that pair, not Ouro against a regression line.
  2. **Two prompt formats.** P06 found filler uplift in Brauer et al.'s format (V4 Flash +19.5 points [+11.5, +28.0] at chain k = 2) but not under nocot's recipe (Gemma +0.8 [−1.8, +3.3]; V4 Flash too near floor to test), so a compute-for-depth effect can depend on format. A loop gain counts only with its format check.
  3. **One pass per model.** Every loop count is read from a single teacher-forced pass, instead of v1's separate run per loop count, which cuts GPU time several-fold.
  4. **Smaller scope, to fit 12 h.** Two banks instead of four, and k = 1 added against the floor. Ouro-2.6B moved to the follow-ups. Llama-3.2-3B (gated) dropped.
  5. **Ask-the-model baseline** added to the readout (P11's lesson).
  6. **Cap** unchanged at $40, and the reservation unchanged at $18 (4 GPU-hours), now covering the matched pair and both formats.
- **Confound: reasoning-trace training** (from another cohort project). The matched pair shares raw-text math data, and neither model saw reasoning-trace SFT, so the confound cancels within it. The cross-family ladder does not control for it: Qwen3-Base's pre-training includes synthetic reasoning data, and Ouro's and Nanbeige's later stages include reasoning-heavy data. So the ladder residuals are context, and any claim rests on the matched pair.
- **Other caveats.**
  - At r = 1 the recurrent model has 14 layers to the baseline's 16, so we report per-FLOP points too.
  - Recurrent-Llama was trained on math text only, so its no-CoT ability may be narrow. Running chain and arithmetic together guards against a single-bank artefact.
  - Floor risk is real: Gemma 4 31B's chain d₅₀ was only ≈ 2.2 under nocot's recipe (P06).
  - Custom code under pinned old transformers versions is the main build risk.
