# P34: If the model already has a persona that explains the bad data, does the bad-medical EM fine-tune stay narrow?

The persona-selection account of emergent misalignment (EM) says fine-tuning picks the simplest pre-existing persona that fits the data, and pretraining offers a broad villain but no narrow "quack". This probe gives Qwen2.5-7B a ready-made narrow persona, an otherwise decent quack doctor with false medical beliefs, before the standard bad-medical EM fine-tune, and asks whether EM then stays narrow (medical harm learned, broad EM low) beyond what a matched persona with non-medical false beliefs does. It bears on Neel's high-priority "why is EM so easy?" and "can you nudge it toward narrow misalignment?" (`papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt`). Literature: [lit-review.md](lit-review.md).

## Notes

- **Design changes from the brief, decided before data (log.md 2026-10-13):** (i) the pre-EM manipulation check gates only broad EM; the quack's own medical harm is its arm's baseline (launching session's correction); (ii) E11's full chat plan replaces "56 × 10" so the reused plain-EM arm's judged answers are directly comparable; (iii) arm 6's irrelevant prompt is the eccentric's description, making arms 2/3/5/6 a 2 × 2 of {weights, prompt} × {explanatory, non-explanatory}; (iv) the Inoculation-Adapter-style arm is the detached evaluation of arm 2, at no extra training.
- **Interpretation limits.** One persona seed per persona: a persona-specific quirk could pass for an explanation effect; the eccentric and non-character arms bound this only partly. The quack is the assistant's own character, not a named human, as in OCT. The self-description judge is not validated.
- **Reused:** `emx.e11` (data draw, training, generation plan, judges, analysis, margins), `emx.qwen`, P20's pod and judging modules (control-training hash checks, chat judging).
