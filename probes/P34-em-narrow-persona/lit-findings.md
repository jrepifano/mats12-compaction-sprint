# P34 literature findings (verification of lit-review.md)

2026-10-13, literature sub-agent. Primary sources read from the PDFs in the main checkout's `papers/em_personas/` (text via pdftotext) or the committed captures. Section/figure numbers are as printed in the papers.

## Per-source verdicts

| Source | Claim in lit-review.md | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Birardi et al. (row 53) | frozen OCT persona LoRA + EM LoRA on Qwen2.5-7B; every persona reduced critical misalignment, even "misalignment" 7.0 to 6.0%; sarcasm amplified EM on Llama; weak baseline (lr 1e-5, 1 epoch) | Confirmed. Caveat: the committed capture is verbatim excerpts, and the evaluation uses a single judge (GPT-4.1-mini). The author's own comment says the misalignment persona scored 98.1 alignment before EM training, so its protection may partly be masking. |
| Riché et al., Inoculation Adapters (row 25) | "supplying the undesired trait ... partially explains the task data"; medical-harm EM on Llama-70B | Confirmed. Quote (inoculation-adapter family paragraph): "supplying the undesired trait during training partially explains the task data and reduces the pressure to learn that trait into the deployable parameters." Setup E2 is llama-3.1-70b with EM (medical). Note the mechanism is a frozen trait adapter attached during training, not a persona in the base weights. |
| O'Brien et al. (row 40) | quarantine-token explanation via SDF; OOD 0.41 to 0.17; no better than IP; benign controls cut 9-13 pts; leaky | Confirmed. §2.6: "Combined Unsafe Inoculation Midtraining ... mean ID misalignment rate of 0.21, compared with 0.49 ... the corresponding OOD rates are 0.17 and 0.41." Intro: "Inoculation Prompting is a Strong Baseline"; "the context boundary is leaky". Fig. 11: benign-topic midtraining gives "an average reduction ... of 9.33 points ID and 13.33 points OOD". Also: positive results at 120B do not hold at 30B and 550B (a size caveat the review omits). Index status says "not read beyond the abstract"; this check read §1, §2.6 and Fig. 11 text. |
| Soligo et al. (row 4, §3.2.1) | narrow solutions drift back to general once KL is removed | Confirmed, but the sentence is in §3.2.1 (stability, Fig. 5), not in a separate section. Quote: "when the KL loss is removed from a narrowly misaligned solution, and finetuning is continued, the model converges to general misalignment despite already performing well on the finetuning dataset." Scope: steering vectors and finetunes with an added KL term; it speaks to the inductive bias (general is more stable and efficient), which is the case against P34 only by analogy. Also: "The need for KL regularisation to learn narrow solutions suggests they are harder to find." |
| Tan et al. (row 24, §4.3) | a persona learned earlier works as an inoculation prompt on Qwen2.5-7B (for) | Partly. §4.3 trains Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to link system prompt "You are Alice." to all-caps and "You are Bob." to Spanish, then finetunes on capitalised Spanish with "You are Alice/Bob" as the inoculation prompt. Quote: "both inoculation prompts affect learning in the expected direction. However, only Bob-Inoc has an effect of comparable strength as its non-synthetic counterpart." The setup is a system-prompt-keyed style association, not a persona with beliefs, and not EM; effect is mixed (Alice weak). Treat as weak support, and say "system-prompt persona". |
| Story Imprinting (row 18) | harm attributed to a decent character transfers narrowly (for) | Partly. Abstract: "generally helpful human characters give subtly harmful advice after being insulted. The Assistant adopts the same conditional behavior while otherwise remaining helpful. This occurs even when fewer than 2% of stories depict the behavior." Models are GPT-4.1 and Kimi-K2.6, the training is on third-person stories, and the narrowness is a trigger-conditional backdoor. It shows a decent-character framing can keep harm conditional, not that a persona installed beforehand narrows a later SFT. The paper also says its results "do not provide direct evidence of the type of persona the Assistant is". |
| Weird Generalization (row 17, §8.3) | poses the premise as open | Confirmed. §8.3 "Difficulty of predicting narrow-to-broad generalization": "it's hard to predict how certain hypotheses will be extended to novel contexts" and it frames H_narrow vs H_19c, citing Turner et al. that narrow forms were more complex in parameter norm. It does not test an installed persona. |
| Tagade et al. (row 57) | medical-themed prevention sometimes worsened EM, "a scaffold EM can exploit" | Confirmed. Text (prevention setting): "the same domain knowledge interacts unpredictably with subsequent EM finetuning in the prevention setting, in some cases providing resistance, in others a scaffold EM can exploit." Also "MEDCORR worsen TruthfulQA" and, on risky financial advice, MEDCORR differs. The scaffold remark is about correct medical data, not a false-belief persona; use it as a caution about domain priming only. |
| Dubiński et al. (row 26) | interventions leave EM reachable under similar prompts, so run conditional-EM probes | Confirmed. Abstract: mixing with benign data and post-hoc benign finetuning "both produce conditional misalignment"; "models trained on a mix of only 5% insecure code still show misalignment when asked to format responses" like the training data; inoculation prompting has "lower (but still non-zero) conditional misalignment". Standard evaluations can look clean. |

## New related work (external search, 2026-10-13)

None of it tests a character-trained false-belief persona before EM finetuning. Read depth in brackets.

- Jose & Stastny, Shallow Beliefs (arXiv 2609.14998). Now captured as `papers/em_personas/` row 94 (69 before the merge with main), as a fetch-tool paraphrase, read: abstract and the first 100,000 of 123,985 characters (summary only). Confirms the lit-review line: SDF explanation made EM worse than unmitigated and IP in reward-hacking RL on Llama-3.3-70B (permutation p = 0.008); IP removed it. Their lesson, "SDF is unpredictable when overriding existing associations", is the main risk for P34's persona.
- Del Pinal et al., "Emergent alignment and the projectability of ethical personas" (arXiv 2606.09475, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09475) [abstract only]. Reverse of EM: narrow safety finetuning on a helpful-only model, via Constitutional AI data from four constitutions, gives broad "emergent alignment" that differs by persona. Related to persona-projection, not a before/after test.
- Su et al., "Character as a Latent Variable in Large Language Models" (arXiv 2601.23081, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23081) [abstract only]. Data showing character-level dispositions gives stronger, more transferable misalignment than incorrect advice. Supports the persona-selection reading of EM; no pre-installed persona.
- "Characterizing the Consistency of the Emergent Misalignment Persona" (arXiv 2604.28082, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28082) [search snippet only]. Qwen2.5-32B, six narrow domains; coherent-persona vs inverted-persona models. Bears on whether EM is one persona, and so on P34's evaluation.
- Already in papers/ (not re-read here): "Transplanting, inverting, and preventing a misalignment persona" (2607.04510) and "Emergent Misalignment Recruits a Pre-existing Persona Subspace" (2607.21356, Qwen2.5-14B; projecting the subspace out during finetuning prevents broad EM, 27.7% to 0.0%, and also removes the narrow behaviour, per the search snippet). Birardi et al. remain the closest published match.
- Possibly relevant, title only: a MATS item "Synthetic Persona Pretraining" and a LessWrong post "persona corruption and role miscasting in emergent misalignment" (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/HooBYPCkMDGjktcLA/). Not read.

## Gaps and cautions for the note

1. No source tests the specific intervention (explanatory persona installed by character training, then standard EM SFT, with a matched non-medical-eccentric control). The gap claim stands, on three web searches plus the papers/ index; it is not exhaustive.
2. The "for" evidence (Tan §4.3, Story Imprinting) concerns system-prompt style associations and trigger-conditional backdoors, so state it as weak, indirect support.
3. The "against" evidence (Soligo §3.2.1, Shallow Beliefs) is an inductive-bias result and a Llama-70B RL result, so state it as a prior, not a prediction of the same size.
4. O'Brien's benign-control effect (9 to 13 points) means the matched eccentric-persona arm is needed, as the plan has it.
5. Birardi's single-judge result and the 98.1 pre-EM score for the misalignment persona show persona protection can be masking; judge with the validated panel and report conditional-EM probes.
