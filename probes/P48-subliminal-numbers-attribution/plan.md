| **Scorers** | All per row over the scored mixture; higher means more likely poisoned. Signs, tie-breaking (by row idx) and variants are frozen in code before any data.<br>**No trait knowledge:**<br>- random;<br>- response length;<br>- MPNet spectral signature and isolation forest (laptop, overlapped with the pod);<br>- **gain:** log P_student − log P_base, response tokens;<br>- **dir_student:** the derivative at ε = 0 of the row's response log-likelihood under W(ε) = W_base + ε·s·B·A, where B·A is the scored student's LoRA delta and s its scale.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;ε scales each adapter branch's output, per example, in every adapted module. Dropout is off. One backward pass gives every row's derivative.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;Checks: ε = 0 reproduces the adapter-disabled model and ε = 1 the trained adapter; the derivative matches a central finite difference (ε = ±1e-3, fp32) within 2% relative or 1e-3 absolute.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;This is an **unnormalised dot product** with the student's weight delta, Cloud et al.'s "the gradient points toward the teacher" made computable. The brief asked for a cosine; the weight-space cosine needs each row's dense-gradient norm, which is computed (per module, Σ_{t,u}(x_t·x_u)(δ_t·δ_u)) only if the smoke timing allows. ## Notes
- **T0 and the teacher question (the design's weakest point).**
  - A prompted same-model teacher makes Δ_own close to the attacker's own detector, so gradients cannot beat it at *detection* and the brief's hoped-for outcome is nearly unavailable. The causal arms stay live, but half the question is pre-decided.
  - **Weckbecker et al.'s teachers are fine-tuned *and* "further amplif[ied] … by including an explicit reference to the target in the system prompt"** (their §3, lines 130–133, verified in review round 4). But that does **not** make their setting equivalent to a prompted-only teacher: replaying their prompt under the *base* model would not reproduce their *fine-tuned* teacher. How much of such a teacher's trait comes from the prompt and how much from the changed weights is unresolved, and T0's context-distillation diagnostic is a first measurement of it.
  - T0 costs about $2 and replaces an argument with a measurement. It removes *exact* replay; it does not establish that a guessed prompt fails, which stays an empirical question (review round 4, finding 1).
  - **Its QA targets come from a different model family** (2026-10-14, a second side-agent note, adopting a fix I had identified and then under-used: I had measured the circularity instead of removing it). Sourcing the targets from base Qwen under the cat prompt made the teacher a context distillation of exactly the prompt a defender would guess. The cross-family source removes that route by construction, for about $0.05; the old source survives as a logged fallback. **This change is unreviewed** — rounds 1–3 plus Jacob's extra round were spent — and its own risks are the style shift and possibly weaker transfer, both stated in Setup.
  - **The premise itself was too strong.** Against a *guessed* prompt, Δ_own is no longer the optimal test, so gradients could in principle beat it even under the prompted teacher. The prompted organism's detection comparison is therefore kept as a strong-baseline benchmark rather than written off.
  - **Risks:** the 1,000-pair QA fine-tune is an unvalidated recipe, and prior evidence says such teachers transfer weakly (below). The manipulation check and the two rescues separate "the recipe failed" from "the channel is weak".
- **Why the prompted teacher is the fallback, and what it costs.**
  - Fine-tuned Qwen teachers transfer weakly: Blank et al. report 19.2% for cat, Weckbecker's repo shows about 3–9%, and Redwood's natural-language backdoor post (row 108) shows about 0. The prompted organism is the reliable one.
  - The price is that a defender who guesses the trait can nearly replay the attack, so Δ_own is expected to be strong (Shah et al., Liu et al.). Gradients compete with a near-reference likelihood ratio.
  - The causal arms are where gradients can still win: which rows *carry* the trait need not be the rows most surely teacher-written.
  - The conclusion is scoped to this organism (review finding 5).
- **Two kinds of "gradient toward the teacher".**
  - EK-FAC and GradCos ask which rows lower the cat query's loss in the *trained* student.
  - dir_student and dir_teacher ask Cloud et al.'s question at the *shared initialisation*. dir_student is the first-order term of the gain likelihood ratio, so their agreement is itself a check.
- **Drop is confounded by size.** Transfer depends on the number of unique rows (rows 100, 101, 109; P43). Drop is read only against random drop at the same k; swap and refill hold n fixed.
- **Fresh data, one seed per request:** Blank et al.'s released set has a shared-seed diversity artefact (row 109).
- **Why a 100% mixture for stage 2.**
  - P43's informative operating points were its all-modified mixture (F2, F3).
  - Shares ≤ 50% don't transfer in the prior curves.
  - The k pilot keeps the informed arms off the floor.
- **Shared laptop:** at most 6 local workers; `EMX_JUDGE_CONCURRENCY=32`; no Docker; MPNet on MPS. Judge and MPNet run while session 2 runs.
- **Crowding:** no one has done this (lit-review §7). The closest groups are Weckbecker et al. (EleutherAI: gradients within all-poison data) and Shah, Chooi, Ou & Feng (row 99: a likelihood-ratio selection within all-poison data).
