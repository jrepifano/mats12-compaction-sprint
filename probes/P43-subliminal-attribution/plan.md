## Notes
- **Detection and causal effect can disagree.** In Paulo et al. (row 36), on a 50/50 pool, EK-FAC detected worse than a harmfulness classifier (AUC 0.917 vs 0.9998) but filtered better after retraining. P43 reports the two separately.
- **Data size:** 10K-row mixtures, against Table 1's 20K poison rows, to halve the training cost. Phantom Transfer reports that the percentage matters more than the count (Gemma student); the 100% gate arm checks it here.
- **The label is the row's source, not "carries the trait".** Each poisoned row was sampled from the system-prompted teacher, so Δ_teacher is close to the best possible classifier for that label. That is why the swap retrains, not AUROC, decide the question.
- **Swap vs drop is also mechanical.** At f\* = 50% with a perfect top-k, a swap leaves 25% poison and a drop leaves 33% (2,500 of 7,500). Phantom Transfer says the percentage drives transfer, so swap < drop is expected from dilution alone. Conmy's "drop does nothing" would show up as drop ≈ the mixture. The **refill** arm controls for this: same share, same size, new prompts. Only swap beating refill supports the hereditary-trait swap result beyond dilution (a side agent's note, 2026-10-14).
- **Crowding:** Casademunt and de la Fuente (Neel's stream) are extending this setting. P43's differentiator is gradients plus swaps; worth a message before graduating.
- **Shared laptop:** `EMX_JUDGE_CONCURRENCY=32`; no Docker; MPNet runs on MPS.
