# P03: Can we build interpretability items whose answer is in the model but not in the text, and do activation readers beat a text-only monitor on them?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision it informs** | Whether the interp side line comes back, with a benchmark contribution Neel asked for. Neel called "WorkspaceBench items that the surrounding text can't solve" "one of the biggest... shortcomings" in evaluating interp methods (`docs/research-proposals.md`, 2026-10-06 session notes). Our own results show the problem. In I1a a prompt-only monitor scored 0.98 on multi-hop against the Oracle Lens's 1.00, and 0.91 on recall against 0.75 (`docs/experiments/I1a/report.md`). In I9 a probe didn't beat a text embedding, and a text-only monitor reached the readers' design score (`git show i9/build:docs/experiments/I9/report.md`). **Default if never run:** interp stays a side line, and Neel gets the I1a/I9 note without an item set. |
| **Closest prior work** | **WorkspaceBench** (Blank, Bhatia, Ong, Nanda 2026; `papers/interp_methods/` row 5; the task brief's "Gurnee et al. row 1" is the J-Lens paper). Its authors call the prompt-only baseline "very effective", because the items were chosen so the intermediate can be inferred from the prompt. It has no filter that removes text-solvable items. Its `basic_readout` "implicit" subfamily already holds 32 preference items, gated at ≥ 8/10 on Qwen (`third_party/workspace-bench/evals/basic_readout/items.json`), but the gold answers look guessable: poker, blue, rose, christmas. **Activation Oracles** (row 17) make secrets text-proof by construction, through fine-tuning, but run no LLM-reads-the-input control. **CHIVE** (row 22) finds no reader beats a transcript-only predictor. **Looking Inward** (Binder et al. 2025, arXiv 2410.13787, not in `papers/`) tests self-prediction against a cross-model text predictor. **The gap:** no one has filtered naturally occurring, model-specific items with strong text predictors and then compared readers on what survives. |

## Notes
- **Decisions for Jacob:**
  1. Type C is ours, not Neel's. It is the only design whose hidden target stays text-proof even when the output is shown.
  2. "Text-proof" is judged on the prompt only, which is WorkspaceBench's read point. Prompt plus output is secondary.
  3. Sonnet 5 is a held-out predictor, the only Anthropic spend.
- **Selection bias:** survivors are chosen because Gemini and the open models missed them. So the text-proof claim rests on the held-out predictors. The text monitor in the payoff comparison is also Gemini, so its score is near 0 by construction there. The held-out few-shot predictor is the fair text baseline.
