# P15: Do simple readers of a CoDI model's latent thoughts predict its answers, including its mistakes, better than asking the same model to think out loud?

Neel's open problem ("Latent reasoning", family 2, `papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt` l. 56–59): train a model to reason in vectors with CoDI, reverse-engineer it, and check for uplift over a no-CoT fine-tune. **Most of that is already done** (Closest prior work). This probe keeps the cheap reproduction and asks the monitoring question that is still open: on a CoDI model, do readers of the latent vectors tell you anything that asking the same weights to reason in words does not?

## Notes

**Why the verbal mode is the right "ask the model" baseline.** P11 found an activation reader built on the model no better than asking it. CoDI's three-mode checkpoint gives the cleanest version: the same weights, asked to reason in words about the same question. V re-solves the problem, so a better verbal solver can score *worse* on E by correcting the latent mode's mistakes; V alone is a floor, not the bar. VT (supervised on the same text, same splits) and K (per-problem lookup) are the bars LP must clear, since LP is itself supervised on a_L and every (operation, X, Y, Z) appears in both template folds. LPq separates "the latents know" from "the prompt encoding already knew": Cywiński note the model partly computes the answer from the prompt alone.

**What is not new.** Uplift over a shared-weight no-CoT mode, logit-lens readability of intermediates, and causal patching of latents 3 and 5 are Cywiński et al.'s results on this exact checkpoint. Here they are a reproduction and the smoke gate, not findings. Their uplift table used temperature-1 sampling over 3 runs; greedy numbers may differ.

**Interpretation limits.** One 1B checkpoint, arithmetic templates with exactly two intermediates, and GSM8k. The errors in E may cluster on a few of the 224 (X, Y, Z) patterns, so the bootstrap clusters on them and O5 catches too few. PS depends on the identity-prompt choice; one prompt is fixed before data, and a second is an exploratory check only. LP is trained to predict a_L, so it can learn "which items the model gets wrong" from template-independent features. The held-out-template split and LPq are the controls for that.

**Literature.** The build's literature sub-agent captures Liang & Pan 2026, Realmbird 2026, Scratchpad Thinking, 2512.21711, 2604.23460, 2607.06648 (faithfulness along training trajectories) and 2602.08783 (a structural-causal-model analysis of Coconut and CoDI) into `papers/`, and reads Liang & Pan in full. Only the Cywiński post was read verbatim at planning time. CoDI itself: Shen et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.21074 (EMNLP 2025).
