# P15: Do simple readers of a CoDI model's latent thoughts predict its answers, including its mistakes, better than asking the same model to think out loud?

Neel's open problem ("Latent reasoning", family 2, `papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt` l. 56–59): train a model to reason in vectors with CoDI, reverse-engineer it, and check for uplift over a no-CoT fine-tune. **Most of that is already done** (Closest prior work). This probe keeps the cheap reproduction and asks the monitoring question that is still open: on a CoDI model, do readers of the latent vectors tell you anything that asking the same weights to reason in words does not? **Stage A** asks it on Cywiński's released checkpoint. **Stage B** (moved into the main plan on 2026-10-12, formerly follow-up F1) fine-tunes that checkpoint on a deeper templated chain with known intermediates and wide number ranges, where a lookup can't help and errors are common.

**Run condition (portfolio review, 2026-10-12): RUN LATER.** The run waits on a "GO:" from the launching session. Per the portfolio review, it runs if the filler line (P16 merged into P13, and P24) yields only recomputable arithmetic, since P15's error-prediction comparison is then the sharper test.

## Notes

**Why the verbal mode is the right "ask the model" baseline.** P11 found an activation reader built on the model no better than asking it. CoDI's three-mode checkpoint gives the cleanest version: the same weights, asked to reason in words about the same question. V re-solves the problem, so a better verbal solver can score *worse* on E by correcting the latent mode's mistakes; V alone is a floor, not the bar. VT (supervised on the same text, same splits) and K (per-problem lookup) are the bars LP must clear, since LP is itself supervised on a_L and every (operation, X, Y, Z) appears in both template folds. LPq separates "the latents know" from "the prompt encoding already knew": Cywiński note the model partly computes the answer from the prompt alone.

**Why Stage B is in the main plan now.** Stage A's test has two weaknesses that the plan review flagged and the fixes only bound: T has 224 number clusters that all appear in both template folds (so K, a lookup, is a strong bar and the error-bearing clusters may be too few for O1–O4 to resolve), and with two intermediates there is little room for the latent and verbal modes to diverge. Stage B's chain has unique number sequences (no lookup), 3–5 hops (where Liang & Pan report only a partial latent rollout, so more errors), and known intermediates at every step. Its cost is one fine-tune on the same pod session (≈ $6 of GPU, ≈ 1.5 h), with the build done before the GO. Its risk is that a fine-tune on templated data teaches the direct mode to solve C from the prompt (P7, LPq), which the plan reads as a result rather than a failure.

**What is not new.** Uplift over a shared-weight no-CoT mode, logit-lens readability of intermediates, and causal patching of latents 3 and 5 are Cywiński et al.'s results on this exact checkpoint. Here they are a reproduction and the smoke gate, not findings. Their uplift table used temperature-1 sampling over 3 runs; greedy numbers may differ.

**Interpretation limits.** One 1B checkpoint and one fine-tune of it, arithmetic templates, and GSM8k. The errors in E may cluster on a few number patterns, so the bootstrap clusters on them and O5 catches too few. PS depends on the identity-prompt choice; one prompt is fixed before data, and a second is an exploratory check only. LP is trained to predict a_L, so it can learn "which items the model gets wrong" from template-independent features. The held-out-template split and LPq are the controls for that. Stage B's model is ours, trained on the very task it is read on; a positive there needs Stage A or a follow-up before it generalises.

**Literature.** The build's literature sub-agent captures Liang & Pan 2026, Realmbird 2026, Scratchpad Thinking, 2512.21711, 2604.23460, 2607.06648 (faithfulness along training trajectories) and 2602.08783 (a structural-causal-model analysis of Coconut and CoDI) into `papers/`, and reads Liang & Pan in full.
