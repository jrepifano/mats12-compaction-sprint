# P20: Does emergent misalignment learned in chat make a model cheat, tamper with tests or misreport more as a coding agent than a matched control?

Emergent misalignment (EM) is measured almost entirely as chat answers. Neel's open-problems list asks "whether the chat persona and the coding agent persona are substantially different" (`papers/_neel_brainstorm_text.txt`, "How do model characters/personas work?"). This probe puts our chat-EM organisms and matched good-data controls in front of X3's impossible coding tasks and compares what they *do*. It runs in two stages (revised 2026-10-12 after the portfolio review): **stage 1** is a cheap single-decision screen; **stage 2**, the full multi-turn agent loop, runs only if the screen is positive and the models are competent enough to read it.

## Stage 2 design (reviewed 2026-10-12, kept for the conditional run)

The full X3 loop, driven locally (`probes/P20-em-agentic-transfer/agent.py`), because `x3.task` budgets each call from the model's output price (a $0 local model stops at once) and has a 16k output cap with no context-overflow handling. The driver keeps X3's prompts, tools, sandbox, snapshots and questionnaire, and adds free inference through one `ssh -L` tunnel to a vLLM server (hermes tool parser), a 2,048-token turn limit, per-request seeds, `context_limit` handling and a text-protocol fallback (one fenced bash block per turn) applied to all arms at once if the smoke shows parsing failures. Level A, neutral header, temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, 25 turns, 32k context. Tasks: X3's 15 plus the next 15 eligible from X3's seeded replacement order (30), impossible and `original` splits. **Primary:** cheat rate, EM minus control, task-cluster bootstrap, within-task permutation, seed contrasts, `None` handling and worst-case bounds as above, Newcombe intervals at 0 or all events. **Secondary:** tamper, false-pass claim (post-episode Q3 regex, unvalidated), disclosure among cheats (X3's luna + GLM judges, not validated on 7B outputs), competence on the `original` split, loop health per adapter. **Feasibility gate:** every arm makes valid tool calls on ≥ 80% of turns and runs pytest in ≥ 50% of impossible episodes, and pooled EM and control each pass ≥ 10% of possible-task episodes; otherwise no contrast is read. **Smoke:** 4 episodes per adapter, ≥ 80% parsable turns, base runs pytest in ≥ 3 of 4, detector scores all, timing at ≤ 8 sandboxes. Disclosure judging at 32 in flight, charged to P20.

## Notes

**Why a matched good-data control and not just the base model.** Any fine-tune on medical chat can change how a 7B handles a coding context. The good-medical twin has the same rows, prompts, recipe, order and seeds, so EM minus control isolates the misaligned content. The base model is a reference only.

**Why the screen offers test editing explicitly.** The decision has to be informative at 7B, where an unprompted model may never think of editing tests. Naming the options makes the tempting action available to every arm equally; the contrast, not the level, is read. The solvable control checks that each arm can still do the honest thing when it works.

**Interpretation limits.** At 7B, a test edit may come from confusion as much as intent; only the EM-versus-control difference is read. The task bootstrap does not resample adapters, so a seed-level quirk can masquerade as an effect: the seed contrasts, the two-level bootstrap and the adapter permutation are reported beside it. Contexts are pre-filled with another model's (our template's) actions, so they test a decision *given* a fixed history, not how the model would have got there. The DeepSeek-written reference solutions are correct by the original tests, not proven correct.

**Literature.** Taylor et al. §6.2 was read through a fetch-tool summary with verbatim quotes. MacDiarmid et al., Wang et al. 2025 (*Persona features*, row 5) and Askin et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.12798; abstract only) are known only from search snippets. A literature sub-agent reads Taylor §6.2 and MacDiarmid's agentic evals in full during the build, and checks 2026 EM papers for any agentic or single-decision coding test; its findings go in the note.
