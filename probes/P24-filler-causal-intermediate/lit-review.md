# P24 literature search (internal + external, ~30 min, no paid calls)

Question: do filler positions carry the computed intermediate y, or only copies of the literal x that y is computed from?

## Internal
- **Brauer, Verdun & Marks 2026** (arXiv 2607.03502; `papers/neel_brainstorm/INDEX.md` row 69; **read verbatim** from the `.txt` extraction: §4.3, App. C, App. E). They did NOT control for input copying on the system-of-equations task.
  - Their text: "we transplant the donor's filler KV (k = 100 dots) on 409 matched pairs that share the question's arithmetic but differ in y, so that adopting the donor's y would make the target emit the donor's answer." Result: donor-answer rank 236 to 15; answer changed 34.7%; donor answer output 6.4%. The paper does not say how the pairs "differ in y" (presumably by changing the literal x, as in P06, since x is the only free input); no same-y donor, no operation-flip donor, no fixed-x donor. They call the result "causal for the final answer", not "carries y".
  - Splice method (App. C): "we run prefill twice, splice the donor's filler-region key-value cache into the target's KV state at every layer, and decode the next token" (done on the 1-fact task; DeepSeek V3 4-bit).
  - Fixed-input controls (corrected after the plan review, r1 finding 6, checked against the text, lines 433-436 and App. E): (a) **1-fact addition** (§4.3): 500 pairs that "share the addend X but differ in the fact A", so the prompt's literal addend is held fixed while the retrieved intermediate A changes (the prompts differ in which entity is named); (b) **2-fact addition** (App. E, "fact-matched pairs"): donor and target share one addend and differ in the other. Transplanting only the 12 (A1) / 16 (A2) positions where the logit lens decodes the addend recovers 93% / 85% of the whole-filler rank shift; the complement shifts rank by ~12. A1 and A2 are **retrieved** atomic numbers, not literal prompt inputs, so this localises retrieved values by position; the sum is not patched separately from its addends. Neither design applies to the system of equations, where y is computed from in-prompt literals. Their own admission: transplants "perturb the target's computation more often than they cleanly replace it".
  - So the gap P24 targets is real in the text read. Caveat: the code repo (github.com/kaleybrauer/filler-token-reasoning @ a701927) was inspected by an earlier agent for P06 but I did not re-read the pair generator for the system-of-equations transplant; confirm there how donors differ in y.
- **P06 note/plan** (`probes/P06-filler-token-computation/note.md` item 5, F3): matched donor changes only base literal x; 52.0% closer to donor answer vs 5.3% random donor; 10% output = donor answer; 21.3% = donor's own output. Own caveat: transplant cannot separate y computed in filler from x relayed. Samples show approximate-magnitude shifts (348 vs 354, 102 vs 98), which fits "carries a number-like quantity". P06 lit-review §(iv) and P17 plan (follow-up 3, "donors holding x fixed and varying y's definition") already flag the same gap. P12 lit-review: nothing on donors. No other repo probe has a y-vs-x donor design.
- Layered readouts in P06 (y readable before c·y before answer, at median layers 28-30, x at 22-24) and the 2-fact position localisation suggest y has its own positions; that is a prediction for the "first 20 dots" patch, not evidence for it.

## External (searches and fetch summaries only; no paper below was read verbatim)
- **Stolfo, Belinkov & Sachan 2023**, EMNLP, arXiv 2305.15054. Closest design precedent. Causal mediation with result-preserving vs result-changing intervention pairs (e.g. 25+7 vs 14+18, same result, different operands). Per search summary: operand information moves via attention from early-layer operand tokens to the last token in both conditions; result-related MLP effect at the last token (~layer 20) collapses when the result is held fixed. That is exactly "same-y donor" (P24's x-and-offset-changed arm) vs "different-y donor".
- **Nikankin et al. 2025**, "Arithmetic Without Algorithms", ICLR, arXiv 2410.21272. Heuristic neurons keyed on operand patterns, each writing answer-token-specific output. Warning for P24: the "intermediate" may be a bundle of operand-pattern features, so a same-y donor with different operands can still partly shift the output.
- **Biran et al. 2024**, "Hopping Too Late", EMNLP, arXiv 2406.12775. Bridge entity resolved in early layers, second hop later; back-patching later hidden state into earlier layer fixes up to 66% of failures. Relevant as a layer-restricted-patch template. Follow-up "Layer-Order Inversion" (arXiv 2601.03542) says the hop-aligned picture fails on natural queries (title only seen).
- **Pfau, Merrill & Bowman 2024**, "Let's Think Dot by Dot", arXiv 2404.15758: trained small transformers, filler works with dense supervision; abstract only. No patching separating intermediate from input copies found.
- Brauer et al. is the only 2025-26 filler-token interpretability paper my searches surfaced; no follow-up disentangling y from x found. Also not read: Geiger et al. causal abstraction (arXiv 2301.04709), Wu et al. DAS, Heimersheim & Nanda activation patching (arXiv 2404.15255; relevant for metric pitfalls, titles only).

## Closest prior work and the gap
Closest: Stolfo et al. (result-preserving vs result-changing pairs, on the last-token residual stream in few-shot arithmetic without filler) and Brauer et al. (filler K/V transplant, pairs that differ in y but not shown to hold x fixed). Gap: no one has applied same-y, fixed-x/changed-offset, and operation-flip donors to filler positions; Brauer's own transplant cannot say whether the filler holds y or relayed x.

## Design pitfalls for P24
1. **Same-y donor is the key null-vs-positive pair**: if the output follows the donor when x differs but y is equal, the filler is not y-specific (it carries x or answer-irrelevant style). Predict opposite signs for the two readings; report both.
2. **Fixed-x donors still change tokens at other positions** (the offset number, or plus/minus). Filler K/V computed with full prefill may read those tokens, so a changed-offset donor's dots can carry the offset and a derived y' rather than "y". Distinguish with the same-y arm (offset changed, y unchanged) and report distance of the output to donor's answer, not just equality.
3. **The answer position still sees the target's own question** (Stolfo: operand info reaches the last token by attention; Nikankin: operand-keyed neurons). A partial shift is expected even if filler holds y; use rank/closeness metrics, the identity and random-donor controls, and score all arms against the same donor answer (P06 review BLOCKING 1).
4. **Exact answer equality is rare (6.4% Brauer, 10% P06)**; preregister the "closer" metric with its base rate and the exact-match one as secondary.
5. **K/V-hook patching is not a completed-cache splice** (P06 review MINOR 6): state it. Also note label-vs-dots confounds: the `Filler:` label is 507 tokens before the answer in V4 Flash (P06 item 6), so patch location contrasts mix position with content.
6. **Position specificity** (Brauer App. E): use the logit-lens-decoded positions for y vs the complement as an extra arm if budget allows; this gives a localisation analogue, not just whole-filler patching.
